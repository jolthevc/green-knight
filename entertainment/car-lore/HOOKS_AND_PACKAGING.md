# Packaging and irresistible cold opens

## The sequence
**Familiar machine or striking image -> truthful mystery / contradiction -> consequence -> curiosity gap -> first frame -> immediate hook -> earned story -> specific payoff.**

We sell the story, not a list of interesting specifications. The package must be understandable to someone who cannot identify the specific model without the thumbnail.

## Strong title approaches
- **Unexpected fate:** "The Car That Failed... Then Became Immortal" (DeLorean, verify exact framing)
- **Cultural obsession:** "Why Everyone Wanted a Hummer" (avoid literal universal claim where unsupported)
- **Impossible rivalry:** "Why Lamborghini Went After Ferrari" (research founding myths carefully)
- **Contradictory legend:** "How the Supra Became Bigger Than the Car Itself" (interpretive and sourced)
- **Emotional paradox:** "Why Car Fans Still Love This Disaster" (choose specific vehicle and substantiate)
- **Prestige curiosity:** "Why Collectors Are Obsessed With the McLaren F1" (precise subject, not unsupported prices)

Titles above are brainstorming directions only, not verified claims. Avoid "The History of," "Top 10," "EVERYONE," "DESTROYED," "Secret" or "The $X Billion Disaster" when they overclaim or flatten the car story.

## Thumbnail philosophy
- **The car is the star.** Usually one dominant instantly recognizable vehicle, great angle, readable silhouette and emotional contrast.
- One high-signal second element if it conveys conflict: archival founder, rivals, factory ruins, courtroom headline or memorable era.
- High-contrast, clean, instantly legible on a phone; avoid tiny labels, 12 competing vehicle photos and dense text.
- No falsely Photoshopped crashes, invented documents, mocked victims or deceptive model-year imagery.
- Make the viewer want to see and *hear* the car.

## Mandatory packaging test
1. Research the core fact and the most compelling documented paradox before scripting.
2. Generate at least eight title possibilities across **obsession, reversal and conflict**.
3. Draft three distinct thumbnail concepts and pair each with a coherent title.
4. Cold-test against two personas: enthusiast and nonfan. What does a stranger care about within one second?
5. Write **one** recommended package and an exact first 30 seconds that begins honoring it.
6. Mark recommended title/thumbnail/hook *proposed* until owner approves.
7. If the truthful opening cannot sustain the premise after 2-3 minutes, pick a better angle or story.

## First minute contract
- 0:00-0:05: show the recognizable car and deliver arresting affection, stakes or contradiction. No greeting, logo bumper, topic list or generic engine montage.
- 0:05-0:30: prove one concrete premise; create an escalating question and a distinctive emotional reason to stay.
- 0:30-1:00: orient the newcomer and give an enthusiast a detail that signals real love and competence.
- Promise a story about **this car**. Don't pivot to a business essay once they click.

## Example: DeLorean opening (illustrative, source-check before use)
"Just LOOK at this thing. Stainless steel. Gullwing doors. A shape that looked like tomorrow. There was almost nothing else on the road like the DeLorean. And the company behind it? An absolute mess. How did a troubled sports car become one of the most recognizable machines on Earth?"

This is sample tone, not a final or verified script.

## Packaging QA
Would a person who loves cars want to click? Would someone who simply remembers the object want to click? Is the key promise answered *in the actual film*? If the thumbnail is stronger than the story, the idea needs more work.
