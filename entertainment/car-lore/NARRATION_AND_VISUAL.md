# Narration, sound and visual direction

## The production feeling
The movie about a car should feel more alive than a conventional business documentary. **Hear the machine. Admire its form. Understand what makes people irrationally devoted.** But never replace research with generic B-roll or stock roaring engines.

## Visual grammar
1. **Hero moments:** carefully selected silhouette, headlights, front three-quarter angle, dashboard, gearshift, badges, interior craft and historical advertising. Make the machine desirable or intriguing.
2. **Narrative evidence:** real factory photos, design sketches, magazines, era-correct interviews, vintage brochures, sourced news reports, accurate timetables, period photographs, motorsport context.
3. **Physical explanation:** short, clean diagrams explaining why a design decision changed the experience. No bloated animated engineering lecture.
4. **Cultural aftermath:** actual ads, street scenes, collector culture, posters, toys, period film references and documented enthusiast communities where relevant.
5. **Signature returns:** revisit the opening car shot or sound in the final payoff; it should mean more by the ending.

## Sound as a storytelling instrument
- Actual, correctly attributed engine recordings and other authentic mechanical sounds when licensed and feasible. Never label a substitute engine as the subject car's genuine audio.
- Music: rhythmic and cinematic where earned; emotional shifts from wonder to suspense to triumph to bittersweet nostalgia; don't use perpetual trailers/risers.
- Quiet hero moment: an earned 2-4 second breath for a visual or sound can be powerful. It is not mandatory at a formulaic timestamp.
- Deliberate foley and period audio only when not misrepresented as archival fact; note reconstruction.
- Source rights and synchronization are required. No unauthorized use of commercial songs because they evoke a period.

## Voice direction
- The narrator conveys informed delight. Admiration in some lines, a grin in others, controlled excitement during turns, room for hush during an iconic detail.
- Perform the exact approved copy with expressive variation, not synthetic bombast.
- Narrator and voice engine remain **unselected**. Audition with `VOICE_CALIBRATION.md` before batch production.

## Every scene in the visual director should specify
- Segment Sxx and paragraph anchor(s) P###.
- Dramatic job of the image, not just a file search term.
- Opening frame -> purposeful movement -> end frame.
- Model/year/trim identification where visual mismatch would be misleading.
- Asset source, provenance, permitted reuse/clearance or pending action.
- Exact verified on-screen claims and labels.
- Sound or silence direction, with authentic audio identified separately from generic foley.
- What is essential vs editor-discretionary.

## Hard no's
- Repeated generic car glamour shots pasted over a dull script.
- Wrong make, trim, engine, period, badge or model year representing the subject.
- AI-generated imagery passed off as authentic historic footage or a real tested vehicle.
- A fake engine sound stated as genuine.
- Copyrighted car commercial, race footage, film clip or social-media footage assumed free to use.
- Constant kinetic captions, stock-video wallpaper, 30-second gaps without new thought, or graphically misleading performance comparisons.
- Glamorizing street racing, reckless driving or deadly crashes.

## Rights workflow
Prefer originally commissioned/owned graphics, permitted brand/public-domain historical assets verified case by case, licensed photos and footage, and documented case-specific exceptions approved by qualified review. **Availability on YouTube or in press coverage never equals reuse permission.** Rights uncertainty blocks packet release until addressed. Record provenance and clearance per asset.
