# Production, editor handoff and release rules

## Workflow philosophy
Car Lore must feel like a premium, joyful automotive documentary even under lean outsourced editing economics. The editor is not expected to invent the story or carry a lifeless script with beautiful car footage.

## Two supported models
A. Green Knight develops researched script, narration and visual directions; editor produces from approved packet.
B. Producer proposes idea/script; Green Knight verifies research, revises into the channel voice and creates the final directors. Owner approval is still required before full production.

## Gates
1. Owner picks a subject and episode angle from **unresearched candidates**.
2. Research vehicle specifications, people, dates, period responses, disputes, anecdotes and likely visuals, with a claim/source record. Flag unreliable lore.
3. Create 8+ truthful title candidates, three thumbnail concepts and one internally recommended package.
4. Write dramatic beat map and complete **10-20 minute** spoken script, target roughly 15 minutes, with verified enthusiast specifics and car appreciation.
5. Independent editorial check for excitement, authentic car love, nonfan comprehension, technical accuracy, fair treatment of real people and source integrity.
6. Produce exact narration director and precise paragraph-anchored visual director, including shot-by-shot first 30 seconds and asset/rights plans.
7. Owner expressly approves final script, package direction and auditioned narrator before full production.
8. Release exactly **three** editor-facing content documents:
   - `01_SCRIPT.txt` : exact clean approved spoken copy, non-spoken title header only.
   - `02_NARRATION_DIRECTOR.md` : exact complete spoken body broken into segments with clearly separated **NON-SPOKEN** performance notes.
   - `03_VISUAL_DIRECTOR.md` : one selected title/thumbnail/first frame; beat and shot map aligned to exact narration paragraphs, car beauty beats, real sound plan and media provenance.
9. Optional 60-90 second integration sample before full cut, if commissioned. Evaluate whether enthusiasm is audible and visually evident.
10. Review actual cut for continuous story movement, rights, audio mix, factual on-screen claims, correct model images, emotional payoff and exact script alignment.
11. Approve publish separately and analyze retention and comments after real distribution. Do not invent metrics or bookings.

## Final editor deliverables
- Finished long-form MP4 (16:9, at least 1080p preferred).
- Custom thumbnail matching title and actual car/model.
- Separate clean narration WAV/MP3.
- YouTube package: chosen title, description, tags when useful, and accurate chapters/timestamps.
- Synced SRT subtitles.

As contractually agreed, retain editable project source, properly documented licenses, asset source list and versioned edits. Do not assume a price, turnaround, revision count, editor engagement, content approval or rights clearance has been agreed.

## Final pass scorecard
- **First 5 seconds:** clear recognizable car, immediate fascination.
- **First 30 seconds:** viewer knows the stakes and what question is being answered.
- **Every minute:** fresh development, surprise, stakes shift, wonderful detail or emotional change, without synthetic cliffhangers.
- **Car-lover credibility:** specific and accurate car delight, no fake driving claims.
- **Nonfan test:** a curious layperson could follow and enjoy it.
- **Editorial integrity:** sources and rights documented, distinctions between fact and opinion.
- **Sensory test:** sound and visuals serve the story, not merely decorate.
- **Conclusion:** fully honors click promise and deepens affection for cars.

Reject a visually polished but passionless or narratively flat cut. Revision is preferable to volume.
