## Nonfan retention verdict
**V1:** The car-and-$550k hook worked, but the next several minutes became a sequential history-and-spec tour. At minutes 5-9, the tuning and game material became descriptive exposition. The auction payoff simply restated a fact revealed in the first 30 seconds, with limited new interpretation.
**V2:** Open on the expensive automatic film car and the showroom paradox, then travel through 1993 Toyota engineering heroism, strong performance, a U.S. sales reversal, the new owner/enthusiast life, the actual Craig Lieberman origin story and cultural amplification, then Toyota's revival and the auction return. Each act changes whose definition of a 'great Supra' controls the story.

## Enthusiast credibility verdict
**V1:** Excellent raw research but excessive generalized admiration ("legendary", "iconic", "passion") and detached engineering lists.
**V2:** Uses factory engineer Isao Tsuzuki's documented 950 weight meetings, hollow-fiber carpet, precise Car and Driver test and 1993 comparison, the unique significance of the 2JZ foundation, real import scene's involvement in casting a Hollywood car, and the manual-versus-automatic debate. Avoids the claim that a stock 2JZ reliably produces 1000 hp.

## Documented structural changes
1. **Hook:** 2021 collector sale, particular automatic film car, 1998 U.S. withdrawal within the same opening. No unsupported ordinary-$550k-valued-Supra claim.
2. **Car worship:** An early sensory look at actual A80 shape followed by '950 meetings' as concrete automotive obsession.
3. **Performance:** Reduces specification dumps; juxtaposes documented 4.6-second test and September 1993 six-car win with U.S. sales weakness.
4. **Midpoint:** Toyota's finished-product thesis versus the tuner's starting-point thesis; that's the central mechanism driving the second half.
5. **Cinema story:** Craig Lieberman and actual import culture have agency; movie amplified an existing scene, rather than creating it.
6. **Late act:** Akio Toyoda, Nürburgring, 2019 BMW collaboration and eventual manual gearbox underscore the fan-built meaning of 'Supra'.
7. **Final callback:** Same $550k film car returns with *new emotional meaning*. Neither claims it is Lieberman's exact original car nor a typical A80 price.

## Strongest source-backed fact turns
- Period `Car and Driver`: 950 meetings, 4.6 sec 0-60, 124lb in a stated comparison; note Toyota's own launch material uses a different spec-comparison delta.
- Sept 1993 `Car and Driver` six-car comparison: Supra overall winner, $40,890 Turbo base in that comparison.
- Toyota North America: just over 11,200 U.S. A80 sales and withdrawal by 1998; A80 still sold in Japan until 2002.
- Original Craig Lieberman interview with Toyota: yellow 1994 Supra at car show and project Redline, later Hollywood hero vehicle.
- Barrett-Jackson 2021 lot 744: orange film-used Turbo, four-speed automatic; auctioneer-reported $550,000.
- Toyoda own 2019 remarks and Toyota release: 17-year global production gap, shared BMW mechanical basis; Toyota 2022 press release: manual added for 2023 MY.
