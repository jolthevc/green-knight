## Independent critical verdict on V1
- **Opening:** the film promised a familiar outcome rather than presenting an immediate arresting incident. Reframed around genuine early door problems verified in 1981 contemporaneous reporting.
- **First 3 minutes:** too much scenic praise and generic executive biography without an urgent predicament. Compress, lead with the contradiction, and introduce a manufacturing challenge much earlier.
- **5-10-minute section:** explanatory paragraphs about industrial difficulty repeated the same generalized point. Replaced with proof that the car was genuinely improving, especially thirty workers sacrificing vacation for door-fit training, **before** the U.S. recession and sales financing killed available time.
- **Founder trial:** risk of salacious derailment. Kept short, verified acquittal, with a humane transition.
- **Final act:** refrigerator origin is a bigger reversal than merely "Hollywood happened"; explicitly explain why *the peculiar real car* was narratively perfect on screen.
- **Conclusion:** avoid four repetitions of generic "flawed but loved." Close on actual owners preserving the physical machines and the lingering beauty of the doors.

## Factual and clearance precautions
1. Specific early defects reported by Car and Driver applied to five early cars, **not every DeLorean**.
2. Thirty workers' training and vacation sacrifice was reported in a later road test, and should not be portrayed as a proven universal practice.
3. The `1982` manufacturing close and 1983 US-parent liquidation have differing dates; specify entities and avoid unsupported blanket "bankruptcy" shorthand.
4. John DeLorean was acquitted; any footage of arrest must avoid misleading implications of conviction.
5. `Back to the Future` and advertising/press footage require a documented media rights analysis, not an assumption of fair use.
