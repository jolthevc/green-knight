# Car Lore voice and narrator constitution

## Our voice in one sentence
**The most entertaining car obsessive you know is telling you a story they are desperate for you to hear.**

Imagine classic automotive-programme enthusiasm, premium documentary structure and independent enthusiast credibility. Treat these as functional inspirations, not instructions to imitate any recognizable host's voice, cadence, trademark phrases, or likeness.

## Seven voice rules
1. **Be a fanatic, not a reporter.** Express justified love and taste. "Look at this thing" can be a great opening if the next sentence proves what is special.
2. **Let the affection get specific.** Say *what* about a shape, sound, design trade-off, cultural reputation or driving experience people adore. Generic "legendary," "insane," "iconic," "game-changing" without proof is not passion.
3. **Be playful and opinionated.** Strong lines, warmth, occasional cheek, laughter at automotive absurdities. Distinguish the narrator's opinion from facts.
4. **Let intensity move.** Awe, suspense, amusement, admiration, rueful disbelief, quiet nostalgia. A monotone fails; permanent hype also fails.
5. **Include everyone.** Translate "mid-engine" or "homologation special" into the practical stakes when needed. Don't lecture, patronize or make newcomers feel ignorant.
6. **Tell a real story.** Put conflict, character, choice and consequence into speech. Never recite a chronological press release, spec sheet or Wikipedia entry.
7. **Have earned reverence.** Pause for sound or visuals only when the car genuinely warrants the attention. Beauty and mechanical poetry are part of the story, not filler.

## The authentic enthusiast test
The narrator should be capable of saying, with sincere affection, "It's a ridiculous car, and that's exactly why we love it." The love extends beyond expensive supercars to accessible cars, imperfect cars, oddball cars, tuner icons and overlooked machines.

## Writing do / don't
| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| "Those doors were spectacular. The reality underneath them was considerably messier." | "This revolutionary innovation would forever transform the automotive landscape." |
| "The Supra wasn't just quick. It became a blank canvas for an entire generation of enthusiasts." (interpretive; support specifics) | "Everyone loved the Supra because it was the best car ever." |
| "Here's why this bizarre decision mattered when you actually turned the key." | "It had X horsepower, Y torque and Z displacement" with no experiential consequence |
| "This thing was outrageous, impractical, and impossible to ignore." | Random superlatives every sentence |
| "Some owners adored it. Critics were less convinced, and here's why." | "Only idiots bought this car." |

Avoid repetitive formulaic openers, counterfeit first-person stories, condescension toward brands or fans, forced internet slang, empty motivational morals, manufactured outrage and fake surprise.

## Performance direction
- Narrator: natural conversational documentary performer with vivid emotional range, warmth, confidence, comedic timing and real curiosity. American or other natural English accent is open pending human auditions. Gender, provider, model, voice ID and detailed audio settings are **not selected**.
- Cold open: immediate, intrigued, confident, delight in the object.
- Beauty beat: ease back, admire; give the viewer room to look/listen.
- Engineering explanation: crisp, grounded, energetic when stakes rise.
- Rivalry/reversal: sharpen pace and contrast, no caricatured villain voice.
- Loss, injury or death: measured and humane, no gleeful sound effects.
- Ending: clear, emotionally satisfying, a bit of earned love for cars.
- No sustained screaming, growling movie-trailer acting, mechanical TTS emphasis or unnatural ALL-CAPS readings.

## Calibration dialogue, not an approved script
**Flat:** "The DeLorean DMC-12 featured distinctive gullwing doors and stainless-steel body panels. Production began in 1981. The model struggled commercially."

**Car Lore:** "Just look at this thing. Stainless steel. Gullwing doors. A shape that looked like tomorrow. The DeLorean was absolutely impossible to ignore. But underneath those gorgeous doors was a much more chaotic story, and somehow, the car that should have been forgotten became immortal."

This is an illustrative voice comparison, not a fact-checked episode or approval to use it as a finished hook.

## Writing benchmark
Write lines that are pleasurable to **say aloud**. If the performer doesn't sound like they enjoy the story, the copy needs work before choosing a different voice. The narrator must be auditioned on both excited, funny and emotionally quiet material. See `VOICE_CALIBRATION.md`.
