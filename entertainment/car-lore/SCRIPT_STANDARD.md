# Car Lore long-form script standard

## Runtime and spoken copy
- **10 minutes target; 8-12 minutes acceptable.** Never pad or overcompress merely to hit a number. Video duration is measured on the finished cut, not inferred from word count.
- Provisional script length **1,150-1,500 spoken words** at an auditioned narrator's natural cadence, allowing car beauty beats, sound, visual holds and pauses. Actual timing overrides the estimate.
- Long-form film, not a listicle, sales pitch, chronological Wikipedia reading, PowerPoint or car spec encyclopedia.

## Adaptable story spine
These beats can move, merge or be omitted when evidence warrants. They are dramatic responsibilities, not titles spoken aloud.
1. **S00 / 0:00-0:30 / Hook:** exact car plus compelling truth, contradiction or wild turn.
2. **S01 / 0:30-1:30 / The dream:** why this machine mattered emotionally; who wanted it, and what they saw.
3. **S02 / 1:30-3:00 / The impossible choice:** original dream, rivalry, gamble, technical challenge or social setting.
4. **S03 / 3:00-5:15 / Becoming a character:** decisive design features, sounds, engineering, people and early reactions; show rather than list.
5. **S04 / 5:15-7:30 / The beautiful mess:** consequences, reversal, internal contradiction, cult devotion, crisis or unexpected success; earn an insight not in the hook.
6. **S05 / 7:30-9:15 / Legend or reckoning:** how understanding changed and why the car matters beyond initial hype.
7. **S06 / 9:15-10:00 / Emotional payoff:** resolve the central question, return to a hero image / sound / artifact, and finish with an earned line that makes cars more fascinating.

## Mandatory story design
- **The car is a protagonist**, with a recognizable visual identity and an emotionally legible want, reputation or contested legacy.
- **One pure car-love beat** that lets us admire a real shape, mechanism, sound, image or enthusiast ritual. The beat must do narrative work.
- **One enthusiast-specific insight** grounded in reliable sources or transparently labeled opinion.
- **At least two meaningful changes** in stakes, audience understanding or protagonist circumstances after the setup.
- **Technical specifics must pay rent:** use a horsepower number only if it changes a decision, explains the experience or demonstrates real significance.
- **No counterfeit experience:** narration must never claim "I drove" or describe a fictional personal sensation as direct evidence.
- **Avoid invented cinematic scenes** about real people, internal thoughts, fake quotes, fake engine sounds or fake race results.
- **Human impact:** accidents, injuries and deaths get sober, sensitive coverage, never comedic editing or implied celebration of hazardous public driving.

## Script file convention
- Canonical `script.txt` or `01_SCRIPT.txt`: `TITLE: [approved title]` as a **non-spoken** header, a blank line, then spoken narration only. No headings, timing, editor notes, word-count notes, source citations, scene directions or paragraph IDs in spoken copy.
- Work internally with S00-S06 and paragraph anchors P001, P002... as needed, never insert these tags into TTS.
- `02_NARRATION_DIRECTOR.md` must reproduce the whole exact spoken body with *separate non-spoken cues* and section/paragraph mapping.
- `03_VISUAL_DIRECTOR.md` must tie scenes to exact P### anchors with visual, evidence, transitions, authentic audio and rights/provenance.
- Any substantive script change triggers an updated matching narrator and visual director and renewed owner sign-off.

## Closing discipline
Answer the question in the title. Avoid generic "never give up" morals and distant abstractions about entrepreneurship. A satisfying Car Lore closer should recover why this particular car matters and make the viewer feel the love.
