# Clickworthy titles, thumbnails and immediate hooks

## Formula
**Familiar film / show / character / creator -> surprising, defensible story -> specific stakes -> instantly legible thumbnail -> opening that pays off the promise.**

The viewer should think, "I know that movie. I had no idea THAT happened." For a less familiar title, the premise itself must be exceptional enough to earn the click.

## Title territories
- Production peril: "How [film] Almost Never Got Made"
- Behind-the-scenes reversal: "The Disaster That Made [movie] Possible"
- Creative choice: "The Decision That Changed [show] Forever"
- Power struggle: "The Battle Behind [film]'s Most Famous Scene"
- Hollywood wager: "Why [studio] Bet Everything on [property]"
- Cultural afterlife: "How [film/show] Became an Obsession"
- Reputation reversal: "The Film Everybody Gave Up On"
- Franchise collapse: "How [specific choice] Ended [franchise plan]"

**Those are structures, not fact-checked statements.** Use only when verified; "everything," "everyone," "destroyed" and "never" are especially prone to overstatement.

## Mandatory package before writing
1. Investigate the central narrative and identify a genuinely source-supported paradox or turning point.
2. Draft at least eight truthful title candidates representing different viewer appeals: movie fandom, human conflict, creative surprise, cultural impact and occasional economics.
3. Design three mobile-legible thumbnail concepts with subject identification and one consequential emotional tension.
4. Select a recommended title/thumbnail pair pending owner approval; record exact facts implied by the image.
5. Write a vivid first five seconds and a first 30-second cold open that immediately earns the click.
6. Verify that the promised reveal is supported and arrives in the final cut, not just in the thumbnail.

## Thumbnail grammar
- Usually one instantly recognizable film/TV still, character, production image, actor or filmmaker; rights require case-specific review.
- Prefer one striking opposing element: script marked REJECTED (only if real), competing creative figure, recognizable studio, an authentic headline, alternate design or casting photograph.
- Minimal type, if any, with legible composition on phones.
- No Photoshopped counterfeit evidence, fake posters, misidentified photos, misleading reactions or invented scandal.

## Hook examples, explicitly unresearched
- "People know Shrek as a joke-filled fairy tale. But the story of how it got made runs through one of Hollywood's most consequential creative rivalries..."
- "For a studio, finishing a movie is supposed to be the hard part. So why did Batgirl never get a release?"
- "Before The Office became a cultural institution, its American adaptation looked far from inevitable..."

These openings are **development sketches** only. Research and revisions must establish precise causality and avoid repeating familiar myths.

## Anti-clickbait rule
No unproven "secret," no misleading threat to creators' reputations, no drama inferred solely from music edits. If the real story is less dramatic than the title, change the title. A recognizable IP is never a substitute for a satisfying story.
