# Studio Confidential: instructions for future agents, researchers, writers and editors

Before working, read README.md, CHANNEL.md, STORY_AND_CONTENT.md, HOOKS_AND_PACKAGING.md, SCRIPT_STANDARD.md, VOICE_AND_VISUAL.md, EDITOR_WORKFLOW.md and PILOT_SLATE.md, plus Green Knight's business/POPCORN_STANDARD.md and entertainment/AGENTS.md. Use current repository text, not historical memory.

## Non-negotiable editorial identity
1. **Love film and television, visibly and specifically.** We're interested in the creative work, people and memorable onscreen results. Genuine fascination, not generic industry jargon.
2. **Look behind the entertainment, not merely at it.** The episode reveals a researched true story that changes how viewers appreciate a film, show, production, franchise, character or phenomenon.
3. **Not reviews, recaps or MBA essays.** Entertainment and narrative lead. Industry economics is used selectively when it reveals the drama, not to claim every Hollywood decision was financial.
4. **10-15 actual finished minutes; 12-13 preferred.** No filler, padding, robotic pacing or exhaustive background for length's sake.
5. **Two-audience test.** A fan gets surprising, credible specificity; an uninitiated viewer can immediately understand the stakes and enjoy the story. Spoilers disclosed where needed.
6. **Reliable sourcing and fairness.** Do not invent private motives, feuds, set incidents, dialogue, studio data, unreleased material, culpability, quotations or sensational claims. Distinguish documented fact, reporting, disagreement and interpretation. Verify primary or strong secondary accounts.
7. **Rights-conscious audiovisual work.** Publicly viewable clips, trailers, screenshots, posters and interviews are not automatically reusable. Track provenance, licenses, claims and clearance. Fair use is contextual, not a guaranteed loophole; refer unresolved issues for appropriate review.
8. **Original, watchable direction.** No AI-generated material posing as real on-set footage, lazy clip montages, repetitive still-image slideshows, generic stock wallpaper or footage without added original storytelling.
9. **Do not conflate channels.** Internet Famous owns primarily internet attention, Fame & Fortune owns celebrity fortunes, Behind Everything owns everyday systems, Car Lore owns automobiles. Studio Confidential owns film/TV stories regardless of whether one aspect touches money, internet virality or a car.
10. **Respect approval boundaries.** Candidate topic is not an approved episode; draft is not an editor packet; editorial specs are not a channel launch. Owner approves selected idea, exact final script, narrator and release.

## Required workflow
Idea -> title/thumbnail hypothesis -> sources + provenance assessment -> narrative thesis and beat map -> researched script -> independent engagement/factual QA -> owner signoff -> three-file editor packet -> visual/audio/rights review -> separate publication signoff -> measured analytics.

Final editor-facing packet must contain exactly:
- 01_SCRIPT.txt: clean approved spoken narration with a single non-spoken TITLE header.
- 02_NARRATION_DIRECTOR.md: full identical narration in labeled non-spoken segments with detailed performance cues.
- 03_VISUAL_DIRECTOR.md: shot and paragraph map, selected packaging, evidence and asset rights/provenance.

Narrator is not yet chosen; voice identity, licensing, brand name/handle clearance, production price and pilot topic are not approved.

After GitHub work, report changed paths, commit URLs and remaining approvals. Avoid editing unrelated folders or automatic portfolio configuration unless instructed.
