# Story territories, audience and episode greenlight

## Five editorial territories, not rigid quotas
1. **Impossible productions:** the films and series that survived extraordinary constraints, financing difficulties, creative crises, disastrous shoots or remarkable problem solving.
2. **The lore behind the legend:** the surprising stories of characters, creative partnerships, scenes, scripts, casting choices, abandoned ideas and artistic breakthroughs.
3. **Hollywood's big bets:** the economics of rights, budgets, streaming, distribution, studio rivalry, franchises and career-defining choices when a true human story is the engine.
4. **Cultural phenomena:** how specific films and shows changed fan behavior, the medium, norms, national conversation or generations of taste.
5. **The rises, falls and reinventions:** franchise peaks, failed universes, misunderstood flops, comeback stories and unusual second lives.

A sixth flexible category is welcome whenever a true film/TV story is irresistible and doesn't fit a bucket. Topics outside Hollywood, including international cinemas and television, are in scope.

## Audience
Primary: mainstream English-language viewers who like movies and television, including nostalgic casual viewers. Secondary: enthusiasts who reward well-researched behind-the-scenes history and filmmakers who recognize informed craft analysis.

**Never assume all viewers have seen the film or TV show.** Introduce only the plot/character context needed. Flag significant spoilers in packaging or early in the film when a reasonable viewer would care.

## Episode greenlight card, required before commissioning
- Recognizable subject and specific audience entry point.
- One-line truthful dramatic question, concrete consequences, emotional reason to care.
- What is surprising to a dedicated fan, with preliminary strong sources.
- A human protagonist or clearly defined production/creative institution to follow.
- At least two consequential changes in the story, where the evidence supports them.
- One interesting filmmaking or performance-specific detail reflecting genuine love of film/TV.
- An optional economics/incentives lens only if causally important.
- Title/thumbnail and first-30-second hypothesis.
- Primary/secondary source leads for all major claims, contested versions of events noted.
- Production feasibility: authentic images, usable interviews, permitted graphics, rights status, likely licensing costs and fallback plan.
- Plausible 10-15 minute story without filler.
- Risk review: defamation, privacy, tragedy, manufactured controversy, rights, reputational fairness.
- Channel overlap check against Internet Famous, Fame & Fortune, Behind Everything and other current projects.

## Decision rubric
- **GREENLIGHT for research:** irresistible and grounded premise, fresh informed-viewer value, plausible asset path.
- **REFRAME:** familiar title but thin story, too much recycled trivia, weaker-than-promised payoff or impossible footage burden.
- **REJECT:** cannot substantiate the hook, requires fabricated drama, only works as a plot recap/review, or has no compelling narrative beyond a studio's revenues.

Do not confuse editorial greenlight with approval of a full script, narrator, rights or commissioning. Proposed titles in PILOT_SLATE.md remain **unresearched candidates**.
