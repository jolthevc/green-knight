# Internet Famous creative greenlight standard

## Both tests must pass
**Entertainment test:** The story has a highly recognizable or instantly comprehensible protagonist, consequential conflict, evolving stakes, reversals and emotional pull. It feels juicy without fake scandal. A bored viewer cannot predict every beat from the first minute.

**Explanation test:** The episode proves, carefully argues or clearly bounds a specific and surprising internet mechanism. A knowledgeable internet-culture viewer learns a distinctive new lens, not generic 'algorithms are powerful' or 'fame is fleeting.'

## The salacious-but-honest rule
Use documented betrayal, public conflict, startling contrast, embarrassment, riches, humiliation, power shifts, unintended consequences and paradoxes WHEN evidence supports them. Strong verbs and a dramatic framing are allowed. Do not equate controversy with wrongdoing or imply crimes, secret motives, abuse, fraud or personal misconduct unless genuinely established and necessary.

The best clickbait is an incredible fact, puzzling reversal or consequential choice that sounds fictional but is real. The title and thumbnail MUST be repaid by the actual video.

## Retention engine
1. Open at the most revealing or suspenseful moment, not at the person's childhood.
2. Within 0-30 seconds, substantiate the promise with a real fact, artifact or outcome. Show the contradiction and a reason to care.
3. Pay off an initial curiosity quickly, then reveal the deeper question. Do not make viewers watch five minutes of setup for the first answer.
4. Every meaningful beat changes what the viewer believes. It adds credible evidence, a decision, a new actor, a reversal, a consequence or a competing explanation.
5. Design the middle deliberately. No reheated chronology, overlong Twitter recap, follower-count list or vague paragraphs about going viral.
6. Explain the internet mechanism THROUGH decisions and events, with short visual explanations where necessary, not a detached classroom intermission.
7. Include the strongest credible complication or alternative explanation. Distinguish correlation, platform design and third-party interpretations from direct evidence.
8. Deliver the exact opening promise and a further counterintuitive conclusion. The ending changes how viewers interpret the opening.

Retention audits at opening, each act and especially the 6-11 minute stretch: What is the viewer trying to find out? What changed? What was learned? Why watch one minute more?

## Research quality
- Build source-to-claim map with primary source posts/videos/interviews, original reporting, documents and relevant platform communications when available.
- Record the date and context of a purportedly viral metric; distinguish views, followers, likes, watch time, subscribers, fans and unique people.
- Avoid algorithm mind-reading. Platforms may disclose general ranking factors, but an exact individual video's causal lift typically cannot be proved from public data.
- Distinguish public criticism from established wrongdoing and verified events from rumor. Verify quotations and keep context.
- Test another explanation: timing, platform changes, outside press, networks, offline fame, paid amplification, genre or sheer chance.
- Substantive conclusions need grounded examples, not one anecdote masquerading as universal social science.

## Hard reject reasons
A title requires alleging something false; the story is only Wikipedia chronology; the takeaway is generic; the most crucial claim is unverified; the subject is too obscure to support curiosity; the middle is dead; the only way to fill 15 minutes is repetition; or the video would be a minimally transformed photo slideshow.

## Before final release
Perform cold-read newcomer and informed-fan reviews. Inspect factual sourced anchors, script voice-readability, first-30-second payoff, weakest two minutes, midfilm reversals, end insight, title/thumbnail fulfillment, segment/paragraph correspondence, narration dynamics and rights plan. An edit that is merely louder is not a better edit.
