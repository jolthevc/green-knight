# Internet Famous

**Fame moves fast. Stories last.**

**One-line promise:** People, memes, videos, challenges, platforms and viral moments are the entry point. The internet and the machinery of attention are the real subject.

**Editorial north star:** Start with a recognizable person, object, meme, online behavior or phenomenon; promise a juicy and legitimate mystery; make the next minute irresistible; and end with a deeper, earned explanation of attention, participation, social psychology, distribution, fandom or platform culture. We investigate the story; we do not ridicule its participants.

## Locked channel specs (2026-10-18)
- Genre: highly entertaining and substantive internet-culture case-study documentaries.
- Runtime: 10-20 minutes; **15 minutes preferred** including pauses and outro.
- Audience: broad, accessible mainstream internet-culture audience, including viewers who know only the thumbnail subject.
- Treatment: dramatic, emotionally vivid, high-stakes, suspenseful, sharp and sometimes salacious, grounded in facts.
- Editing: archival photography, authentic public posts, screenshots, sourced headlines, simple maps/timelines/graphs, motion/zoom, selected cleared footage. Thoughtful editing rather than complicated animation.
- Tone: confident, curious, incisive, surprising, occasionally witty. Never smug, cruel, mocking, demeaning, tabloid-unverified or lecture-heavy. Treat real people fairly and humanely, including when criticizing documented conduct.
- Narrator: consistent natural documentary voice to be selected/approved by owner. None selected yet.
- Distribution: YouTube long-form; shorts optional after long-form validation.
- Status: channel specifications established; no completed episode or approved editor packet implied.

## Scope and tone (locked 2026-10-18)
A story can be about a **person OR a phenomenon**: viral song, famous image, app feature, meme, mass challenge, fandom, game, format, shared behavior or internet sensation. **The internet is always the true subject.** No punching down, appearance jokes, speculation passed as truth or entertaining the viewer at someone else's expense. Provocative and entertaining is compatible with empathy and credible criticism. Use the [fairness standard](PEOPLE_AND_FAIRNESS.md) for sensitive claims; legal risk can be reduced but never eliminated.

## Launch recommendation
Possible person-led pilot: Rebecca Black and negative virality, with emphasis on the internet's behavior rather than mockery of a teenager, subject to primary-source research and owner topic choice. Possible phenomenon-led pilots: The Dress, Ice Bucket Challenge or Harlem Shake, also unresearched and unselected. Do not present a working hook or pilot topic as a commissioned or completed video. Other candidates include Dream, Vine, Alex from Target, MrBeast, Jenna Marbles, Lil Nas X, Addison Rae and Charli D'Amelio, each with a different internet mechanism.

## Boundary with Green Knight business channels
This channel is about internet attention, celebrity, behavior and fame, not routine corporate rises and falls. An economic mechanism is welcome when it explains a human internet phenomenon. Fame & Fortune is for celebrity wealth and business decisions; Fallen Angels is for company failure. Cross-link closely overlapping stories and avoid duplicating the same central takeaway.

## Required deliverable philosophy
The episode must work as a thrilling narrative even without the analytical thesis, AND the analysis must materially change how an informed viewer understands the story. Either alone fails.
