# Channel constitution

## Mission
Explain the forces that turn people, videos, songs, images, memes, games, challenges, online communities, apps and everyday behaviors into internet sensations, and reveal why some phenomena accelerate, evolve, inspire devotion, decay or disappear.

**Signature idea:** The person **or phenomenon** is the protagonist. The internet is the real subject. An episode never requires a celebrity.

## Viewer contract
- Click: I recognize this person, object, trend, meme or cultural moment, and the specific paradox is irresistible.
- Watch: the story keeps taking unexpected, evidence-earned turns.
- Finish: I understand a powerful, specific mechanism of internet culture that I did not understand before.
- Share: I can summarize the underlying insight in one surprising, credible sentence.

**Respect is a foundational rule:** See [PEOPLE_AND_FAIRNESS.md](PEOPLE_AND_FAIRNESS.md). Be thrilling and candid without personal ridicule, rumor laundering or character attacks. No amount of click potential overrides this rule.

**Broad scope:** See [SCOPE_AND_STORY_TYPES.md](SCOPE_AND_STORY_TYPES.md). Stories about ideas, things and mass internet behavior are equal-status with stories about creators.

## Editorial lanes
1. Accidental fame and negative virality: notoriety versus affection.
2. Creator empires and audience conversion: views to fans to bargaining power and money.
3. Mystery, identity and parasocial fandom: how audiences build imagined relationships.
4. Platform-shaped fame: how product format, distribution and incentives create stars.
5. Reinvention and portability: transitioning from platform dependence into durable careers.
6. Meme lifecycles and forgotten sensations: distributed participation versus sustained demand.
7. Internet conflict and backlash: careful, well-sourced analysis of collective behavior, without piling on.
8. Cultural moments and creator-led entertainment: where participatory internet culture reshapes mainstream media.
9. Memes, media objects and the grammar of online culture: photos, sounds, phrases, short clips and remixes.
10. Viral participation and challenges: why crowds do things collectively instead of only watching.
11. Online communities, fandoms and games: how distributed groups produce and sustain cultural moments.

## Narrative promise
Not merely 'what happened to X,' but 'what X reveals about the internet's attention machinery,' whether X is a creator, a photograph, a meme, a short-form trend, an online challenge or a shared cultural moment. The film is a case study, not a five-point lesson or dry lecture.

## Popular appeal and boundaries
Prioritize universally recognizable subjects, high emotional stakes, contradictory outcomes, specific surprising facts and clear causal puzzles. It must be graspable by someone unfamiliar with fandom acronyms or platform lore.

Never directly mock or belittle individuals, even ones who became widely ridiculed online. No fabricated personal motives, unsupported accusations, invasive private or intimate details, harassment encouragement, misleading edited clips, fake 'leaked' content or exploitation of vulnerable people. If scandal is genuinely integral, distinguish verified public record, direct on-record allegations, denials and uncertain interpretations with proportional treatment.

## Five recurring analytical questions
- Spark: why did the initial audience notice?
- Amplifier: what behaviors, intermediaries, platforms and media multiplied attention?
- Conversion: what became fans, business, status or durable ownership, if anything?
- Consequence: what changed for subjects, participants, communities, platforms and culture?
- Insight: what specific principle survives when we remove the subject's name or the meme's label?

These are research prompts, NOT five forced visible headings.
