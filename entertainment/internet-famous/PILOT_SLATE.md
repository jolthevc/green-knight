# Internet Famous: initial story slate (unresearched candidates)

Every title below is a provisional creative direction, NOT an established factual finding or approval to produce.

| ID | Working clickable subject/title | Case-study question | Mechanism / distinction | Editorial risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IF-I0001 | The Internet Made Her Famous for Being Hated (Rebecca Black) | How does ridicule cause mass reach without lasting fandom? | Negative virality vs audience affinity | Do not quantify criticism without evidence; treat harassment with care |
| IF-I0002 | The Face Reveal That Changed Everything (Dream) | Why can anonymity magnify expectations? | Identity projection and parasocial stakes | Prove exactly what changed; avoid cherry-picking backlash |
| IF-I0003 | The App That Made Stars, Then Disappeared (Vine) | How can a platform create careers but fail to retain value? | Creator portability vs platform capture | Avoid monocausal account of platform closure |
| IF-I0004 | An Internet Celebrity Before He Knew It (Alex from Target) | Why is attention different from a durable audience? | Viral spike and conversion | Verify downstream career outcome |
| IF-I0005 | How MrBeast Turned Attention Into an Entertainment Machine | How does repeated spectacle become repeatable production? | Scale, format evolution, creator reinvestment | Avoid unverified finances and algorithm claims |
| IF-I0006 | Why Jenna Marbles Walked Away | What can fame demand from the person behind the persona? | Audience expectations and creator exit | Treat public statements in context |
| IF-I0007 | How Lil Nas X Used the Internet to Break Through | How did participation and memes connect to a hit? | Remix and participatory distribution | Avoid attributing success to one single factor |
| IF-I0008 | From TikTok Fame to Pop Ambition (Addison Rae) | How does platform visibility become another career? | Fame portability vs craft/market validation | Distinguish critical response and sales data |
| IF-I0009 | The Rise of TikTok's First Mega Stars (Charli D'Amelio) | What does perfect platform timing enable? | Format fit, social proof, network effects | Distinguish timing from talent and repeatability |
| IF-I0010 | What Happened to the First Viral Stars? | What determines whether a meme lasts? | Attention decay, participation and ownership | Scope no more than 3 useful cases |
| IF-I0011 | The Meme Everyone Shared but Nobody Owned | Who captures value from collectively produced culture? | Meme diffusion and ownership | Select a specific researched case |
| IF-I0012 | When the Internet Turned on Its Favorite Creator | Why do strong fandoms sometimes reverse? | Parasocial backlash and expectation gaps | Select a well-documented case, avoid pile-ons |
| IF-I0013 | The YouTuber Who Outgrew YouTube | What creates platform-independent leverage? | Brand and distribution portability | Select sourced real case |
| IF-I0014 | How a Creator Built a Community Without Going Viral | Is sustained affinity sometimes more valuable than a spike? | Depth versus reach | Find true case with comparable evidence |
| IF-I0015 | The Viral Moment That Never Became a Career | Why can't every phenomenon be monetized? | Attention, intent and market mismatch | Identify specific defensible subject |

**Recommended pilot candidate:** IF-I0001, only if user selects it. Strong relatable human stakes and a precise, testable distinction between fame and liking. For first-run editorial variation, consider one platform-format episode (Vine) and one creator-growth episode (MrBeast).

Before commissioning: select actual episode, source its central claim, find 3-5 credible surprises, demonstrate why 15 minutes is earned, and create a defensible title promise. No topic is research-complete.
