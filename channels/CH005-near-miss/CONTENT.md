# Content: Near Miss

## 1. Editorial Territory
- **Central:** documented events where large potential harm was averted by judgment, procedure, or luck.
- **Adjacent:** events with limited realized harm (an injured crew member, a damaged plant) where far worse was stopped. State the real harm plainly.
- **Outside:** events where deaths are the core story, speculative future threats, fiction, open investigations.

## 2. Engine
Fixed arc: normal day, hidden flaw, escalation, decision, save, counterfactual. Freshness comes from varying the flaw type, domain, era, and saver: a pilot, a student, a tired engineer, a developer, a failed switch, the weather.

## 3. Pillars

### Launch Core
**Aviation Saves.** Air Canada 759, the Gimli Glider, BA 9, BA 5390. Strong when CVR or ATC records exist and the mechanism is clear. Weak: minor turbulence events, exhaustively covered stories with no new angle.

**Nuclear and Military Close Calls.** Petrov 1983, Goldsboro 1961, Norway 1995, Davis-Besse 2002. Strong when a declassified or regulatory record exists. Weak: single-source legend.

**Flaws Caught in Time.** Citicorp Center 1978, dams, bridges, plants. Strong when there is a discoverer and a fix racing a deadline. Weak: routine maintenance findings.

### Test Pillars
**Digital Near Misses.** The xz Utils backdoor, grid events. Strong with a single detective and a concrete clue. Weak: abstract vulnerability explainers. Y2K is eligible only with its counterfactual labeled disputed.

**Space and Planetary.** Gemini 8, Soyuz MS-10, the July 2012 solar storm. Weak: asteroid scares with no real event.

## 4. Recurring Motifs
The person who noticed. The last safeguard standing. Luck mistaken for design. The secret kept for decades. The fix racing the clock. The ignored warning.

## 5. Format
Standard Reconstruction only: 18 to 24 minutes, full arc. No new formats until the core is proven.

## 6. Topic Selection
A strong topic has: (1) sourced proximity to disaster, (2) a mechanism explainable in one diagram, (3) a decision moment or clear lucky break, (4) a primary record, (5) a recognizable stake, (6) available visual evidence. Reject any topic failing test 1 or 4.

## 7. Framing
Frame around the margin and the saver, not the category.
- **Weak:** Nuclear false alarms of the Cold War.
- **Strong:** The night a Soviet officer decided the computer was wrong about incoming missiles.

## 8. Timeliness
Nearly all evergreen. Recent incidents become eligible only after the final report is published.

## 9. Depth
One event, one core mechanism fully explained, limited context. Counterfactual takes roughly 10 to 15 percent of runtime.

## 10. Off-Brand Near-Misses
- **Chernobyl divers:** inside a real tragedy; the second-explosion claim is contested.
- **Titan submersible:** it happened.
- **Apophis:** a probability update, not an event.

## 11. Failure Modes
- Counterfactuals inflated past the evidence.
- Recycling the famous five stories.
- Every saver a flawless hero; show doubt and error honestly.
- Domain whiplash that breaks the format.

## 12. Expansion
- **Natural:** rail, maritime, chemical, grids.
- **Experimental:** medical (pending privacy review), market circuit breakers.
- **Separate channel:** true tragedies, near-future risk.
