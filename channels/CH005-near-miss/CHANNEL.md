# Channel Definition: Near Miss

## 1. Identity
**Name:** Near Miss  **ID:** CH005

**One line:** A documentary channel that reconstructs real catastrophes that almost happened, ending in an evidence-graded counterfactual.

**Short description:** One documented close call per episode: a jet lined up on a taxiway full of airliners, a skyscraper with a hidden wind flaw, a backdoor slipped into widely used Linux software. We rebuild it moment by moment, show the flaw and the save, then walk through what the record says would have followed.

## 2. Thesis
**Market:** Disaster and investigation documentaries hold loyal, long-watch audiences, but supply is mostly single-domain and grief-centered. A save-centered channel can serve the same appetite with a lower emotional cost and a more advertiser-friendly frame.

**Editorial:** Near misses carry disaster suspense plus resolution. The counterfactual turns relief into awe at the margin and gives the channel a recurring ending viewers can anticipate.

## 3. Why Viewers Subscribe
For the format and the trust, more than the domain: reliable suspense that respects their intelligence, ends in relief, and never exaggerates.

## 4. Scope Rule
In scope when the averted outcome dwarfs any realized harm and the story centers on what was stopped. Minor injury or damage is acceptable if stated plainly. Events where deaths are the core of the story are out.

## 5. Opportunity (judgment, unvalidated)
- Supply is deep; incident databases hold thousands of events, and a few hundred likely clear our story bar.
- Audience overlaps aviation, engineering, Cold War history, and tech viewers.
- Library is highly evergreen.
- Sponsorship fit (security, insurance) is plausible but unproven.

## 6. What This Channel Is Not
Not a tragedy channel, not news, not doomsday prediction, not listicles, not professional training.

## 7. Success Looks Like
- Retention holds through the counterfactual.
- Viewers watch outside their entry pillar.
- The three launch pillars produce reliably strong videos.
- Research load per episode is sustainable.

## 8. Strategic Risks
- **Cross-domain dilution:** aviation fans may not follow into cyber. Mitigated by launching on three core pillars and testing the others.
- **Famous-story exhaustion:** Petrov, Arkhipov, and Apollo 13 are heavily covered; retell only with a fresh document or angle.
- **Counterfactual overreach:** the signature payoff can drift into speculation.
- **Emotional ceiling:** save framing may click below tragedy framing.
- **Research cost:** report-grade sourcing is labor-intensive.

## 9. Hypotheses to Test
1. Save framing earns clicks near the channel median across pillars. Weakened if only titles implying deaths perform.
2. Viewers are format-loyal. Weakened if each pillar behaves like a separate audience.
3. The counterfactual holds late retention. If viewers leave once relief lands, shorten or restructure it.
4. Obscure events perform when given a familiar anchor.

## 10. Open Questions
- Is the name Near Miss distinct and available on YouTube and in trademark search?
- Does the digital pillar keep the core audience, or should the channel stay physical-systems only?
- Minimum evidence standard for a counterfactual claim, to be formalized with the fact-check standard.
- Can medical near misses be sourced without privacy problems? Held back until resolved.
- Default stance on famous stories: avoid, or retell only with new primary material?
