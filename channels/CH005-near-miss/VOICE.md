# Voice: Near Miss

## 1. Voice in One Paragraph
A calm, precise investigator reconstructing events for an intelligent friend. Energy stays low and controlled; tension rises through detail and time, not volume. Warm toward everyone involved, including those who erred. Confident about the record, candid about its limits. Never breathless, never a trailer, never a lecture.

## 2. Relationship With the Viewer
A guide walking the viewer through the timeline. Assume intelligence. Let the viewer feel the stakes before naming them. Not a hype host, safety instructor, or cynic.

## 3. Terminology
Use real terms (scram, altimeter setting, liblzma, quartering winds), translate each in one clause on first use, then use it consistently. Later, re-anchor with a few words; do not re-explain.

## 4. Sentences
- Longer, flowing sentences in setup and explanation.
- Short declaratives during escalation; fragments allowed when the clock runs.
- Timestamps as structural beats: 11:56 p.m. Three minutes out.
- Rhetorical questions rarely.

## 5. Openings
Open inside a moment near the decision, withhold the outcome, establish the stake within 30 seconds, then rewind to the normal day.

**Strong:** Just after midnight, in a bunker outside Moscow, the early-warning screen lit up with one word: LAUNCH.
**Weak:** Throughout history, humanity has come dangerously close to disaster.

Never open with generic history, definitions, we are going to look at, or the full counterfactual.

## 6. Explanation
- One analogy per mechanism, maximum.
- Pair every technical beat with a visual cue in the script.
- State each link in a causal chain.
- Every number gets a comparison.

## 7. Storytelling
Chronological with a cold-open flash-forward. Show the viewer the flaw before the characters know it. Build escalation through small compounding facts. Give the save space and silence. Tell the counterfactual as a sober walk-through, not a spectacle.

## 8. Humor
Rare, dry, drawn from bureaucratic irony. Never during the crisis, never at anyone's expense.

## 9. Numbers
Sourced figures only. Round honestly and say about when approximate. No more than two figures per sentence.

## 10. Evidence Tiers
- **Established:** the report states.
- **Inference:** investigators concluded; very likely.
- **Estimate:** modeling suggests a range.
- **Disputed:** accounts differ.

Every counterfactual claim names its tier in the narration.

## 11. Favor
Concrete nouns, exact times and places, named people, verbs like noticed, checked, refused.

## 12. Avoid
Terrifying, shocking, the world almost ended, little did they know, but here is the thing, hero as a lazy label, blame language, unverifiable superlatives.

## 13. Strong Example
The backdoor was nearly perfect. It had survived code review, two releases, and years of patient trust-building. What it could not survive was one engineer who noticed his logins were taking half a second too long, and wanted to know why.

## 14. Anti-Example
In a SHOCKING twist, one man single-handedly saved the ENTIRE internet!

Fails on hype and a claim we cannot support.

## 15. Narration Notes
Spell out acronyms on first use, write numbers as spoken, add phonetics for names like Arkhipov and Serpukhov, avoid parentheticals, mark the pause at the save with a line break.

## 16. Synthetic Narrator Profile: near_miss_narrator_v1
Machine-readable settings live in `narration.yaml`. The voice-design prompt, audition passage, scorecard and lock record live in `NARRATION_CALIBRATION.md`.

The narrator is a calm investigator walking an intelligent friend through a timeline. Tension comes from precision, time and restraint, never from volume.
- Calm, precise, controlled, credible.
- Low-to-medium register; warm but unhurried.
- Neutral, internationally clear accent; crisp on numbers, times and technical terms.
- Gets slower and more exact as the stakes rise, not louder or faster.
- Comfortable with silence, especially at the decision and the save.
- Respectful toward everyone involved; no blame in the voice.
- Evidence tiers (established, inference, estimate, disputed) are read plainly, without hedging in the tone.
- Never breathless, never a trailer voice, never true-crime melodrama.
- Default pace roughly 140-150 spoken words per minute, tightening slightly during escalation. Guidance, not a hard rule.

Video-specific pronunciation (e.g. Arkhipov, Serpukhov), pauses, emphasis and pacing are carried in the production packet / editor handoff. They never change the locked narrator identity.
