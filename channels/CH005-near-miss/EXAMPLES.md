# Examples: Near Miss

Examples calibrate judgment. Copy the pattern, not the topic.

## 1. Excellent Concepts
**The Skyscraper That Almost Fell on Manhattan (Citicorp Center, 1978).** Hidden wind-load flaw, a race against hurricane season, secret night repairs, a secret kept about 17 years. Pattern: a familiar place with a hidden flaw and a real deadline.

**Half a Second: The xz Backdoor (2024).** A patient attacker, widely used software, one engineer curious about login lag. Tests the digital pillar. Keep the explanation at lock-and-key level. Scope the stake honestly: caught before it reached most stable Linux releases.

**The Weather Rocket That Almost Started a Nuclear War (Norway, 1995).** Obscure event, familiar stake, post-Cold War surprise. Tests the familiar-anchor hypothesis.

## 2. Weak Concepts
- **Top 10 Nuclear Close Calls:** a listicle cannot run the arc. Reframe: the strongest single entry.
- **The Titan Submersible:** it happened. No reframe on this channel.
- **Chernobyl's Heroic Divers:** inside a real tragedy; counterfactual contested. Not recommended.

## 3. Framings
- **Y2K.** Weak: What was the Y2K bug? Strong: The disaster engineers prevented so well that people call it a hoax. Counterfactual labeled disputed.
- **Goldsboro, 1961.** Weak: A B-52 crash in North Carolina. Strong: Two hydrogen bombs fell on North Carolina, and one switch was the last safeguard. Stated as the declassified record documents it.

## 4. Titles
**Strong:** The Skyscraper That Almost Fell on Manhattan. How One Engineer's Slow Login Stopped a Linux Backdoor. The Night a Computer Told the USSR Missiles Were Coming.
**Weak:** Near Misses You Won't Believe (generic). CVE-2024-3094 Explained (technical). This Man Saved 8 Billion People (inflation). Manhattan Almost DESTROYED (sensational).

## 5. Thumbnails
**Strong:** a bolted steel joint circled in amber on a dark blueprint; a red Cyrillic warning screen cropped tight; taxiway lights with aircraft tails ahead, cockpit perspective.
**Weak:** mushroom cloud (implies it happened), hooded hacker (generic), five-disaster collage (no focal point).

## 6. Strong Hook
July 7th, 2017. San Francisco, just before midnight. An Airbus A320 is on final approach, lined up with a strip of lights. It is not the runway. It is a taxiway, and four airliners full of passengers are sitting on it.

Why: stake clear in 15 seconds, outcome withheld.

## 7. Weak Hook
Aviation is one of the safest ways to travel, but sometimes things go wrong. Today we look at a scary incident.

Why: preamble, no stake, announces instead of shows.

## 8. Strong Counterfactual Passage
The investigators did not model a collision, and we will not pretend they did. Here is what the record establishes. The crew began the go-around only seconds before reaching the first aircraft on the taxiway, and passed low over the waiting jets. Here is the inference. Had the go-around come a few seconds later, the A320 would likely have been at the height of the tails ahead of it. What would have followed is beyond the report, and beyond this video.

Why: names each tier, refuses to invent a death toll, and lets the true margin carry the awe.

## 9. Weak Counterfactual Passage
If that pilot had waited one more second, over a thousand people would have died in the worst aviation disaster in history.

Why: unsourced figure, false precision, fear-bait.
