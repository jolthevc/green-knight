# Audience: Near Miss

## 1. Primary Viewer
Aged 25 to 55, often watching on TV or desktop in the evening, often to the end. They have seen aviation breakdowns, nuclear explainers, and engineering-failure videos. They can follow a checklist, schematic, or cockpit transcript when guided. Repeated tragedy drains them.

A video earns 20 minutes if it offers a clear mechanism, a real clock, and a payoff they could not predict.

## 2. Motivation (ranked)
1. Suspense with a safe ending.
2. Mechanism curiosity: how did this fail, and how was it caught?
3. Awe at the margin.
4. Admiration for competence under pressure.
5. Quiet reassurance that systems and people sometimes work.

## 3. Desired Reactions
- I had no idea it came that close.
- That one person noticed that?
- Now I understand why that safeguard exists.
- I need to show someone this.

## 4. Knowledge Baseline
**Assume:** runways, reactors, missiles, malware, load-bearing beams.
**Explain once, with one analogy or diagram:** approach clearances, scram, warhead safing, supply-chain attacks, wind loading, orbital timing.

## 5. Sophistication
Informed generalist. Follows multi-step causal chains, likes real numbers with context, accepts stated uncertainty. Simplify by choosing the one mechanism that matters, not by blurring it.

## 6. Attention Profile
- Lean-back, long-session viewing; 18 to 24 minutes is comfortable.
- Accepts a scene-setting cold open, not a slow preamble.
- Wants pacing that accelerates with the clock.
- Accepts sidebars only when they explain the flaw.

## 7. Emotional Profile
**Arc:** calm, unease, dread, relief, awe.
**Alienating:** gore, grief, panic narration, doomism, mocking people who erred, political point-scoring.

## 8. Boring to This Viewer
Long biographies, untethered regulatory history, number dumps, safety lectures, vague claims of near-apocalypse, AI-sounding narration.

## 9. What They Click
- A recognizable stake: a named city, airport, arsenal, or piece of infrastructure.
- One surprising detail: one switch, half a second of lag.
- A margin, when it is true.
- A person-sized saver.

## 10. Adjacent Audiences
- **Aviation enthusiasts:** welcome; heavy jargon loses the core.
- **Tech and security viewers:** drawn by digital episodes; code walkthroughs lose the core.
- **Cold War history fans:** natural fit; pure political history drifts.
- **Engineers:** value accuracy; textbook depth slows the core.

## 11. Comparable Consumption
Mentour Pilot, Plainly Difficult, Kyle Hill, Real Engineering, Wendover, Air Crash Investigation, Well There's Your Problem.

## 12. Anti-Audience
Professionals wanting certification-grade detail, live-news seekers, conspiracy and doomsday viewers, fans of on-camera personalities, children.

## 13. Assumptions to Test
- **Tension without grief is enough.** Observe CTR and retention across framings. If wrong, lean harder into the size of the stake, never into implied deaths.
- **Format loyalty beats domain loyalty.** Observe cross-pillar returning viewers. If wrong, narrow to the strongest two or three pillars.
- **Graded uncertainty is accepted.** Observe comments and counterfactual retention. If wrong, shorten and tighten claims, never inflate them.
