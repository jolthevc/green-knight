# Packaging: Near Miss

## 1. Philosophy
Clarity of the stake plus the promise of the margin. The viewer should instantly grasp what could have been lost and that it was not. Curiosity lives in how it was stopped and how close it came. Specificity beats intensity.

## 2. Viewer Questions
How close did it come? Who noticed? What one thing stopped it? Why did nobody hear about this?

## 3. Titles
- Title Case, about 60 characters or fewer.
- Name the stake with a recognizable proper noun when it helps.
- Almost, nearly, and stopped are on-brand; rotate them.
- Numbers only when sourced.
- No all-caps words, no stacked punctuation, no implied death tolls.

## 4. Thumbnails
- One focal object where the flaw or save lived.
- Dark ground, amber highlight on the critical detail; red only when it is in the real evidence (a warning screen) or marks the decision.
- Three words maximum.
- Faces only of real, documented, central people. No stock screaming faces.
- Never explosions or wreckage for events that did not happen.

## 5. Title and Thumbnail Roles
The title carries the stake; the thumbnail carries the detail. Example: The Skyscraper That Almost Fell on Manhattan, with a bolted steel joint circled in amber.

## 6. Curiosity Without Misrepresentation
Packaging may withhold the mechanism and the saver. It may not claim a magnitude the counterfactual cannot support, imply casualties, or imply the event is ongoing. If the true margin is modest, package the mechanism instead.

## 7. Familiarity vs Novelty
Give obscure events a familiar anchor: Norway 1995 becomes The Weather Rocket That Almost Started a Nuclear War. Famous events need a fresh document or angle.

## 8. Process
1. Identify the stake, the truest margin, and the saver or lucky break.
2. Draft 3 to 5 title territories: stake, detail, saver, secret.
3. Develop thumbnail objects, pair, critique.
4. Check every claim against the counterfactual's evidence tier.

## 9. Strong Patterns
- **The [Thing] That Almost [Outcome]:** clear; overuse risk.
- **How [Clue] Stopped [Stake]:** strong for flaws and digital.
- **[Stake] Was [Margin] From [Outcome]:** sourced margin only.
- **The Secret [Near Miss] Nobody Told [Group]:** only when a real secret existed.

## 10. Weak Patterns
Top 10 Scariest Near Misses (listicle). Analysis of Wind Loading Error (too technical). This Changed Everything (vague). Millions DIED... Almost (bait).

## 11. Thumbnail Calibration
**Strong:** a cropped warning screen reading LAUNCH; a cockpit view lined up on taxiway lights with aircraft ahead; a terminal with one highlighted line. Each reads in one second: a dangerous thing plus one wrong detail.
**Weak:** mushroom clouds, burning planes, collages, hoodie hackers, red arrows, heavy text.

## 12. Anti-Patterns
Photoreal AI disaster imagery, fear-bait, imitating disaster-channel thumbnails, one recycled composition every episode.

## 13. Experiments
Stake-led vs saver-led titles. Object vs documented-face thumbnails. Margin number vs none. Famous name vs familiar anchor.

## 14. Opening Alignment
Within 60 seconds, the video shows the title's stake, establishes the thumbnail's detail, and confirms the outcome is being withheld. Any margin in the title is substantiated in the counterfactual.
