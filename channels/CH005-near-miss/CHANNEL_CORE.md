# Channel Core: Near Miss (CH005)

## 1. Channel in One Sentence
Near Miss reconstructs, minute by minute, real catastrophes that almost happened. Each episode shows the hidden flaw, the escalation, the moment someone caught it (or luck did), and an evidence-graded account of what would have followed.

## 2. Core Viewer
An adult, roughly 25 to 55, who already watches aviation, engineering, and disaster breakdowns for the tension and the mechanism, not the body count. They find repeated tragedy draining. They want the adrenaline without the funeral.

## 3. Viewer Promise
A true, sourced story with real stakes and a good ending. You will understand the exact flaw, feel the clock run down, see what stopped it, and learn honestly how close it came. When this channel says close, it was close.

## 4. Positioning
Disaster channels are mostly single-domain and built on tragedy. Near Miss is built on the save and works across domains. Its durable edge is not the look. It is the format plus the trust: a fixed arc and a counterfactual that states its evidence tier instead of inflating the margin.

## 5. Content Engine
One fixed arc (normal day, hidden flaw, escalation, decision, save, counterfactual) applied to a deep, growing record of documented close calls: NTSB, AAIB, NRC, and NASA reports, declassified files, security disclosures, and engineering case studies.

## 6. Pillars
**Launch core:** Aviation Saves; Nuclear and Military Close Calls; Flaws Caught in Time.
**Test pillars:** Digital Near Misses; Space and Planetary. Each earns a permanent slot only if it holds the core audience.

## 7. Voice
A calm, exact investigator telling the story to a smart friend. Tension comes from timestamps, specifics, and withheld outcomes, never adjectives. Mechanisms get plain language and one analogy. The narrator respects everyone involved, separates luck from judgment, and names the evidence tier of every counterfactual claim. Humor is rare and dry.

## 8. Packaging
The title names a recognizable stake and signals that it almost happened. The thumbnail shows the single object where the flaw or save lived: a joint, a screen, a switch, a line of code. Never show a catastrophe as if it occurred. If the true margin is modest, package the mechanism, not the margin.

## 9. Visual Identity
Dark, evidence-first, instrument-panel documentary. Real documents, transcripts, photos, and schematics lead. A hero diagram carries every mechanism. The Clock tracks time during escalation. Amber marks the flaw; red marks the decision. Counterfactuals use a labeled, desaturated blue-grey stylized mode that can never pass as footage.

## 10. Ten Rules
### Do
1. Anchor every story in a primary record.
2. Make the flaw understandable before the crisis peaks.
3. Let sourced timestamps create the tension.
4. Label every counterfactual claim: established, inference, estimate, or disputed.
5. Credit the specific people and processes behind the save.

### Do Not
1. Cover events where the catastrophe happened or deaths are the center of the story.
2. Inflate distance-to-disaster beyond the source.
3. Use photoreal AI imagery of real events, real people, or disasters that never occurred.
4. Make listicles, compilations, or coverage of open investigations.
5. Moralize, mock the people who erred, or use fear-bait packaging.
