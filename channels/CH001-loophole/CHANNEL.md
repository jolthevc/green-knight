# Channel Definition: Loophole

## 1. Identity
**Name:** Loophole

**ID:** CH001

**One line:** A faceless story channel that shows strategy-minded sports fans why rules exist by reconstructing the exploits that forced them to be rewritten.

**Short description:** Each episode takes one rule and tells its origin through whoever broke it without technically breaking it. Examples include a basketball game that ended 19-18, a Formula 1 car with a fan bolted to the back, and a soccer team that scored on itself on purpose. Each story covers the exploit, the outrage, the rewrite, and what the sport became.

## 2. Thesis
**Market:** Rules content performs within single sports, but no brand owns cross-sport rule origins told as stories. Recognizable leagues give titles built-in hooks. Gamers already consume exploit narratives heavily.

**Editorial:** The loophole arc is naturally dramatic. It has:
- a clever protagonist
- an institution caught flat-footed
- a letter-versus-spirit question
- a concrete resolution

The structure is portable, so a viewer who comes for cricket can stay for F1.

## 3. Why Viewers Subscribe
Viewers subscribe for the exploit, not a sport. They stay for:
- a reliable arc
- a voice that makes unfamiliar sports legible
- the feeling of becoming the fan who knows why the rules are the way they are

## 4. Differentiation
The difference is durable because it lives in the editorial engine, not in naming or aesthetics. The channel combines cross-sport breadth, a fixed arc that always ends in a rule change, and an admiring-but-fair tone. The Amendment visual makes that engine recognizable.

## 5. Experience
Watching should feel like a heist story with a rules lesson inside it. The pace is brisk at setup, patient at the mechanism, and satisfying at the redline. A newcomer to the sport never feels lost.

## 6. Opportunity and Economics
The opportunity is global, multi-sport, and highly evergreen.

**Monetization is a hypothesis, not a forecast:**
- a sports-fan advertising profile
- equipment affiliates
- sponsors, subject to portfolio policy

**Supply is the real constraint:**
- Famous, well-documented cases likely number in the dozens.
- Mid-fame cases and Rulebook Arcs must carry the long tail.

## 7. Not This Channel
- Not news or hot takes.
- Not doping, corruption, or match-fixing stories.
- Not weird-rules listicles or rankings.
- Not betting tips.
- Not a reference for officials.

## 8. Success Looks Like
- Recognizable-case episodes earning organic distribution.
- Viewers crossing sports.
- At least two pillars producing winners.
- Mid-fame cases holding up on strong framing.
- A graphics-led model that holds quality without broadcast footage.

## 9. Strategic Risks
- **Rights (largest):** The channel only works if diagrams, stills, and clippings can carry the visuals.
- **Supply:** The famous tier may exhaust quickly.
- **Loyalty:** Viewers may subscribe to their sport rather than to the format.
- **Accuracy:** Popular single-cause origin myths are often wrong.
- **Repetition:** Stalling and tanking stories recur across sports and can blur together.

## 10. Hypotheses to Test
- **Cross-sport binge:** Supported by suggested and end-screen traffic between sports. Weakened by sport-siloed viewing.
- **Graphics carry retention:** Weakened if drop-offs cluster in diagram-only sections.
- **Framing beats fame:** Supported if mid-fame cases compete on CTR when strongly packaged.

## 11. Open Questions
- Rights posture for short fair-use archival clips. This needs a portfolio decision before the first video.
- Synthetic or human narration. The dry tone depends on delivery.
- Whether betting-adjacent sponsors are acceptable under portfolio policy.
- Whether esports cases belong in the initial slate or later.
- How deep the supply really goes. Validate this by building a 40-topic backlog before launch.
