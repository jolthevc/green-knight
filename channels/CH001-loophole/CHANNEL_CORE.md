# Channel Core: Loophole (CH001)

## 1. Channel in One Sentence
Loophole is a faceless long-form channel that tells the stories of clever, technically legal exploits that forced sports to rewrite their rules.

## 2. Core Viewer
The core viewer is a fan who cares more about how the game works than about last night's highlights. They follow one or two sports closely. They will happily watch a great story from a sport they don't follow. They love the moment someone finds a gap nobody else saw.

## 3. Viewer Promise
Every episode delivers one complete arc:
- the rule as written
- the person who found the gap
- the exploit in action
- the backlash
- the rewrite
- how the sport adapted

You leave understanding the rule better than most fans, with a story worth retelling.

## 4. Positioning
Nearby channels each stop short of this territory:
- Tactics channels analyze the current season.
- Sports history channels lean on biography.
- Rules explainers stay inside one sport.

Loophole owns cross-sport, story-first rule origins. It also owns one specific pleasure: admiring a move that was technically legal.

## 5. Content Engine
Every rule has an origin, and many origins are exploits. The fixed arc runs loophole, exploit, backlash, rule change, adaptation. It works across pro leagues, Olympic sports, and motorsport.

Supply is mined systematically from three sources:
- governing-body amendment histories
- eponymous rules, such as "the Sean Avery rule"
- each off-season's rule changes

The occasional Rulebook Arc episode traces one heavily patched rule through several exploits, which extends supply without becoming a listicle.

## 6. Pillars
- **Tactical Exploits:** in-play moves that used the letter of the rules against their intent.
- **Format and Incentive Breaks:** competition structures that made losing, stalling, or scoring on yourself the rational choice.
- **Engineering the Gap:** equipment that beat regulations written for older technology.

## 7. Voice
The narrator is a dry, precise storyteller who plainly enjoys the exploiter and still respects the sport. Each script quotes the rule, then shows the gap. Humor comes from understatement and from real absurdity, never from mocking fans or players. The narrator explains sport-specific mechanics in one or two plain sentences, states facts flatly, and flags disputes explicitly.

## 8. Packaging
Each package pairs a recognizable anchor with an impossible-seeming act. The title states the act or its consequence and withholds the mechanism. The thumbnail shows one object, scoreboard, or diagram. Packages never call a legal move cheating and never say "banned" when a rule was only amended.

## 9. Visual Identity
The channel looks like a rulebook come alive:
- paper-and-ink textures
- original tactical diagrams
- archival stills and clippings as evidence
- the signature Amendment, where old rule text is struck through in red and the new clause is written in

The palette is off-white paper, ink black, flag yellow for the exploiter, and amendment red. The channel is built from graphics, not broadcast footage.

## 10. Ten Rules

**Do**
1. Quote the actual rule before showing the exploit.
2. Finish the arc, including adaptation.
3. Give the exploiter's logic a fair hearing.
4. Recreate plays with original, labeled diagrams.
5. Use rule news only as a door into an evergreen origin.

**Do Not**
1. Cover plain cheating, such as doping or match-fixing.
2. Make weird-rules listicles.
3. Rely on unlicensed broadcast footage.
4. Claim single-cause rule changes the record doesn't support.
5. Sneer at the sport, officials, or fans.
