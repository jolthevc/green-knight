# Production Standard

This extends EDITOR_CREATIVE_STANDARD.md, VISUAL_QUALITY_STANDARD.md, EDITOR_BRIEF_TEMPLATE.md, and NARRATION_STANDARD.md.

## 1. Model
**AI-assisted (Green Knight):**
- Ideation and backlog mining.
- Research, including sourcing the exact rule text.
- Outline and script.
- Packaging concepts.
- The production packet, with diagram specifications.
- Channel narrator definition and calibration materials.

**Human:**
- One editor with motion-graphics ability.
- Narration generation and timing using the locked Loophole voice.
- Thumbnail execution by the editor or a designer.
- Rights review.
- Fact QA.
- Final approval.

Judgment concentrates in narration delivery, diagram design, archival selection, and pacing. Research is finished before the edit, so the editor never rediscovers the story.

## 2. Format
- Long-form, 16:9, 1080p minimum (4K preferred), 24 or 30 fps.
- Runtime of 11-18 minutes, with a target of 14.
- Proposed calibration cadence of 2 videos per month.
- Narration: synthetic channel voice, selected and locked by Green Knight; generated by the editor during production.
- Narrator readiness: REQUIRED. The Loophole narrator profile must be locked before production begins. Finished narration audio is not required before PRODUCTION_READY.

## 3. Narration
Green Knight owns the approved words and the Loophole narrator identity. The editor owns how that approved narration breathes against the picture.

Green Knight provides:
- the approved final script
- the locked Loophole narrator profile
- provider voice access
- baseline model/settings where recorded
- pronunciation notes
- the channel voice standard

The editor generates narration in the approved provider as part of production.

**The editor may:**
- Generate and regenerate approved lines or passages.
- Split narration wherever useful for the edit.
- Adjust pauses, spacing, emphasis, and delivery pace within the channel style.
- Create room for visual reveals or diagrams.
- Modestly time-stretch clips while preserving pitch.
- Iterate narration while seeing the actual picture.

**The editor may not** silently change the spoken wording, factual meaning, or narrator identity. If a line needs different wording, it returns upstream to editorial.

Relevant narration exports should be retained under `Production/Narration/` when useful for revision or transferability. Green Knight does not require an automated segment/master/manifest structure.

## 4. Inputs Before the Edit
- Approved script.
- Locked Loophole narrator profile and editor access to the approved voice.
- Production packet.
- Source log with rule-text extracts and clipping provenance.
- Diagram specifications.
- Licensed assets, or a list of assets to license.
- Thumbnail brief with 2-3 concepts.
- Pronunciation notes.

## 5. Packet
The packet is organized by section. Each section contains:
- Narration block.
- Visual objective.
- REQUIRED diagram specification: actors, sequence, and the highlighted exploiter.
- Exact old and new rule text for the Amendment, with the year.
- Preferred archival items.
- Pacing notes.

Secondary visuals are marked OPEN.

## 6. Responsibilities
**Editor:**
- Narration generation, iteration, and timing using the locked channel voice.
- Diagram animation.
- Sourcing from approved libraries.
- Pacing, music, and sound.
- Typography within the brand kit.
- Flagging any visual or narration issue that could misstate a play or rule.

**Green Knight:**
- Thesis, research, and approved script.
- Narrator identity, voice selection, and revocable provider access.
- Rule verification.
- Brand kit: fonts, palette, token library, and Amendment template.
- Rights decisions.
- Final approval.

## 7. Thumbnails
The thumbnail is executed after the first cut, from the packaging concepts. It requires Green Knight approval.

## 8. Revisions
- Two rounds.
- Notes are tagged MUST FIX, STRONG PREFERENCE, or OPTIONAL THOUGHT.
- Script or claim changes return to editorial. This includes any change to narration wording.
- Delivery-only narration changes remain within editor latitude.

## 9. Channel QA
- Narration matches the approved script.
- Narrator identity remains consistent with the locked Loophole profile.
- Diagrams match documented sequences.
- Amendment text and year match the real rule.
- On-screen scores and dates are correct.
- Recreations and archival material are labeled.
- No unlicensed league footage or logos.
- The package promise lands by 30 seconds.
- Multi-cause rule changes are acknowledged.

## 10. Rights
- No league broadcast footage by default. Any fair-use clip requires explicit rights review.
- Stills from licensed agencies or the public domain.
- Clipping provenance recorded.
- Licensed music only.
- AI assets follow VISUAL_STYLE.md.

## 11. Delivery
Files:
- CH001-V0001_cut_v1.mp4
- CH001-V0001_master.mp4
- CH001-V0001_thumb_final.png
- CH001-V0001_captions.srt

Retain relevant narration exports, project files, diagram sources, and additions to the reusable token and Amendment library when useful for future revisions or contractor transfer.

Access is revocable at Drive folder and voice-platform level. Raw provider API keys are never shared with editors.

## 12. Cost
Target cost is set during calibration of the first five videos. Track editor, thumbnail, voice-platform, and archival licensing costs as useful. Licensing and graphics hours are the main variables to watch. The reusable library should reduce diagram time over successive episodes.

## 13. Failure Modes
- Generic diagrams where the exploiter isn't highlighted.
- Random sports stock.
- Broadcast-clip shortcuts.
- A formulaic Amendment.
- Graphics scope creep inflating cost.
- Narration wording or narrator identity silently changed in the edit.
- Treating the baseline TTS settings as more important than natural delivery against picture.
