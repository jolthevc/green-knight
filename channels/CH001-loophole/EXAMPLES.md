# Examples and Anti-Examples

These examples calibrate judgment. Copy the reasoning, not the topics.

## 1. Excellent Concepts

### The Fan Car: Brabham BT46B, 1978
- **Fit:** Fans were allowed if their primary purpose was cooling. The car won its only race, the Swedish GP with Niki Lauda, and was then withdrawn.
- **Strength:** An iconic object, a quotable clause, and a fast backlash.
- **Lesson:** The clause gap is the story. Motorsport is incidental.

### Barbados vs Grenada, 1994
- **Fit:** Barbados needed to win by two. A golden goal counted double. Late in the game, an own goal forcing extra time became the smart play, and Grenada ended up attacking both nets.
- **Strength:** The outcome sounds impossible in one sentence.
- **Lesson:** Obscure teams are fine when the absurdity carries the story. The "rule change" here was abandonment of an experimental rule, and the script must say so rather than inflating it.

### Pistons 19, Lakers 18, 1950
- **Fit:** The Pistons stalled to neutralize George Mikan. The 24-second clock arrived in 1954.
- **Strength:** A rule every fan knows, with a surprising origin.
- **Lesson:** Present the game as the famous symptom, not the sole cause.

## 2. Weak Concepts
- **Top 10 Weirdest Sports Rules**
  - Why it tempts: search interest.
  - Why it fails: no arc, no exploit, and interchangeable with any channel.
  - Reframe: one rule, one origin exploit.
- **Lance Armstrong**
  - Why it tempts: fame.
  - Why it fails: plain cheating.
  - Reframe: none.
- **Deflategate**
  - Why it tempts: it is rule-related.
  - Why it fails: a disputed violation, not a gap.
  - Reframe: the Patriots' 2015 ineligible-receiver formations, which prompted an NFL rule change.

## 3. Framings
- **Olympic badminton 2012**
  - Weak: "The badminton scandal."
  - Strong: "A group stage made losing the best strategy, so eight Olympians tried to lose."
- **2008-09 swimsuits**
  - Weak: "Swimsuit technology."
  - Strong: "The suits that broke the record books so badly swimming outlawed them."

## 4. Titles
**Strong:**
- "The F1 Car So Good It Only Raced Once": truthful, and makes the viewer ask why.
- "They Scored on Themselves to Win": a pure act. The opening explains it at once.
- "Why Keepers Can't Pick Up Back Passes": a familiar rule with a hidden origin.

**Weak:**
- "The Spirit of the Game": abstract.
- "The Biggest Cheat in F1 History": false, because the car was legal.
- "Analyzing FIFA Law 12 Amendments": technical and joyless.

## 5. Thumbnails
**Strong:**
- The fan car's rear fan, isolated, with a red redline. It reads as an outlawed object.
- A scoreboard reading 19-18. It reads as an impossibly low score.

**Weak:**
- A stadium panorama with text: no focal point.
- A shocked emoji over rule text: off-brand and illegible.

## 6. Hooks
**Strong:**
> "On the last ball of the match, New Zealand needed six to tie. Australia's captain had an idea that was completely legal. He told his brother to roll the ball along the ground."

This works because it gives stakes, legality, and the act in three sentences, with the mechanism and the backlash still to come.

**Weak:**
> "Cricket is a sport full of tradition, and in 1981 something happened that nobody would forget."

This fails because it is a preamble with a vague promise and no act.

## 7. Imitation Warning
Do not clone the stall story across sports, and do not put the redline on every thumbnail. Each new episode needs its own one-line absurdity.
