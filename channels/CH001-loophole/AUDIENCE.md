# Audience Definition

## 1. Primary Viewer
The primary viewer is a fan in their mid-twenties to thirties.
- They follow the NBA, the NFL, or the Premier League weekly but skip highlight packages.
- They argue with friends about whether a play was legal.
- They'd rather see how a team gamed the system than watch a dunk.
- They watch YouTube in the evening, on a TV or laptop, and choose 12-18-minute videos deliberately.

A video earns their time when it teaches them something their group chat doesn't know.

## 2. Motivations (in order)
1. Mechanism curiosity: how was that legal?
2. Admiration for someone outsmarting the system.
3. Insider status: knowing why a rule exists.
4. Debate: should that have been allowed?
5. Nostalgia for eras of their own sport.

## 3. Desired Payoff
- "Wait, that was legal?"
- "So that's why that rule exists."
- "I kind of respect it."
- "Sending this to the chat."

## 4. Knowledge Baseline
**Assume:** the basic shape of major sports, such as scoring, periods, and fouls.

**Explain in one or two plain sentences:** sport-specific mechanics such as downforce, golden goal, ineligible receivers, and underarm bowling. Also explain how the relevant governing body changes rules.

**Never assume:** that the viewer follows this sport.

## 5. Sophistication and Attention
The viewer is a fan-literate generalist.
- They follow a multi-step causal chain if each step is shown visually.
- They enjoy letter-versus-intent nuance.
- They reject legalese and engineering beyond what the exploit needs.
- They will sit through 12-18 minutes but need the absurd outcome within 20 seconds.
- They accept short context sidebars only when those sidebars set up the gap.

## 6. Emotional Profile
**Target emotions:** amused, impressed, mildly scandalized, satisfied.

**Alienating tones:**
- preachy moralizing
- fan-war tribalism
- true-crime gravity
- hype-reel shouting

## 7. Boring to This Viewer
- Long biographies before the exploit.
- Rulebook recitation without a story.
- Season recaps and stat dumps.
- "Changed the sport forever" claims.
- Sportsmanship lectures.

## 8. What They Click
- Famous names paired with absurd outcomes.
- Banned or outlawed objects.
- Rules they see every week, told through their origins.

For lesser-known sports, the absurd act, not the sport name, earns the click.

## 9. Adjacent Audiences
- **Gamers and speedrunners:** They already love exploit stories. Occasional esports or patch-note parallels can broaden appeal. Gaming jargon would damage the core audience.
- **Casual sports-curious viewers:** Reach them through recognizable cases. Never simplify the mechanism to do so.
- **Non-US fans:** Reach them through cricket, rugby, soccer, and F1. Avoid US-only framing.

## 10. Comparable Consumption
- **Secret Base / Jon Bois:** story-first sports weirdness.
- **Summoning Salt:** exploit escalation.
- **Tifo Football:** illustrated tactics.
- **Chain Bear:** F1 technical clarity.

## 11. Anti-Audience
- Viewers seeking live analysis or betting picks.
- Officials wanting a technical reference.
- Viewers wanting scandal exposés.
- Viewers wanting highlight footage.

## 12. Assumptions to Test
- **Viewers cross sports.**
  - How to observe: traffic between sports.
  - If wrong: consider sport-led playlists or series.
- **International appeal.**
  - How to observe: geography in analytics.
  - If wrong: rebalance the sport mix.
- **The audience skews toward adult sports fans.**
  - How to observe: demographics in analytics.
  - If wrong: revisit the monetization hypothesis.
