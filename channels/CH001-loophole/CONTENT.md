# Content Definition

## 1. Territory
**Central:** rule changes provoked by legal or ambiguous exploits in pro leagues, Olympic sports, and motorsport.

**Adjacent (needs a genuine legal edge):**
- Scandals that exposed a real gap.
- Dominance-driven rule changes where the player used the rules as written.
- Esports patches, later.

**Outside:**
- Doping, bribery, and match-fixing.
- Officiating controversies with no rule change.
- General sports history.

## 2. Engine
Every episode follows the same arc: loophole, exploit, backlash, rule change, adaptation. Setting, era, and mechanism change each time. The question stays constant: clever or unsporting?

**Supply is mined, not remembered.** Sources include:
- governing-body amendment histories and meeting minutes
- rules named after people
- "primary purpose"-style clauses
- each off-season's rule changes, traced back to what provoked them

## 3. Pillars

### Tactical Exploits
**Definition:** Players or coaches using the letter of in-play rules against their intent.

**Strong topics:** a visible, recreatable play and a documented rule response.

**Weak topics:** subtle refereeing interpretations.

**Examples:**
- Pistons 19-18 and the shot clock.
- Hack-a-Shaq.
- The soccer back-pass rule.
- The Sean Avery rule.
- The Berkoff blastoff.
- The 1981 underarm delivery.
- The Patriots' 2015 ineligible-receiver formations.

### Format and Incentive Breaks
**Definition:** The competition structure itself made perverse play rational.

**How it differs from Tactical Exploits:** here the rules of play were followed, but the format rewarded the wrong outcome.

**Strong topics:** an outcome that sounds impossible in one sentence.

**Weak topics:** seeding math with no drama.

**Examples:**
- Barbados vs Grenada, 1994.
- The Disgrace of Gijon, 1982.
- Olympic badminton, 2012.

**Expansion:** tanking and draft-lottery reform, used sparingly.

### Engineering the Gap
**Definition:** Equipment that beat rules written for older technology.

**Strong topics:** one iconic object with a dramatic debut.

**Weak topics:** incremental spec changes.

**Examples:**
- The Brabham BT46B fan car.
- The Brawn double diffuser.
- The 2008-09 polyurethane swimsuits.

**Expansion:** golf, cycling, bats.

## 4. Formats
- **Case File (default):** one exploit through the full arc, 12-16 minutes.
- **Rulebook Arc (occasional):** one rule patched repeatedly, such as offside, backstroke underwater distance, or F1 aero, 15-18 minutes. Each exploit must escalate from the last, or it becomes a listicle.

## 5. Topic Selection (in order)
1. An impossible-sounding outcome stated in one line.
2. A documented rule change.
3. Sources available, including rule text and contemporary press.
4. The play can be recreated visually.
5. Recognizability.

## 6. Framing
Every framing names the gap and the consequence.

- **Weak:** "The history of the shot clock."
- **Strong:** "One team refused to shoot so one player couldn't beat them, and basketball eventually needed a clock."

## 7. Evergreen vs Timely
The channel is overwhelmingly evergreen. New rules are hooks into origins only. No breaking news.

## 8. Depth and Accuracy
- One core mechanism per episode, explained completely.
- Context only as the gap requires.
- Where a rule had several causes, say so on screen and in narration.

## 9. Near Misses
- **Deflategate:** a disputed violation, not a gap.
- **Fosbury flop:** no backlash. It is viable only if framed around why the rules allowed it.
- **Lance Armstrong:** cheating.

## 10. Failure Modes
- Stalling and tanking stories blurring together. Space them out and vary the pillar.
- Clustering in one sport. Avoid long same-sport runs in a slate.
- Single-cause overclaims.
- Chasing obscure cases with no one-line absurdity.
- Dropping the adaptation beat.

## 11. Content Tests
- Do format breaks outperform engineering stories when the cases are similarly famous?
- Do strongly framed mid-fame cases compete with famous ones? The first slate should include both.

The answers set pillar weighting and how deep into supply the channel can go.
