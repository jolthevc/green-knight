# Pretty Penny: creative contract
The entertainment is **recognition turning into disbelief**. A familiar purchase, fee, product or ordinary behavior reveals a surprising economic machine. Viewers should feel they will never look at the object in quite the same way again.

**Universal hook:** Start with something people do, buy or resent, not a company earnings report. Make one everyday question unusually difficult to answer. Possible hook families: why the apparently free benefit has a payer; who makes money from a low-price product; who controls a fragmented-looking market.

**Dramatic motor:** Follow the object's dollar, incentive or risk through a small number of participants. A reveal must actually change who the audience believes pays, benefits or has leverage. Contradict the easy explanation with sourced costs and incentives.

**Signature payoff:** One clean, memorable truth about the hidden arrangement, plus the consequential exception or loser. No unverified 'every dollar' pie charts.

**Creative red flags:** long stacks of unit economics, all episodes tracing a receipt identically, generic pricing tips, obscure B2B finance without familiar stakes, monetization-first topic selection.

**Distinct from others:** A present-day or recurring money system, not a founder's one decision (Money Moves), a complete fortune-building path (First Billion) or a one-off deal (Changing Hands).

**Pilot test:** Can a non-expert explain where money goes after hearing once? Is the premise understandable as a fascinating everyday paradox in one title and one frame? Can we test a visual money flow without the animation doing all the storytelling?

**Preferred opening experiment:** Run one object-centered cold open against one apparent-benefit-versus-real-payer cold open. Keep whichever is accurate and leads to a sharper second question.

Green Knight now owns the title/thumbnail promise, first frame, detailed first 20–35 seconds, narration dynamics, story movements and breathing points, visual sequence design with recurring environments and transformations, selective evidence/text, and ending callback. The editor executes the work, rather than independently choosing the story's visual conception. Read business/EDITOR_HANDOFF_STANDARD.md and business/DIRECTOR_PACKET_REVIEW.md; these supersede any earlier channel-specific editor-owned guidance.

Script, performance, visual plan and their integration MUST all pass independent and joined QA after all revisions. No formerly scripted episode automatically qualifies for packet approval.
