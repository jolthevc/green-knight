# Proposed visual identity

Warm editorial ink illustration for adults: approachable, expressive and clearly handmade in appearance. Cream backgrounds, dark slightly imperfect outlines, simple flat shapes and a few deliberate colors. Avoid childish proportions, glossy 3D rendering and visual clutter.

## Palette

| Color | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ivory | #F7F3E8 | Default background |
| Charcoal | #252822 | Text, ink lines, primary contrast |
| Forest | #214E3B | Primary emphasis, money routes |
| Sage | #A7B89A | Secondary fills, supporting objects |
| Terracotta | #C97456 | Costs, friction, contrasting emphasis |
| Ochre | #D7AA49 | Spotlight, reveal, selected detail |

Use charcoal on ivory for reading. Pale accents are fills, not small text colors. Check contrast in the rendered frame; color alone must not carry meaning. Use only two or three accent colors per scene.

## Typography

Fraunces Semibold: short chapter/title cards and editorial headlines.
DM Sans Regular/Medium: explanatory labels, captions and chart annotations.
DM Sans Bold: numeric emphasis and thumbnail text.

Keep long text out of frames. Prefer sentence case, generous spacing and a clear hierarchy. Check actual font licenses and availability in the editor's tools. If unavailable, propose one visually comparable serif/sans pair before substituting. Fonts are a pilot recommendation, not a previously delivered asset package.

## Reusable visual vocabulary

Receipts, coins, wallets, arrows, counters, labeled containers, simple storefronts and expressive simplified adult characters. Maintain stroke weight, proportions, shadow convention and illustration texture across scenes. No mascot or logo is locked yet.

Show the mechanism changing: a dollar travels, fees split, a cost stack builds, inventory turns or ownership moves. Use selective motion and purposeful reveals instead of animating every limb. Evidence documents may appear as clearly sourced inserts inside the illustration system.

Create a short pilot style board showing one character, a money-flow scene, a data graphic, a title card and a thumbnail. The editor should recommend the easiest sustainable approach and challenge choices that hurt readability, storytelling or production economics.

## Production checks

Maintain stable art direction even if tools change. Label reconstructions and illustrative figures. Avoid generated fake screenshots, faux headlines and invented logos presented as evidence. Keep real quotes and numbers as editable verified text overlays. Check phone-size readability, captions and end-screen safe space.

Templates establish consistent identity; each episode still needs original research, visual storytelling and editing. Production method alone does not guarantee reach or monetization.
