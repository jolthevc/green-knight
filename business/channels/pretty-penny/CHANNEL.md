# Editorial lane

Explain how the economics of a familiar thing actually work. The protagonist is a system or money mechanism, even when a company anchors the story.

## Viewer and payoff

A curious adult should understand the story without finance training. Start from an experience they recognize: paying, waiting, booking, buying, borrowing or being charged. End with an answer about who pays, who benefits, which incentives shape the experience and what the intuitive explanation missed.

Broad appeal comes from recognizable stakes and surprising explanations, not removing complexity. Explain unfamiliar terms when they first matter. No stock picks or personal investment instructions.

## Pillars and recurring formats

| Format | Central question | Examples of subjects, not researched episodes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Receipt | Where does the customer's dollar go? | Coffee, concert tickets, delivery |
| Hidden Empires | Who quietly controls a familiar category? | Suppliers, distribution, licensing |
| Why It's Like This | Which economic incentive created an odd practice? | Loyalty programs, bundling, pricing |
| Follow the Fees | Who funds the apparent benefit? | Cards, insurance, mortgages |
| The Business Model | How does an apparently strange offer make money? | Memberships, freemium, marketplaces |

These are series inside Pretty Penny, not separate channels. Do not force every episode into a literal itemized receipt when evidence cannot support the allocation.

## Boundaries

A company biography alone is insufficient. Collapse-centered episodes belong to Fallen Angels. A pivotal strategic decision belongs to Money Moves. Discrete high-stakes transactions belong to Changing Hands. Deception mechanics belong to Fool’s Gold. Family inheritance and control belong to Silver Spoon.

Allowed overlap: illustrate a current system with a historic decision or failure, while the dominant promise stays how the money works. A reused subject requires a different mechanism and explicit connection to earlier IDs.

## Standards

Research can overturn the initial hypothesis. Distinguish verified fact, plausible interpretation and an illustrative model. Do not assert that the hidden beneficiary always takes the most profit without evidence. Show a counterpoint and close with the most useful supported insight.

Revenue ambitions influence topic selection but never substitute for appeal, accuracy or suitability. A high-RPM claim remains a hypothesis until measured.
