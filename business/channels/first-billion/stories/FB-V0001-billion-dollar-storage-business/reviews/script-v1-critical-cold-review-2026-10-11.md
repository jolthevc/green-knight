## Three-sentence cold viewer experience

The familiar row of storage doors initially sounds accessible, but an undefined billion-dollar ambition soon collapses into a lecture about assumed property revenue and cap rates before anything consequential happens. Halfway through, the script becomes a tour of acquisitions, developments and platform economics, so there is no clear protagonist's constraint or irreversible turning point. The ending recites the same modeled figures and adds generic business vocabulary, leaving the listener informed about basic accounting but not surprised enough by the difference between buying assets and creating wealth.

**Strongest moment to preserve:** P017–P019, where changing income, market pricing and debt causes a dramatic loss of equity even though the buildings remain. **Weakest stretch:** P011–P016, especially P012–P015's acquisition/development/management detour. **Overall verdict:** **REVISIONS_REQUIRED**, driven by P1 narrative and promise issues, not a mere stylistic preference.

## Five highest-leverage issues, with priorities

1. **P1: Script's central promise conflates a large acquired asset portfolio with wealth creation.** v1 P009–P011/P021. Listener might leave believing buying $2.1bn of property with $1bn investor equity automatically creates founder wealth. **Fix:** explicit fair-price acquisition distinction and capital sourcing before big scale. **Evidence:** scenario framing and owner-rights bridge, no invented historical facts.
2. **P1: Missing decision-driven spine.** v1 P009→P011→P012–P015. "Multiply to 180" precedes the very problem of funding and selection. **Fix:** show one property and its modeled valuation, *then* what it costs to buy, then available growth routes, then 180. **Evidence:** S003/S004 real scale examples remain as context.
3. **P1: Weak opening reward and familiar entry.** v1 P001–P007. **Fix:** one room and monthly equivalent per 100 SF, make the billion/personal wealth contradiction legible immediately; protect accuracy that $150 is effective area revenue, not quoted rent. **Evidence:** scenario arithmetic; no new empirical rate.
4. **P1: Middle reads as a sector survey.** v1 P012–P015. **Fix:** compress buy/build/manage and tie their economics to capital/ownership, single real-world growth scale check, and new supply risk. **Evidence:** opened S003/S004/S005.
5. **P1: Financial density and summarizing ending.** v1 P016–P022. **Fix:** isolate annual cash from asset equity, use fewer spoken precision figures, reserve stress and sponsor ownership as earned reveals; close on the single physical room's revised meaning. **Evidence:** no model arithmetic changes, full research remains.

**P2**: unnatural staging phrases ("Now comes the first powerful mechanism", "So here's the complete answer") and repeated billion-dollar transitions; remove them unless narratively needed.

**KEEP**: 65,000 square-foot unit economics, concrete valuation math, distinction between third-party management and owned assets, real issuer evidence, $1.053bn→$463m stress, and the fact that a founder may own only minority equity.
