## Revised audience promise

An aspiring entrepreneur has no property portfolio or construction budget. Start by improving an independently owned storage site for fees, not magically constructing an institutional-scale building. Overcome owner skepticism, enormous corporate competitors, thin management revenue, expensive debt and neighborhood oversupply. Earn a first local deal possibly structured as seller-retained equity + bank mortgage + outside investor, with no guaranteed no-cash sponsor ownership. Establish a neighborhood cluster, then larger institutional financing and a **conditional** 180-facility billion-dollar-equity calculation. End by asking what extra value the company created and the founder actually contracted to keep.

The story must portray a credible **sequence and decision gates**, not an executable investment guarantee or automatic successful startup.

## The two-stage worked model

**First modest site:** hypothetical 20,000 net rentable available SF; $14/year effective per available SF, inclusive of occupancy and concessions; $280k annual site revenue. 50% NOI margin **after property management costs** => $140k annual NOI. 5% management fee on revenue = $14k *gross operator revenue*, assumed to be INCLUDED in property expenses, not extra NOI. Assumed 8% cap => $1.75m property gross value. Deal possibility, contingent on lender/owner/investor approval: senior bank mortgage $875k (50%), seller retains $525k equity (30%), outside investor contributes $350k (20%). Sponsor manages for fee and possibly negotiates *after-hurdle* upside with no automatically granted 20%. At 7% hypothetical debt interest => $61,250 annual debt interest, $78,750 pre-principal/pre-reserve NOI remainder. Conditional $30k annual NOI improvement /8% => **$375k potential incremental gross value before cost**. Not a promise.

**Institutional end state:** only *after* verified results and repeated financing, assume 180 hypothetical larger properties averaging 65,000 NRSF at $18/year effective SF, 65% NOI margin, and hypothetical 6.5% cap. Each $760,500 annual NOI /0.065 => $11.7m gross property; 180 properties => $2.106bn gross property, assumed $1.053bn property debt and $1.053bn property equity. Investor equity roughly $1.053bn must be contributed at fair prices; founder wealth is not automatically created. Adverse scenario from prior v3: 10% lower revenue, 60% margin, 7.5% cap, debt unchanged => $463.32m residual equity. 20% sponsor equity, **if negotiated and still held**, ~$200m paper based on rounded $1bn, before other claims.

## Industry evidence and barriers

2026 Self-Storage Almanac reports **65,000+ US active facilities**, and using **2025 operator data** top five operated 19.3% of facilities, 35.6% of rentable SF. Historical McDonald's US 13,706 sites in 2025; Starbucks 2025 North American 18,311 **including Canada**: combined US counts necessarily below 32,017, materially below 65,000. **Never** say more storage than all fast-food franchises. National facility count does **not** imply unsaturated local storage markets or eager retirement sellers.

Public Storage **completed** acquisition of National Storage Affiliates July 22, 2026, evidence of powerful consolidators; Public Storage is a listed REIT, not automatically a PE buyer. Extra Space 2025 had 1,856 third-party-managed properties and itself markets the ManagementPlus service, proving entry route exists but also incumbent competition. Q2 2026 TractIQ/REIT data describe **uneven** rate/supply recovery, not blanket demand boom.

**Potential differentiation:** trusted operator for specific owners and neighborhoods, scoped management trials, clear measurement of improved *net* rent, owner succession/JV offers, cluster density and local retention. Not promised discounts from elderly owners, not assured NOI lift, and not a claim that a startup has better revenue-management technology than Extra Space.

**Hard stops:** owner/customer privacy, inability to get first contract, $14k annual management revenue per example property, bank guarantee/covenant demands, owner seller-rollover refusal, property inspections, interest coverage, local competing construction, red flags in rent roll, insurance/capex/revenue concessions, poor local demographics, capital partner terms, down-cycle refinancing, founder dilution. Skipping a bad site is a positive skill, not a story failure.
