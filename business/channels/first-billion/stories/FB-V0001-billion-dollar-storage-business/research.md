# Research: Building a billion-dollar storage business

**Episode** FB-I0001 / FB-V0001 | **Format** worked business model | **Research as of** 2026-10-11 UTC | **Currency** nominal US dollars | **Geography** hypothetical United States portfolio.

## The central question and a falsifiable view

Could an asset-backed storage company reach at least **$1bn in modeled equity value** through repeatable storage economics? Hypothesis: yes under plausible but demanding combinations of revenue per rentable square foot, property operating margin, portfolio size, valuation cap rate and loan-to-value. This is an **asset-equity value** exercise, not founder net worth, securities advice, a 2026 property appraisal, or a real company's plan.

The critical mechanism is:
property revenue − property operating expense = property NOI; NOI ÷ **assumed** cap rate = modeled gross property value; modeled gross property value − outstanding modeled property debt = **modeled property equity**. This omits corporate-level valuation adjustments, holdco preferred equity, hedges, transaction costs, taxes and contingencies. The final ownership percentage determines the founder's potential paper share.

| Input or derived metric | Per representative mature site | 180 sites | Basis |

| Gross stabilized property value | $11,700,000 | $2,106,000,000 | NOI ÷ .065 |
| Debt balance | $5,850,000 | $1,053,000,000 | **Assumed** 50% loan to modeled property value |

**Checks:** $136.89m/.065 = $2.106bn; $2.106bn − $1.053bn = $1.053bn. Derived appraised worth is **$180 per available NRSF**, versus S004's **~$155/NRSF actual 2025 blended purchase outlay**. This is not an assertion that a new entrepreneur could acquire or build representative stabilized properties for $180/NRSF or at a 6.5% yield. If acquired at modeled fair value, buying the properties alone creates no immediate economic gain; investors have exchanged capital for property exposure.

Interest rate, HQ overhead and reserve **are assumptions**, not documented financing offers or capex budgets. Actual debt interest and cap rates may move together; all-cash investors may target different economics.

## Growth pathway, funding feasibility and failed attempts

**Starting resources:** model assumes access to one stabilized store and ~$5.85m site equity plus ~$5.85m financing, before costs. This is an institutional-scale initial ask, **not an accessible path from zero capital**. A sponsor could syndicate third-party equity, but retains negotiated economics rather than automatic 100% ownership.

**Repeatable unit:** lease units, collect rent, keep facility maintained and secure, manage move-ins/out, operating expenses and local advertising. Not every available square foot earns identical rent; model compresses occupancy, rates, discounts and other property income into an effective revenue/NRSF measure.

**Expansion:** (i) acquisitions of stabilized facilities require large equity checks and debt underwriting; (ii) development requires entitlement/construction/lease-up and may require years to stabilize; (iii) fee management can gain scale with less capital but confers fees, not real-estate equity. Sequencing 180 properties is a hypothetical end-state, **not a proven transaction pipeline**. A realistic portfolio would mix sizes, geographies and vintages, and face integration, pricing and concentration risks.

**The full funding ledger:** ~$1.053bn assumed cumulative property debt and ~$1.053bn residual sponsor+investor property equity at the modeled valuation. This is a mark-to-model **capital structure**, not a source-and-uses proof for acquiring 180 buildings or a fundraise plan. If acquisition costs differ from valuation, capitalization, retained cash, dilution and preferred investor rights must be modeled from actual deals. Carry fees, closing costs, cash drag and debt amortization are excluded. No promise the same leverage is obtainable at every property or that the lender finances 50% of appraised value without covenants.

**Ownership bridge:** If the sponsor holds a hypothetical 20% of $1.053bn common-equity value, the rough gross paper stake is ~$210.6m before debt not already embedded, company-level costs, investor preferences, taxes, lockups and fees. For listener simplicity the script uses a **round $1bn equity × 20% = $200m illustration**; neither number is a personal cash balance. A sponsor may earn fees/promotes, but preferred return/waterfalls cannot be inferred in absence of legal documents.

**What can fail:** wrong neighborhood or overbuilding, rate cuts, occupancy weakness, high tax/insurance/capex costs, development budget overruns, debt maturities, rising refinance costs, acquisition price too high, uneven small-market exit values, inability to source enough repeatable sites, and sponsor equity dilution. The documented Public Storage and Extra Space commentary rebuts a frictionless infinite-replication story.

**Strongest competing explanation / limits:** large incumbent margins partly reflect acquired real estate bases, branding, low-cost capital, pricing data, vendor scale and historical entry timing. Buying 180 profitable assets with investor money at fair prices *assembles* a billion-dollar-equity vehicle rather than proving a founder invented value out of nothing. Faster expansion can dilute and destroy returns. True "billionaire founder" outcomes require controlling a valuable ownership fraction, liquidity and net claim verification. The scenario cannot predict how many years the target would take, whether such capital would be raised or whether 180 comparable sites would be available at fair prices.

## Editorial result, not investment conclusion

The episode is strongest as a guided reveal: a mundane property produces NOI; capitalization prices the income; leverage subtracts claims; scale demands billions of funding; adverse economics slash residual equity; contractual ownership determines who benefits. It should not suggest six-and-a-half percent is today's market cap rate, that our hypothetical balance sheet is a lender term sheet, or that storage magically makes billionaires.
