# First Billion script: 8–10 minutes

Research and synthesize before writing the full first draft in one pass. Revise internally and follow the shared editorial and script-review workflow. Do not request approval for each paragraph.

## Narrative progression

| Approximate movement | Story job |
| --- | --- |
| Opening | Recognizable starting point, surprising scale, honest question and a clearly defined outcome |
| Early payoff | Show the first viable economic unit and why it matters |
| Expansion | Trace the actual or modeled steps that made more units possible; introduce cost and cash constraints |
| Capital and ownership | Explain who funded growth, what they received and who retained value |
| Complication | Test the apparent recipe against risk, unsuccessful attempts, counterevidence or sensitivity |
| Resolution | Bridge to the defined milestone and deliver one earned insight about what built the outcome |
| Closing | One brief relevant CTA after the answer |

These are story jobs, not fixed timestamps or obligatory chronological sections. Total actual video must be 480–600 seconds including pauses and closing. Do not pad a thin model or speed-read too many financial relationships.

## Hook and discovery

Prepare two or three honest hooks. Establish what the billion means and whether the path is historical or illustrative in the opening movement. Do not title a model as though someone actually achieved it. Deliver an early useful discovery; let it raise a deeper question about scaling or ownership. Do not withhold all substance until the milestone.

Map each beat's changed understanding and why the next follows. A larger number alone is not progress. Build tension through decisions, constraints and trade-offs rather than repeated genius claims. The strongest insight should deepen the obvious explanation, not supply a generic lesson about hard work.

## Step-by-step without a lecture

Follow a concrete transaction or viable unit before introducing labels. Explain the customer, revenue, cost and cash consequence in manageable passages. Show how the next step changes the economics; never multiply one good unit infinitely without testing demand, financing and execution.

Retain one clear financial relationship at a time in narration. Keep full arithmetic, exact figures and sensitivity tables in research; speak the comparison that advances understanding. Clearly label fictional teaching numbers and derived results. Ground human stakes in evidence.

## Intellectual checks

Trace starting resources, successful and failed steps, funding, stakes and milestone basis. Test the strongest alternate explanation. Distinguish founder ownership value from company revenue/valuation and gross proceeds from cash retained. Do not invent first-billion dates or declare one route universally best.

Worked-model episodes must expose assumptions and a meaningful downside case. Historical episodes must not sound like guaranteed how-to instructions. Resolve the opening with a specific, defensible insight and limits relevant to this case.
