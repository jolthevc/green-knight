# First Billion: creative contract
The entertainment is **the audacious attempt to build something enormous from a believable beginning**. It is a participatory business-building challenge, not a course promising anyone can become rich.

**Universal hook:** A recognizable business and a seemingly impossible scale target. Begin with the smallest plausible door, not a gigantic financed asset. State whether the story is actual history or a clearly hypothetical model.

**Dramatic motor:** First gatekeeper -> first tiny win -> the economics are less attractive than expected -> specific obstacle -> credible experiment/mitigant with cost -> bigger opportunity -> capital/ownership reckoning. Give the viewer decisions that really change the plan, rather than an uninterrupted finance spreadsheet. Historical episodes follow real evidence, not retrofitted bootstrap fiction.

**Signature payoff:** How each stage genuinely unlocked the next, what could break, and who actually ends up owning the value. The conclusion should make ambition feel both possible to imagine and hard-earned.

**Creative red flags:** financial advice, false attainability, repeated hypothetical qualifiers overwhelming tension, first unit teleported into a huge portfolio, 'billion revenue' presented as personal wealth, obstacles solved by naming software or AI.

**Distinct from others:** End-to-end wealth/business-building mechanism, not one winning decision (Money Moves), a discrete purchase (Changing Hands), or general unit economics (Pretty Penny).

**Pilot test:** Could a viewer actually describe the very first offer and why the customer/owner would say yes? Does every major financing leap require earned proof? Is the route captivating to someone who has no intention of becoming an entrepreneur?

**Strong reference:** Storage pilot has rare financial honesty; test a decision-driven narrative that keeps the capital bridge while reducing narrated assumption stacking.

## Current director-led creative production rule (all eight channels)

Green Knight now owns the title/thumbnail promise, first frame, detailed first 20–35 seconds, narration dynamics, story movements and breathing points, visual sequence design with recurring environments and transformations, selective evidence/text, and ending callback. The editor executes the work, rather than independently choosing the story's visual conception. Read business/EDITOR_HANDOFF_STANDARD.md and business/DIRECTOR_PACKET_REVIEW.md; these supersede any earlier channel-specific editor-owned guidance.

Per story, release only the three fully reviewed final files in `editor-packet/`: `01_SCRIPT.txt`, `02_NARRATION_DIRECTOR.md`, `03_VISUAL_DIRECTOR.md`. Script, performance, visual plan and their integration MUST all pass independent and joined QA after all revisions. No formerly scripted episode automatically qualifies for packet approval. Preserve this channel's distinctive audience promise and visual identity; do not force identical transitions or formulaic “Chapter 1” cards.
