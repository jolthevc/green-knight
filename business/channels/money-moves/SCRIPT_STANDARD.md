# Strategic decision story: 8–10 minutes

Research and synthesize first. Write the complete spoken draft in one pass, then review and revise internally. The owner selects the concept, not each paragraph.

## Narrative architecture

| Approximate window | Story job |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:25 | Recognizable stakes and a surprising choice; clear viewer promise |
| 0:25–1:30 | Establish the constraint and deliver the first useful insight |
| 1:30–3:00 | Explain the real alternatives and their trade-offs |
| 3:00–5:00 | Show the move, execution and causal mechanism |
| 5:00–7:00 | Add resistance, costs, a rival response or counterevidence |
| 7:00–9:30 | Explain the outcome and the limits of the lesson |
| Final 5–20 seconds | Brief relevant next-video invitation when available |

These are planning windows, not mandatory section lengths. Total finished runtime is 8–10 minutes. A complex pivot can need more than one decision beat; choose a clear episode boundary.

## Hook and progression

Open on a choice a viewer can understand. Why refuse a seemingly attractive option? Why move work to the customer? Why sacrifice one advantage to gain another? These are prompts, not claims about a selected company.

Prepare two or three supported hook alternatives internally, choose one and commit to it. Align the first 30 seconds with the title/thumbnail. Avoid greetings, early subscription requests and generic biographies.

Reveal the useful strategic tension early. Keep curiosity alive through changing constraints, trade-offs and new evidence. Do not hide the entire decision until the closing, or repeat that it was a “genius move” before explaining it.

Every beat advances the model: what was possible, why they chose, what changed and what complicated the outcome. About every 30–60 seconds check whether something meaningful has progressed; this is our editorial diagnostic, not a platform rule or arbitrary cut rate.

## Evidence and causality

Reconstruct what was knowable at the time. Distinguish contemporary evidence from retrospective interpretation. Real alternatives need sources. Explain the mechanism connecting the decision to the outcome, and address credible alternative causes.

Avoid reducing years of execution to one leader's brilliance or one mistake. Use measurable outcomes with baseline, date and scope. Specify whether a figure is revenue, profit, market share, usage or valuation.

Counterfactuals are labeled analysis. Do not present an invented boardroom conversation, exact private thought or imaginary offer as fact. Never describe a tactic as guaranteed to work.

## Spoken writing and visuals

Short, concrete speech with varied rhythm. Explain terms through the situation rather than jargon. Use a recognizable product or behavior as the entry point. Avoid a textbook list of frameworks.

Plan visuals that show the actual choice: two paths, an obstacle, a resource or before/after structure. The decision board is a device, not a requirement to turn narration into bullet points.

script.txt starts with the **non-spoken** `TITLE: <metadata.title>` and a blank line; the remaining body contains spoken words only. Keep citations, other headings, timestamps, cue tags and visual instructions in sidecars. Paragraph anchors follow body wording only; omit title when recording.

## Payoff

Resolve the opening question. Give one specific insight and its limits. Explain the trade-off that made the move work in this context, rather than giving generic entrepreneurship advice.

End with one main CTA after value, usually an adjacent published episode. If none exists, use a short channel invitation. No phantom end-screen link or stack of engagement requests.

## Internal review

Check title promise; supported alternatives; contemporary knowledge; mechanism; execution; counterevidence; causal modesty; repetition; cue anchors; estimate and production feasibility.

A complete scripted package includes the shared story artifacts plus the decision timeline, alternatives and counterfactual limits in research/synthesis. The actual final narration/video must be measured before runtime approval.

## Broad-audience discovery

Apply [ENGAGEMENT_STANDARD.md](../../ENGAGEMENT_STANDARD.md). This channel's story engine is a consequential choice. Make the real constraint and alternatives intelligible before the decision. A brief “which option would you take?” can create participation where evidence supports the choices. Let execution and trade-offs challenge the initial judgment; never invent a boardroom scene.

Use the newcomer and informed-skeptic lenses. Record each beat's changed understanding and why the next follows. Choose an earned first payoff and a specific final insight. Aim for substantial learning delivered through the story, with warmth and room to follow, rather than a list of business frameworks.
