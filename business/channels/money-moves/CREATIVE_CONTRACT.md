# Money Moves: creative contract
The entertainment is **standing beside a consequential decision when the right choice is not obvious**. The channel should feel like a high-stakes strategic drama, not a business school case memo.

**Universal hook:** One recognizable product or company, a fork in the road, and a costly choice. Name what the company risked losing and the alternative viewers would initially choose.

**Dramatic motor:** Constraint -> genuine alternatives -> decision -> execution -> response from customers/rivals -> unexpected trade-off. Show changing incentives and consequences; do not pretend a single CEO thought invented an organizational transformation.

**Signature payoff:** Viewers understand *why* the move made sense in its actual conditions and why copying it may fail. A confident insight with real caveats.

**Creative red flags:** CEO worship, retrospective 'genius' certainty, lists of strategies, unverified boardroom dramatization, three minutes of founding history before an actual choice.

**Distinct from others:** The decision and alternatives are central, not the ongoing system behind a price (Pretty Penny), an entire collapse (Fallen Angels) or the financial execution of a deal (Changing Hands).

**Pilot test:** Pause 90 seconds in. Can the viewer name the options and what each costs? At the end, can they explain what changed because of the decision? Does the script earn emotional uncertainty before showing the outcome?

**Opening variants:** An apparently irrational choice; a threat with two unattractive responses; two rivals choosing opposite strategies. Use only documented choices.

## Required popcorn-good channel contract (2026-10-11)
Read business/POPCORN_STANDARD.md. **Earned engagement is the first creative test, with truthful evidence as the minimum floor.** A first-timer experiences a high-stakes *choice* among real alternatives, under constraints. An expert encounters evidence of what decision makers knew at the time, why the rejected option was plausible, a misunderstood incentive or a causal mechanism behind the result. **BAD:** a company success timeline with 'leadership was visionary.' **GOOD:** a specific decision whose downside, opportunity cost and later strategic second-order consequences emerge as successive reversals.

Run TWO explicit tests on every new channel concept, idea and scripted episode: **newcomer pull** (immediate human stakes, first-listen comprehension and real desire to continue) and **already-expert reward** (a specific warranted new lens or insightful causal model). Name the weakest likely middle sequence, create authentic reversals, and show how the best strategic insight *unfolds inside the plot*. A correct but predictable lecture, a generic takeaway or beautiful prose without audience momentum is REVISE/REFRAME, never 'ready.' Never invent twists or inflate unsourced certainty. On review/revise, record both verdicts and actual paragraph/beat fixes rather than praising general quality.
