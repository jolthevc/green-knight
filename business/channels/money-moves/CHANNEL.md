# Editorial lane and research rules

Pretty Penny asks “How does the money work?”
Money Moves asks “Why did they make that choice, and how did it change the business?”

The protagonist is a decision under constraints. The viewer should see the options, understand the trade-off and recognize why the result was not inevitable.

## Broad audience

Use companies, products and habits people recognize across retail, entertainment, travel, food, technology and consumer services. Start with an experience, product or consequential competitive tension. Explain strategic terminology only when needed. Avoid narrow executive training and generic lists of business lessons.

## Expansive story engines

| Series | Central question |
| --- | --- |
| The Pivot | Why abandon the old approach and bet on another? |
| The Advantage | Which deliberate choice created an advantage rivals struggled to copy? |
| The Price | Why price, package or distribute the product that way? |
| Rivals | Which competing decisions changed the contest? |
| The Constraint | How did a limitation become an advantage, and what did it cost? |
| The Next Move | Which expansion or reinvention changed the business? |

One episode centers a consequential decision or a tightly connected sequence. Do not force a long organizational transformation into a fictional single boardroom moment. Rivalries need a defined contest and turning point, rather than a century-long summary.

## Channel boundaries

| Dominant viewer promise | Route |
| --- | --- |
| Follow the customer dollar through a system | Pretty Penny |
| Understand a strategic choice and its consequences | Money Moves |
| Reconstruct a collapse or lost advantage | Fallen Angels |
| Understand a trade, acquisition or deal's execution and stakes | Changing Hands |
| Explain a deception mechanism and its exposure | Fool’s Gold |
| Explain inherited wealth, family ownership or dynasty control | Silver Spoon |

An acquisition may support Money Moves when the strategic rationale is central; a transaction-led story belongs in Changing Hands. Failed decisions can feature here when the choice and alternatives are central. A collapse autopsy belongs in Fallen Angels.

Example distinction: IKEA's cost/revenue system is Pretty Penny; IKEA's choice to move assembly to the customer is Money Moves. This is a framing example, not a verified claim of one isolated founding decision.

## Research beyond hindsight

After the shared research packet, explicitly answer:
1. What constraint or threat did decision-makers face?
2. What options were actually available at that time?
3. What did they know then, and what only became clear later?
4. What did they choose, when, and through which observable actions?
5. How could the choice produce the claimed outcome?
6. What did it cost, who resisted, and what nearly undermined it?
7. What alternative explanations account for success or failure?
8. What evidence would weaken our preferred interpretation?

Use contemporary filings, earnings calls, announcements, court/regulatory records where relevant, credible interviews and reporting. Later memoirs and company retrospectives are useful but can rationalize history; identify their perspective. Source disagreements stay visible.

Maintain a decision timeline, evidence of alternatives and an outcome baseline with dates. Separate an announced plan from actual execution, growth from profitability, and correlation from causal attribution. Do not invent deliberations, private motives, dialogue or missed offers.

Label counterfactuals as reasoning, not events. “Could have” needs evidence of a viable alternative, not merely an option imaginable today. Show execution, timing, resources and luck alongside the decision. Credit or blame may be distributed across an organization.

## Editorial view

The payoff is a specific insight into the trade-off, not “be innovative,” “take risks” or “listen to customers.” Explain when the move works, what its costs are and why copying it might fail under different conditions. Claims of a moat, monopoly or unassailable advantage require evidence and qualification.
