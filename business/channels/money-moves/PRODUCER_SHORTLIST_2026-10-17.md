# Money Moves: selected ten-idea Fiverr producer brief (2026-10-17)

**All ten are existing proposed MM-I ideas; no topic is newly selected, commissioned or completed.** The objective: entertaining business documentaries about a *consequential strategic decision*, its alternatives, tradeoffs, outcomes and deeper verified economic mechanism. Target 8–10 minutes and a Modern MBA / Wendover / WSJ hybrid of genuine documentary footage and purposeful financial diagrams. No generic industry explainer or stock montage.

## Producer workflow

1. Separate paid ~60-second integrated sample, owner-supplied script; owner approves voice before recording.
2. One full pilot topic agreed; producer researches and delivers **the complete first-draft script**, source leads and proposed visual concept.
3. Owner reviews/revises/expressly approves **the complete final script and narration voice before editing, recording or full video production**.
4. Producer makes the full pilot with cleared assets, animation, narration, sound, edit, title and thumbnail under separately agreed terms; owner reviews the completed film.
5. A ten-topic slate **does not** mean ten videos have been commissioned. Any batch and terms require separate agreement.

**Source status:** The hypotheses and source leads below need research verification; do not treat them as established facts, invent dialogue or attribute unsupported motives.

## Ten full pitches

### 01. Domino’s Told America Its Pizza Was Bad (`MM-I0012`)
**WHY THIS GETS A CLICK:** A company publicly attacks its own product.
**CORE QUESTION:** Why publicly criticize the product you already sell?
**STORY ANGLE:** Domino’s launched a revised core pizza and marketed the change directly. Test how product improvement, honesty, pricing and digital ordering each contributed to later performance.
**POSSIBLE OPEN:** A national pizza chain went on television to tell customers why they disliked its pizza.
**STORY PATH:** Customer criticism; recipe and campaign; separating brand attention from product and tech gains.
**SURPRISING PAYOFF TO TEST:** An admission can be a competitive strategy if something actually changes afterward.
**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Use documented recipe-change advertising, pizza footage and a side-by-side timeline of product reformulation, brand response and digital ordering. Distinguish concurrent changes rather than claiming one ad caused the turnaround.
**RESEARCH START:** Domino’s original recipe-change release  |  Open original-source lead
### 02. Adobe Stopped Selling Photoshop. Customers Kept Paying. (`MM-I0010`)
**WHY THIS GETS A CLICK:** Customers object as a software business rewrites how it gets paid.
**CORE QUESTION:** Why trade big one-time purchases for smaller recurring payments?
**STORY ANGLE:** Adobe shifted innovation and licensing from perpetual Creative Suite versions to subscriptions. Explore adoption friction, recurring revenue, distribution and customers who resisted.
**POSSIBLE OPEN:** Imagine owning your favorite creative software. Then the next version is no longer sold that way.
**STORY PATH:** Perpetual purchase model; 2013 shift and outrage; revenue timing and strategic trade-offs.
**SURPRISING PAYOFF TO TEST:** A subscription can be financially powerful while making ownership feel worse.
**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Move from boxed Creative Suite purchases to an animated recurring-payment timeline; overlay documented company revenue-mix changes, customer trade-offs and transition costs.
**RESEARCH START:** Adobe fiscal 2013 Form 10-K  |  Open original-source lead
### 03. Amazon’s Bet on Unlimited Shipping (`MM-I0008`)
**WHY THIS GETS A CLICK:** Making shipping feel free changes the reason to place an order.
**CORE QUESTION:** Why would an online store make shipping feel free?
**STORY ANGLE:** Amazon accepted a costly member benefit to remove the mental friction of placing small orders. Test whether order frequency and retention justified the investment, rather than claiming causality without data.
**POSSIBLE OPEN:** Open with an order worth less than the delivery charge. Amazon responds by making delivery feel unlimited.
**STORY PATH:** Membership fee vs shipping subsidy; changes in order behavior; rivals and long-run logistics burden.
**SURPRISING PAYOFF TO TEST:** Why Prime economics and basket frequency are not the same as membership profit.
**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Open with an inexpensive online order, then animate the Prime membership fee, shipping obligation, shopping frequency and logistics investment. Do not equate a member’s orders with Prime profitability.
**RESEARCH START:** Amazon 2005 Prime announcement  |  Open original-source lead
### 04. Disney Started Charging for a Faster Line (`MM-I0028`)
**WHY THIS GETS A CLICK:** Two families enter the same park, but one can pay to wait less.
**CORE QUESTION:** Why sell priority access to a park ride that guests already paid to enter?
**STORY ANGLE:** A major theme-park product-pricing redesign made some queue privileges paid. Explain capacity, guest satisfaction and yield without asserting an unverified single profit motive.
**POSSIBLE OPEN:** Two families buy park tickets. One pays extra to spend less time in line.
**STORY PATH:** Legacy FastPass experience; launch of paid options; fairness, capacity and guest trade-offs.
**SURPRISING PAYOFF TO TEST:** Priority has a price when scarce capacity meets impatient demand.
**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Animate ride capacity and two queue paths over authentic park footage, then show the guest-experience and pricing trade-offs without reducing the decision to a single unproven motive.
**RESEARCH START:** Disney Parks official Genie launch materials and park pricing archives  |  Primary documentation not yet independently checked
### 05. Apple Fired Intel From the Mac (`MM-I0013`)
**WHY THIS GETS A CLICK:** An established computer platform risks breaking compatibility to control its own chips.
**CORE QUESTION:** Why risk breaking software compatibility to build your own chips?
**STORY ANGLE:** Apple chose vertical hardware-software integration. Explore performance-per-watt, transition costs, developer cooperation and the value of controlling the roadmap.
**POSSIBLE OPEN:** An Apple laptop contains an Intel chip. Apple decides the next one should not.
**STORY PATH:** Intel dependency; chip-development investment; product improvements versus transition frictions.
**SURPRISING PAYOFF TO TEST:** Supply control can become product design control, but migration is expensive.
**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Bridge archived Intel-Mac launches to the 2020 Apple silicon transition; visualize hardware/software layers, performance-per-watt questions and migration costs. Ground comparisons in dated evidence.
**RESEARCH START:** Apple 2020 silicon announcement  |  Open original-source lead
### 06. Target Turned Its Stores Into Online Warehouses (`MM-I0015`)
**WHY THIS GETS A CLICK:** A retailer turns physical stores into a possible online-delivery advantage.
**CORE QUESTION:** Why fulfill online orders from stores instead of only new warehouses?
**STORY ANGLE:** Target used existing stores for pickup and fulfillment. Investigate inventory accuracy, last-mile expense, staffing and where in-store convenience beats pure e-commerce.
**POSSIBLE OPEN:** The easiest way to beat delivery was to make a parking spot your checkout lane.
**STORY PATH:** Online competition; Drive Up pilot; store labor, margins and scale limits.
**SURPRISING PAYOFF TO TEST:** A store can be an expensive liability or a nearby fulfillment advantage.
**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Map an online order from the nearest store instead of a remote warehouse; animate pick/pack labor, distance, inventory availability and the real trade-offs in store-led fulfillment.
**RESEARCH START:** Target 2017 Drive Up announcement  |  Open original-source lead
### 07. Ryanair Bet on Airports No One Wanted (`MM-I0026`)
**WHY THIS GETS A CLICK:** A cheaper flight might land farther from the place you want to go.
**CORE QUESTION:** Why fly passengers to less convenient airports?
**STORY ANGLE:** Low airport fees and simplified turnaround could support low fares, balanced against ground transport time and passenger experience. Verify route-specific economics.
**POSSIBLE OPEN:** The cheapest flight to a famous city sometimes lands nowhere near its center. That is the point.
**STORY PATH:** Airport bargaining; turnaround and ticket economics; customer trade-offs.
**SURPRISING PAYOFF TO TEST:** Distance from the destination can become a cost advantage for the airline.
**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Show real routes on animated maps with primary/secondary airport locations, airport charges, turnaround diagrams and the traveler’s ground-transport trade-off. Validate the airport examples.
**RESEARCH START:** Ryanair annual reports, airport agreements and official route histories  |  Primary documentation not yet independently checked
### 08. Coca-Cola Changed Its Secret Formula. Then Reversed Course. (`MM-I0036`)
**WHY THIS GETS A CLICK:** A beloved drink changes its taste, and the company has to confront the backlash.
**CORE QUESTION:** Why replace an iconic product even when customers identify with it?
**STORY ANGLE:** Taste tests, competitive pressure and the emotional value of a familiar product collided. Verify consumer-test claims and the real timing and role of classic Coke.
**POSSIBLE OPEN:** A drink loved for generations suddenly tastes different. The company did it on purpose.
**STORY PATH:** Cola rivalry; formula change and reactions; reintroduction and brand effects.
**SURPRISING PAYOFF TO TEST:** What consumers prefer in a blind sip can differ from what they want to buy.
**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Use properly sourced 1985 advertising, packaging and contemporary reaction; contrast taste-test design, product decisions and the Classic Coke return on a dated timeline.
**RESEARCH START:** Coca-Cola historical New Coke archive and contemporary 1985 reporting  |  Primary documentation not yet independently checked
### 09. YouTube Decided Creators Should Get Paid (`MM-I0018`)
**WHY THIS GETS A CLICK:** A video platform decides to pay the people uploading content for free.
**CORE QUESTION:** Why share ad revenue with users uploading free videos?
**STORY ANGLE:** The partner program changed incentives for creators and the platform. Test whether payments increased supply and quality, avoiding numerical attribution without records.
**POSSIBLE OPEN:** In 2007, YouTube made a strange offer: upload your own show, and the platform might pay you.
**STORY PATH:** Early video sites; initial partner rollout; creator investment and advertiser trust.
**SURPRISING PAYOFF TO TEST:** Distribution wins may depend on paying the people who create the content.
**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Start with an early uploader, then animate creator–platform–advertiser payments, the Partner Program’s actual rollout and how creator incentives could change the content ecosystem.
**RESEARCH START:** YouTube 2007 Partner Program announcement  |  Open original-source lead
### 10. T-Mobile Stopped Making Customers Sign Contracts (`MM-I0034`)
**WHY THIS GETS A CLICK:** A phone carrier challenges the lock-in that its competitors relied upon.
**CORE QUESTION:** Why remove the service contract that kept wireless customers captive?
**STORY ANGLE:** T-Mobile challenged contract, device-financing and service-plan structures. Compare acquisition benefits with churn, network and handset financing risks.
**POSSIBLE OPEN:** Your phone carrier wants to lock you in for years. One rival says: what if we stop?
**STORY PATH:** Carrier lock-in norms; Un-carrier mechanics; competitors and real pricing trade-offs.
**SURPRISING PAYOFF TO TEST:** Removing a lock-in can create an advantage if the new economics still work.
**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Show a customer’s phone bill and the trade between long service contracts and device financing; use dated Un-carrier ads, churn/customer metrics and competitor responses with correct context.
**RESEARCH START:** T-Mobile 2013 Un-carrier press releases and annual reports  |  Primary documentation not yet independently checked
