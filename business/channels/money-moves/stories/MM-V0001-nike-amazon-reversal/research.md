## The timeline and contemporary information
2017, limited Amazon Retail pilot announced by then CEO Mark Parker with brand presentation/product storytelling rationale (S001). 2019, Nike publicly ended the pilot, emphasizing more direct, personal consumer connections, while continuing to work with other partners (S002). Independent sellers still listed Nike goods on Amazon, so Nike was not absent from the marketplace (S006). June 2020, Consumer Direct Acceleration emphasized digital, Nike stores and selected partners (S003). 2022, Nike disclosed NIKE Direct at about 42% of Nike Brand revenue and fewer wholesale accounts globally (S004). October 2024, Elliott Hill became CEO; Nike retrospectively says he rebuilt important wholesaler relationships and values their product feedback (S010). May 2025, Nike/Amazon public statements confirmed resumed US direct sourcing, broader partner distribution and run-off time for some overlapping independent sellers (S006, S007).

## Decision tree without invented boardroom motives
Option A: continue limited Amazon Retail pilot and accept constraints around price, listings and platform control. Option B: negotiate or limit assortment and presentation, plausible but no observed private negotiating record. Option C: end direct Amazon supply and emphasize Nike-owned properties and selected partners, which happened. Option D: reenter Amazon Retail as part of an integrated marketplace, announced in 2025. Each decision changes retailer reach and customer presentation. Do not ascribe a precise internal vote or claim every option was formally considered.

## Full economic mechanism
Owned retail can capture consumer ticket revenue and customer relationship but must fund retail labor/stores, app/customer acquisition, fulfillment, returns and inventory risks. Wholesale sells product to retailers, generating lower sell-in revenue per item but shifting some retail work and furnishing discovery, comparison, retail staff service and feedback. Amazon third-party marketplace merchants can offer Nike merchandise even without direct brand-to-Amazon sourcing. Therefore leaving a supply arrangement cannot make shoppers stop starting their search on Amazon. This is a strategic inference, not a measured Nike lost-sales figure. Revenue share is not evidence of per-pair operating profit or margin.

## Financial cross-check, dates and units
- Fiscal 2022: Nike Direct about 42% of **Nike Brand** revenue (S004).
- FY2025 versus FY2024: **Nike Inc.** total sales $46.309 billion vs $51.362 billion. **Nike Direct** $18.783B vs $21.519B, decline 13% reported. Nike Brand Digital ~$9.6B vs $12.1B, decline 20% (S005).
- FY2026 versus FY2025: **Nike Brand wholesale** $27.453B vs $25.883B (+6% reported); **Nike Direct** $17.720B vs $18.783B (-6% reported). Total Nike Inc $46.398B vs $46.309B (S008).
- Fiscal 2027 Q1 ended August 31, 2026: worldwide Nike Brand wholesale $6.804B vs $6.839B (-1% reported); Nike Direct $4.142B vs $4.514B (-8% reported); North America Nike wholesale $2.981B vs $2.736B (+9% reported). Scope, currencies and period differ from annual data (S009).

## Competing explanations and causality limits
Nike's revenue changes also reflect product cycles, competition including brands in running, Nike sportswear inventories and promotions, changing fashion trends, pandemic e-commerce pull-forward, region-specific weakness including Greater China and leadership/execution. Not every missed sale can be assigned to lost shelf space. No Amazon-specific sales/profit disclosures, controlled customer incrementality study, hypothetical 'Nike never left' counterfactual or honest way to quantify dollars lost due solely to 2019 Amazon pilot exit. Nike management itself described the 2025 Amazon restart as not materially changing near-term group sales (S011; confirmed in Nike's official 2025 Q4 earnings transcript, page 23). FY27 Q1 shows global wholesale still contracting even as North America improved. A credible episode must portray the unresolved commercial result.
