Owner selected idea on 2026-10-12. Working selected title: **Nike Left Amazon. Why Did It Come Back?**. Runtime target 8-10 min, exact recorded timing pending.

**Newcomer question:** If Nike quit Amazon, why could consumers still find its shoes there? Answer arrives at P002: Nike ended a direct Amazon Retail supply arrangement, but third-party vendors kept selling Nike goods.

**Expert-level discovery:** The wholesale and marketplace trade-off is not merely giving away retailer margin versus owning the ticket price. Retailers and marketplaces can originate discovery, enable comparison and supply feedback, while direct channels bear fulfillment, acquisition and return costs. No source isolates the incremental Nike demand destroyed by closing a specific account.

**Decision:** Nike tried a limited direct Amazon pilot in 2017, ended it in 2019, accelerated a broader selective/direct-first approach, then resumed Amazon direct sourcing in 2025 alongside reengagement with other wholesale partners. Available alternatives included continuing, modifying or limiting the pilot; formal rejected negotiation terms are unknown.

**Fit and dedup:** This is Money Moves, explaining a meaningful choice, option costs and later reversal. It is not a Pretty Penny channel-pricing explainer or a Fallen Angels postmortem. Global 50-idea registry was checked; no matching Nike/Amazon channel-choice promise. New episode ID MM-V0001.

**Promise / return image:** A shoe stays in the same search results when a supply line vanishes. The final question is who creates the next buyer, not simply who collects a sale. Never claim Nike products disappeared or the Amazon exit caused global losses.
