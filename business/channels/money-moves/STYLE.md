# Black-and-white strategy illustration

Simple black-and-white stick characters, clear objects and selective animation. Adult, approachable and lightly witty. The story should feel like a smart explanation drawn in front of you.

Core direction: black-and-white stick-character illustration.
Pilot recommendations: the following exact palette, fonts and reusable layouts.

## Palette

| Color | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warm white | #FAFAF7 | Main canvas |
| Ink | #1C1C1C | Characters, primary lines and text |
| Mid gray | #737373 | Secondary arrows and supporting details |
| Light gray | #D8D8D2 | Panels and background objects |

Keep important text dark on the warm-white canvas. Gray fills can distinguish options, but use labels, line styles and positions too. Avoid low-contrast gray small text. Real evidence inserts may retain their original colors; our illustrated scenes remain monochrome.

## Typography

Inter Medium: labels and short captions.
Inter Bold: title cards, decision labels and thumbnails.
IBM Plex Mono Medium: optional figures, dates and compact evidence labels.

These are recommendations to test in the editor's tools. Confirm availability and licensing. Use one principal sans serif, consistent sizes and generous whitespace. Avoid an unrelated decorative font in every episode.

## Characters and objects

Round heads, simple expressive eyes/brows, slim limbs and stable proportions. Use stance, gesture and a few minimal accessories to signal roles. Adult humor comes from the situation, not exaggerated childish faces.

Represent an organization with a labeled building, product or consistent character role. A stick figure is a symbolic stand-in, not an authentic likeness. Do not fabricate real people's words, expressions or private actions.

Keep line weight, camera viewpoint, scale and text treatment consistent. Props should fit the topic; no permanent chessboard or suited executive in every scene.

## Signature device: the decision board

A reusable board can show the constraint, two or three real options, the chosen path and the consequence. Build it through the story rather than revealing a wall of text at once.

Use selective motion: an option is crossed out, a cost transfers, a barrier rises, a route changes, an actor reacts. A freeze before the choice can create room to think. Evidence must substantiate which options existed; do not invent a false choice to make the board dramatic.

Other useful scenes: side-by-side rival choices, a simple before/after system, a constrained resource moving, a timeline with one highlighted turn. Variation should clarify new information.

## Evidence and thumbnails

Real headlines/documents are sourced inserts, with publication date where relevant. Recreated evidence is labeled. Charts use editable verified text and accurate units. Do not generate fake headlines as proof.

Thumbnail concepts: one recognizable object or symbolic company, a clear contrast and optional short text in Inter Bold. Monochrome is a pilot hypothesis to test at phone size, not a guarantee of clicks.

## Sustainable production

Start with a small character/prop library and 5–8 reusable scene layouts. Reuse assets and identity while creating episode-specific scenes and explanations. Test a 30–45 second scene with meaningful motion before commissioning a batch.

Ask the editor to challenge anything expensive or unclear and suggest simpler implementations that preserve the character style. Constant full animation is not necessary; selective movement and good timing can carry the story.
