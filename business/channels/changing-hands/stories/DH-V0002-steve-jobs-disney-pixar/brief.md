## Central question and promise

How did Walt Disney's purchase of the studio that made *Toy Story* turn Apple's cofounder, Steve Jobs, into Disney's largest **individual** shareholder? The answer is not a personal cash payment or a claim that he controlled Disney: the 2006 **fixed-ratio all-stock acquisition** gave Jobs 138,000,004 new Disney shares for 60,000,002 Pixar shares, plus three pre-existing Disney shares, leaving him at ~6.3% of the enlarged Disney group. The exchange also made Pixar a 100%-owned Disney subsidiary and put Jobs on Disney's board.

**Everyday entry:** Recognizable *Toy Story*, Disney and Steve Jobs. **Changed understanding:** A huge buyer can pay for a company by issuing ownership in itself; the seller becomes a significant owner of the buyer. **Deeper question:** Why was this better for both sides than renegotiating a film-distribution agreement, and what was at risk in merging their cultures?
