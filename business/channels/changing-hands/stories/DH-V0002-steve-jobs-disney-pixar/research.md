## Decisive new research finding

The original script was materially less interesting than the primary documents. Disney and Pixar **co-owned the covered pictures**, Disney held **exclusive distribution/exploitation rights**, and (subject to exceptions) Disney's choice generally prevailed when the two disagreed about certain derivatives like sequels. Pixar's filings also stated it did not share in theme-park revenue. Pixar could pursue a distributor for *future new films* but was not free simply to transport the old, successful characters to a new studio. See Disney/Pixar SEC S-4/A material-contract discussion pp85–87 (S003), Pixar 10-K Business/Risk Factors (S002). The deal was not principally repurchasing rights Disney already controlled. It secured the **future creators of new characters and stories** under one corporate structure, alongside a governance/culture bargain.

**Important precision:** Co-owning films is distinct from Disney's exclusive exploitation rights; avoid saying Disney was the sole beneficial owner of every old film or that Pixar had no contractual rights. The “Disney's decision generally prevailed” phrase is limited to specified derivative disagreements and exceptions, not a universal override of Pixar's creative operations.

## Ownership and funding mechanism

`Pixar shareholder holding N shares → N × 2.3 newly issued Disney shares`. For illustrative N=10, 10 Pixar →23 Disney. Disney **did not** finance the acquisition by delivering $7.4bn cash to Jobs. Transaction equity valuation at announcement includes fully diluted Pixar equity and was based on Disney's market price. Pixar carried more than a billion dollars in its own cash which Disney's press release deducted when computing an announced “net of cash” figure. This is not necessarily identical to conventional audited enterprise value, after-tax Jobs proceeds, or realized investor profit.

Jobs controlled ~50.6% of Pixar's outstanding stock (economic and voting influence); his vote agreement committed shares representing **40%** of outstanding Pixar equity, less than his total personal holdings. His position in Disney after close was **~6.3%**. Different denominators, dates and legal facts. 6.3% did not let Jobs run Disney unilaterally; Disney board seat separate from the shares, Catmull/Lasseter operational leadership separate again.

Disney's existing shareholders were diluted by issued consideration. Their group owned a larger combined business rather than the same Disney plus a cash outlay. Disney accepted integration risks, while Pixar holders swapped independent studio exposure for ownership in Disney's wider film, parks and media business and took Disney stock-price exposure. The trade's future success was *not* guaranteed by its parties.

## Competing explanations and limits

- **Disney “bought Toy Story rights”**: incomplete and potentially false if taken literally, as it already had major exploitation/derivative rights under prior co-owned film contract. It **bought Pixar as company**, including future talent and business.
- **Pixar could just take every character to another studio**: false; old catalog/characters constrained by Disney contract.
- **2004 negotiations ended Disney's movie rights**: false; discussion was future films after existing commitments.
- **Steve Jobs got $7.4bn cash**: false; all-stock aggregate announced equity valuation, not personal cheque.
- **Jobs owned Disney**: false if implying control; ~6.3% at closing.
- **Disney forced Pixar to sell**: not established; Disney/Pixar boards considered legitimate alternatives, risks and fiduciary duties.
- **Merger clearly was right choice**: counterfactual unobservable, so no invented ROI or private emotions.
- **Share price was fixed**: exchange *ratio* was fixed; market value varied.
- **What happened to sequel/theme park revenue?** Pixar had significant derivative rights and specified opportunities for passive royalties/co-production; do not assert total disenfranchisement.
- **What do historic stock holdings prove?** SEC snapshot May 2006, not ongoing wealth, personal tax or subsequent sales.
