## Core thesis

Pixar and Disney were an effective but increasingly strained *contractual partnership*. After renewal talks broke in 2004, Pixar explored other distributors; Disney could continue trying to strengthen animation without a merger. Iger revived talks in 2005 and raised a full combination on October 12. The boards then negotiated how Pixar's operation and culture would be led and **the share exchange ratio**. Their 2.3-for-1 agreed structure let Disney own Pixar outright while making Jobs, previously Pixar's majority shareholder, a ~6.3% Disney shareholder plus Disney director, not a controlling Disney executive.

## Structure: who gave what to whom

**Before close:** Pixar was publicly traded. Jobs held **60,000,002** Pixar common shares, slightly more than half of Pixar's outstanding common shares. Disney held its preexisting equity base and contractual rights in various Pixar films; Disney *did not yet own 100% of the Pixar corporation*.

**Merger consideration:** For **every one Pixar common share**, the former holder received **2.3 newly issued Disney common shares**. Example, explicitly illustrative: 10 Pixar → 23 Disney. Previously outstanding Disney shares stayed outstanding but were a smaller percentage of an enlarged count. Pixar's outstanding common shares were exchanged/cancelled, and Pixar became a wholly owned subsidiary of Disney. Former Pixar investors, including Jobs, owned minority stakes in Disney. Disney issued approximately **277.9 million shares** (S006).

**Jobs reconciliation:** 60,000,002 Pixar shares × 2.3 = **138,000,004** Disney shares as reported by Schedule 13D; Jobs also held **three** Disney shares before the merger => **138,000,007** total, approximately **6.3%** of Disney's outstanding common shares, as defined in that filing (S007). This is **not** 6.3% of Pixar or a majority of Disney. A large individual ownership position is not equivalent to control of Disney.

**Vote:** Jobs owned about **50.6%** of Pixar's outstanding stock but the Jan 24 agreement committed shares representing **40% of Pixar's outstanding stock** to approval (S001, S003). Do not conflate the two percentages or imply that he alone guaranteed regulatory or other approval.

**Valuation:** Disney's Jan 24 release quoted **$7.4bn total transaction value**, measured at Disney's Jan 23 closing share price and fully diluted Pixar shares, and **$6.3bn net of just over $1bn of Pixar cash** (S001, S010). These were **announcement-date stock valuation calculations**, not billions wired to Jobs nor a constant closing share value. Contemporary media reported ~**$3.9bn** market value for Jobs' received shares after May close (S008), a **paper mark** subject to price changes. The $3.9bn cannot be treated as realized trading profit, cost basis, or proceeds. No exact personal after-tax windfall is reconstructed.

## Why accept stock instead of a plain distribution deal?

**Pixar:** avoid finding a new world-class distributor, keep Disney's reach, expand creative franchises across Disney's networks, parks and products, make Pixar shareholders participants in broad Disney returns. Public Pixar board materials called these *anticipated benefits*, not guaranteed post-close returns (S003).

**Disney:** use own stock as acquisition currency, control the Pixar corporation and future output, internalize negotiations between previously separate shareholders, potentially extend brand cross-sales. It also issued shares that diluted existing shareholders and risked damaging Pixar's creative process. Iger's reason for paying this much was an informed strategic judgment, not proven inevitable success.

**Management bargain:** Catmull led combined animation operations and Lasseter held creative roles across Pixar/Disney animation; operations/locations retained, Jobs received a Disney director seat. The filing expressly shows cultural/governance design was central before price finalized (S001, S003, S009). Avoid asserting Disney guaranteed Pixar's forever-independent creative control.

## Alternative explanations, counterevidence, uncertainty

1. **“Jobs sold Pixar for $7.4bn cash.”** False: Disney **issued stock** to all Pixar holders; $7.4bn is an aggregate announced valuation.
2. **“Jobs became Disney's owner.”** Misleading: he became Disney's largest **individual** shareholder, ~6.3%, not majority owner, Disney CEO or shareholder with unilateral control (S007/S008).
3. **“The two companies had already split permanently.”** False: 2004 termination of *future contract talks*, not termination of ongoing Toy Story/other distribution rights; new talks in June 2005 and final May 2006 corporate acquisition (S002/S003/S004).
4. **“Pixar could freely do anything else.”** It explored external distributors but its own filings classify preliminary discussions, and there were genuine distribution risks.
5. **“A 2.3 ratio meant guaranteed $7.4bn in cash.”** False: fixed **shares**, floating dollar value (S003 Risk Factors).
6. **“Jobs personally negotiated every item.”** Merger proxy details boards, advisors, Iger, Jobs and creative leadership. Specific conversations are reported at filing level; don't invent secret dialogue, exact private motivations, ultimatums or a stopwatch deadline.
7. **“The deal was obviously good.”** Pixar disclosed loss of operational control and integration/cultural risk. Disney holders faced dilution, acquired execution risk, and unknown future film outcomes. No historical counterfactual certifies the best alternative.
8. **“Apple was part of the merger.”** False: Jobs was CEO of Apple, but Apple was not merger counterparty.
9. **“Disney's buyer just got a logo.”** It acquired a **corporation and production team** with distribution, creative and franchise capabilities, a different mechanism from DH-I0007 separated asset/brand rights.
