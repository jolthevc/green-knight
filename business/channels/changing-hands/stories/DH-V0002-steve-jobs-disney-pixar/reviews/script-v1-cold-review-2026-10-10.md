## Prioritized interventions

**P1-A, exact ownership of film rights (P005–P010).** Narration gives impression Disney bought the right to use existing characters. Primary S-4/A pp85–87 says both co-owned covered Pictures but Disney had exclusive exploitation/distribution rights, decision rights over derivatives in many disputes and theme-park benefits; Pixar 10-K confirms Pixar couldn't simply take existing characters to another distributor. **Fix:** One concrete reveal: Pixar could change distributors for *future movies*, but it could not necessarily take *Nemo / Toy Story rights* or theme-park cash with it. That's why Disney was buying the engine behind new films, not the existing catalog again. **Evidence:** reopen S002/S003 (done). **Listener benefit:** makes the deal strategic rather than a generic M&A case.

**P1-B, introduce Jobs' leverage before boardroom (P007–P021).** He is an offstage narrator subject until P019. **Fix:** introduce his roughly half of Pixar at the 2004 breakdown; use his 40% support obligation only near signing in one clean sentence, not re-explaining both stakes. **Evidence:** S001, S003, S007. **Benefit:** audience knows whose decision moves the deal.

**P1-C, replace abstract middle with named choices/timed negotiation (P009–P016).** Several “alternatives” paragraphs describe what could happen but not what actually did. **Fix:** show June 2005 resumed distribution talks → Iger Oct 12 buyer proposal → Pixar board/Jobs protecting independent creative process → Jan 12 no price agreement despite roles → Jan 21 2.3 share exchange. Add Pixar's practical constraint, but don't invent adversarial quotes or private dramatic dialogue. **Evidence:** S-4/A Background pp46–49. **Benefit:** plot moves through documented action rather than definitions.

**P2-D, number barrage and speechability (P017–P024).** Good core 2.3 share mechanics submerged by $7.4/$6.3, ~278m, exact Jobs 60,000,002 → 138,000,004 +3 → 138,000,007, $3.9bn. **Fix:** In *spoken* text, say 2.3, roughly $7.4b aggregate stock value, about 138m Jobs Disney shares, ~6.3%. Keep $6.3b net Pixar cash, exact odd ending counts, ~278m issuance and $3.9b marked value in fact packet/sidecars unless editorial causality demands them. **Evidence:** S001/S006/S007. **Benefit:** one number at a time, fewer corrections and pauses.

**P2-E, sharpen ending and remove meta-commentary (P025–P030).** “Consider the trade,” “The deeper reason” and triple recap slow an already resolved climax. **Fix:** contrast Disney already owning the past film exploitation versus choosing to own Pixar's future creative engine. End with simple action/result: the creator's majority stake was surrendered to receive minority ownership in a wider Disney empire. **Evidence:** S002/S003/S007. **Benefit:** earned final insight, not a shareholder lecture.

## Audio-only and truth risks

No visual can rescue unclear distinction between Pixar 50.6% share ownership, Jobs' 40% committed shares, and Disney 6.3% stock. Never call 2.3 exchange a fixed dollar cash price, or describe $7.4bn as paid *to Jobs*. “Disney owned all Toy Story” would oversimplify the co-owned covered film agreements: Disney had exclusive distribution/exploitation and specified sequel-decision advantages, not merely one exclusive universal trademark right; state a defensible everyday consequence. Post-2006 success cannot be assumed as motive or proven deal IRR. Company projections on cultural protection are not evidence the promise was permanently achieved.
