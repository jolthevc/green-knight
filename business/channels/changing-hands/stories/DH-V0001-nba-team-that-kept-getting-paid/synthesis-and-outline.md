# View and story architecture: DH-V0001
Prepared after research and before the complete spoken draft on 2026-10-10.

## Editorial view
Thesis: By preserving an enduring claim on a defined television-income pool, the Spirits' owners separated financial participation from operating a team; the eventual settlement addressed that claim through ownership and financing.
Causal chain: excluded franchise seeks compensation -> continuing media participation -> larger receipts as qualifying pool grows -> scope disputes over new media -> settlement and announced debt/equity reorganization.
What the obvious story misses: the payment denominator, the difference between revenue rights and team ownership, and why owning the recipient changes the economics.
Strongest counterargument: hindsight makes unexpected growth look like a perfectly forecast plan. Retain uncertainty and the genuine desire to own an NBA franchise.
Known: shutdown, payment direction and duration, reported fraction/protection, disputed categories, announced framework, participant-confirmed settlement.
Inferred: how these rights changed bargaining power; why the announced structure can exchange a stream for cash/control.
Not established: net returns, ideal settlement valuation, every closing step, present-day ownership.
Takeaway: The important asset can be a contractual claim on somebody else's continuing business.
Runway: two transactions, one concrete fraction, one expansion complication, a media-definition dispute, and a human reason to settle support 8-10 minutes without league-history filler.

## Hook alternatives
1. Chosen: the Spirits never played in the NBA but received money tied to its television business after shutting down. Immediately distinguish ABA history and show the surviving contract. Strong title alignment; no disputed headline total.
2. Price-first: a reported half-billion-dollar settlement for owners of a vanished team. Strong amount, but less clear denominator and too much pressure on a reported figure; retain this amount for later.
3. Document-first: four modern clubs on one side of a 1976 contract. Accurate but requires more setup before the audience cares.
