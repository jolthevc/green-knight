## Constraints and alternatives
The departing teams needed an agreement allowing entry into the NBA; the Spirits sought compensation and had legal claims to release. The precise claim that they had an unconditional unilateral veto is not necessary and is not made. Fixed cash and future participation were economically distinct alternatives; no unsupported claim that one specified cash offer was exactly equivalent to the accepted package.

Later the owners could keep the right and continue disputes or agree to a restructuring. Long duration and uncertain revenue made valuation sensitive to growth, scope and the owners' time horizon. A growing stream is not infinitely valuable. The script explains the trade without estimating a discount rate or declaring a uniquely fair price.

## Counterevidence and alternative explanations
- Foresight alone is inadequate. Dan's retrospective interview acknowledged that actual growth exceeded their expectations (S006); negotiated protection does not prove prediction.
- Immediate merger pressure helps explain willingness to accept future obligations (S008). Avoid incompetent-owner or tricked-NBA caricatures.
- Full NBA-franchise ownership included different revenues, costs, control and resale value. We have not valued that counterfactual.
- S002-S003 adjudicated forum, not ultimate media entitlement. The script explicitly separates allegations from adjudicated facts.
- S004 was conditional. S005 confirms a settlement, but no inspected document proves every announced step's final amount and timing. Narration says what was announced and what was subsequently reported.
- The income recipient was a partnership with other interests and historical creditor claims. References to the brothers are human shorthand; no claim that every dollar was theirs individually.
- Receipts are not after-cost profit. The absence of a roster does not mean there were no costs or risks.
