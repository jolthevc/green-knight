# Research references for editor: DH-V0001

Script v3, exact SHA-256 `7d5945bf807fe9dad826173cd5d5cc860602c16a4bf379acf0224cc1ed2ac067`. Research supports factual accuracy; visual conception belongs to the editor.

The Spirits were an ABA franchise, never an NBA team; they ceased operations in 1976. Dan Silna's 2011 interview recalls the unsuccessful Pistons bid, only 600 Spirits season tickets and a competing excluded club, the Kentucky Colonels, accepting a one-time payment. Those are attributed retrospective accounts. Four NBA entrants were Denver, Indiana, San Antonio and the New York Nets. The Spirits partnership, not the defunct on-court team or the two brothers alone, held the contract.

One-seventh applied to each of four specified qualifying TV shares, not one-seventh of all NBA revenue. The $7m example in P008 is invented. Actual references: approximately $1.35m in 1987-88 court-recorded settlement payments and $17.5m reported for 2010-11; these are not personal net profits. The 28-team provision limited formula dilution, not payment variation. New media category underpayment remained an allegation; NY courts chose federal venue, not the monetary merits.

The reported $500m 2014 settlement should not be called a publicly itemized payout to the brothers. The [NBA's conditional release](https://pr.nba.com/nba-aba-spirits-settlement/) described an equity stake for four payers, remaining old owners' equity, proposed new Spirits debt and a partial borrowing distribution. Ozzie's later settlement confirmation does not supply undisclosed debt amounts or prove execution of every contemplated financing step.
