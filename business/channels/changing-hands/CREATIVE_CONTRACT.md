# Changing Hands: creative contract
The entertainment is **a financial caper with real stakes and no fictional villains**. Ownership, obligations, leverage or timing changes hands in a contest a newcomer can follow.

**Universal hook:** Name something people recognize, an improbable payment or astonishing ownership result, and the question of how it happened. Start with the prize or trap, not the instrument taxonomy.

**Dramatic motor:** Who wants what -> who controls what -> who owes what -> constraint -> deal/trade terms -> turning point -> who actually gained, lost or retained control. Introduce technical vocabulary only after a tangible example.

**Signature payoff:** The one contractual or economic mechanism deciding the outcome. Distinguish notional/capital, enterprise/equity, gross/net and paper/realized only where needed.

**Creative red flags:** finance terminology as excitement, generic deal recaps, excessive negotiating dates, invented countdowns, saying an announced plan definitely closed, no human stakes beyond big dollars.

**Distinct from others:** The financial contest or transaction is the narrative motor. Money Moves owns strategic operations; Silver Spoon owns inherited family control; Fool's Gold owns deceit; Fallen Angels owns decline.

**Pilot test:** At minute one, can a stranger name the stakes? At the midpoint, can they explain what each party stands to gain or lose? At the end, can they repeat the exact constraint that flipped leverage?

**Strong reference:** The Spirits of St. Louis contract has an instantly legible paradox. The Disney/Pixar script should keep the creative-future versus existing-character-rights tension more prominent than chronology.

## Required popcorn-good channel contract (2026-10-11)
Read business/POPCORN_STANDARD.md. **Earned engagement is the first creative test, with truthful evidence as the minimum floor.** A first-timer can follow a real human wager, disagreement or apparent financial impossibility without learning trading jargon first. An expert learns the decisive but easily missed right, obligation, funding constraint, collateral structure or sequencing that determined who actually controlled value. **BAD:** endless tickers and prices. **GOOD:** one contractual or capital mechanism reverses who appears to be winning; put it on screen and show its consequence. Distinguish notional wealth, mark-to-market, realized cash and ownership.

Run TWO explicit tests on every new channel concept, idea and scripted episode: **newcomer pull** (immediate human stakes, first-listen comprehension and real desire to continue) and **already-expert reward** (a specific warranted new lens or insightful causal model). Name the weakest likely middle sequence, create authentic reversals, and show how the best strategic insight *unfolds inside the plot*. A correct but predictable lecture, a generic takeaway or beautiful prose without audience momentum is REVISE/REFRAME, never 'ready.' Never invent twists or inflate unsourced certainty. On review/revise, record both verdicts and actual paragraph/beat fixes rather than praising general quality.
