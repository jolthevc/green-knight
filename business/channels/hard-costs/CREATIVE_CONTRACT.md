# Hard Costs creative contract

**Entertainment:** make familiar structures and everyday trades extraordinarily interesting by exposing a surprising business mechanism, with human decisions and real consequences.

**Hook:** start with a recognizable object, famous structure, ambitious tradesperson, vivid project predicament or source-supported financial paradox, not jargon, an industry summary or an impressive crane montage.

**Story motor:** a real person/object/project -> specific puzzle -> earned early answer -> choice or obligation makes the answer incomplete -> consequences and real counterevidence -> deeper economic mechanism -> an ending that changes how the viewer sees the beginning. A diagnostic, not an obligatory section template.

**Operator payoff:** how documented people built or lost a business, what was actually required to acquire the first customer and scale operations, what money and risks changed, what an operator could conditionally apply. No “ten secrets,” motivational guru claims or guaranteed $100M outcomes.

**Icons payoff:** a globally recognizable landmark told through actual financiers, builders, contracts, schedules, workers and the project's business legacy, not sightseeing.

**Double test:** Newcomer can understand, care and keep watching from the first minute; knowledgeable contractor/investor receives a non-obvious, defensible insight. Each major beat changes viewer belief or stakes. No empty suspense, fabricated conflict, phony archival documents, generic insights or unsupported financial claims.

**Distinct lane:** construction-specific business truth. Every episode should convey a concrete business insight; no broad generic real estate/investment advice.

**Editor:** Green Knight directs exact narration, one final title/thumbnail/first frame, scenes, graphic transformations, dramatic transitions and audio timing. Practical execution freedom belongs to the editor. All shared creative, source, review and director-owned exactly-three-file release standards apply. No genuine narrator/visual approval means NOT PRODUCTION READY.
