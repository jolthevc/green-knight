# Hard Costs: 19 editorial categories
All categories are **editorial scope**, not separate channels, quotas or automatically approved episodes. An episode can touch multiple lanes; record the primary business mechanism.

## Extraordinary stories
1. **Why Projects Fail:** project overruns, bankruptcies and contractual/operational breakdowns.
2. **The Great Construction Scams:** substantiated fraud, corruption, bidding misconduct and exposure, with rigorous legal sourcing.
3. **America Under Construction:** major public infrastructure, financing, builders, contracts and value distribution.
4. **Icons: Great Structures:** Empire State Building, Golden Gate Bridge, Hoover Dam, Sydney Opera House, Panama Canal and more. Show the money, people, builders and economic stakes, not simply engineering or tourism.

## Money, deals and bottlenecks
5. **The Economics of Construction:** job profit, cash, retainage, claims, labor, risk and incentives.
6. **Follow the Money:** trace dollars and obligations from owner/lender through GC, subs, suppliers and workers.
7. **The Deal:** how major contracts are bid, priced, bonded, financed, awarded and delivered.
8. **Why Can't We Build That?:** permits, zoning, utilities, infrastructure capacity, land, regulatory barriers.

## Companies and entrepreneurs
9. **The Companies That Build America:** profiles of builders, suppliers, specialty companies, manufacturers and service firms.
10. **Hidden Empires:** overlooked giants in aggregates, rental, scaffolding, testing, distribution and specialist products.
11. **Contractor Millionaires / Built From Nothing:** documented real entrepreneur and trades-company growth stories.
12. **The Construction Wars:** consequential industry rivalries, with strategy and economics rather than brand gossip.
13. **The Operator's Playbook:** sophisticated, honest stories showing how firms scale, what others could replicate, and what could break. Examples: how plumbers build $100M-revenue businesses; from one truck to a regional firm; acquiring a first contractor; recurring-revenue service.

## The physical economy
14. **The Trades:** electrical, plumbing, HVAC, roofing, fire, concrete, elevator and other specialty contracting.
15. **The Supply Chain:** source, manufacturing, logistics, distributors, site and installation of materials and systems.
16. **The Invisible Building:** elevators, HVAC, piping, controls, fire and safety systems and long-term maintenance.
17. **The Labor Market:** skilled workforce economics, apprenticeship, unions, immigration, shortages, productivity and staffing.

## Reinventing construction
18. **The Future of Building:** robotics, AI, prefabrication, industrialization and frontier construction economics.
19. **The Construction Experiment:** test innovative methods against reality: capital, schedule, labor, cost, regulation, quality and deployment.

## Recurring storytelling franchises, not extra categories
- **Who Gets the Money?** is usually category 6.
- **Hidden Giants** is usually 10.
- **Built From Nothing** is usually 11.
- **The Bottleneck** is usually 8, 15 or 17.
- **The Deal That Went Wrong** is usually 1 or 7.

## Programming principle
Alternate broad curiosity and narrow operator utility. Every film, even an Icons episode, must reveal a meaningful construction-business mechanism. The channel is not generic architectural history, a how-to trades course, a news feed or a listicle factory. Narrative structure follows the particular story. Check cross-channel duplicate mechanisms before commissioning.
