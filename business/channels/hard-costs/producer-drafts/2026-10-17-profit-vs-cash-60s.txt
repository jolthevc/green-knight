TITLE: How a Profitable Construction Project Can Bankrupt a Contractor

Imagine winning a ten-million-dollar construction contract.

You've priced the labor, materials, and equipment. If everything goes according to plan, you'll make eight hundred thousand dollars in profit.

Sounds like a great deal.

But six weeks later, you're calling the bank.

Because construction has a strange problem.

You often have to spend the money long before you collect it.

Your workers need paychecks. Suppliers demand deposits. Equipment rentals keep piling up.

But your customer might not pay until the work has been inspected, approved, and invoiced.

And even then, part of that payment might be held until the project is finished.

So on paper, your business is profitable.

On the jobsite, construction is moving forward.

But in your bank account, the money is disappearing.

And the faster you build, the more cash you might need.

That's the paradox of construction.

You can win the contract, build the project, and expect to make a profit...

And still go bankrupt before you get paid.
