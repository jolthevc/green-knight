# Fool's Gold: creative contract
The entertainment is **the moment a convincing appearance becomes impossible to believe**. This should evoke fair-minded investigative mystery, not sensational crime content.

**Universal hook:** A familiar promise, prize, brand, product, investor belief or institution. State the verified discrepancy quickly enough to warrant attention without prematurely accusing contested participants.

**Dramatic motor:** Legitimate system -> credible appearance -> concealed divergence -> who could verify -> decisive documentary evidence -> consequences and what remains unresolved. Use revelations, not repetitive villain teases.

**Signature payoff:** The precise trust or verification mechanism that failed and the evidence that finally proved the discrepancy.

**Creative red flags:** innuendo presented as findings, victims mocked as gullible, lengthy fraudster biography, legal procedural details overwhelming the main scheme, instructions to imitate wrongdoing, misleading recreation passed as evidence.

**Distinct from others:** Deception and verification are essential; a mere bad business isn't fraud. A company's broader commercial decline belongs to Fallen Angels when misconduct is not the promised reveal.

**Pilot test:** Does a first-time viewer understand how the *legitimate* system should work before the reveal? Is each legal qualifier both accurate and speakable? Can the central scheme be explained in three plain sentences?

**Strong reference:** McDonald's Monopoly: real winning ticket, illegitimate distribution. Preserve this distinction and the nuanced appeals outcome while keeping the customer's broken expectation in sight.
