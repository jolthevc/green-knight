# Research dossier | FG-V0001 McDonald's Monopoly

**Canonical:** v2, SHA-256 `9c86e31841030a86c68cf943bf9e789e39af8dbc0df88725b8fa1ddf7399d496`. **As of:** 2026-10-10. Opened documentary research includes all sources recorded in `evidence.json`, including one new appellate case (S009). This does not imply archival image/music licenses.

## The verified story, rather than a generic fraud narrative

McDonald's used Simon Marketing as an outside games administrator. High-value winning pieces had to be deliberately disseminated into packaging and newspaper inserts. **Jerome Jacobson was Simon's director of security with responsibility for disseminating these high-value stamps**, as confirmed by the 11th Circuit's `Chandler` opinion (S005); a later California insurance coverage opinion (S009) calls this the nationwide **seeding** role. This legitimate responsibility, rather than mere presence near a printer, gave him leverage to intercept rare pieces. He ultimately pleaded guilty to stealing winning pieces; a different set of defendants later obtained acquittals because the prosecution failed to establish their knowledgeable participation.

A genuine winning token might pass redemption even if the legitimate custody chain had been broken before the ticket reached the public. In the **initial 2001 government allegation**, intermediaries recruited prize claimants, the issuer paid advertised prizes, and part of the payout was allegedly shared among claimants, recruiters and Jacobson. This is not proof that every participant knew of Jacobson's theft or that all game pieces were rigged.

## Where the economic value moved

**Legitimate expectation:** random placement of rare tickets → customer acquires eligible game piece → issuer validates win → issuer delivers prize.

**Documented/charged deviation:** Jacobson-controlled security distribution → stolen real winning piece → recruiter → eventual claimant → legitimate issuer payout → alleged kickbacks upstream. This does not require forged stickers; the risk sits in control of genuine scarce assets.

**Dollar clarity:** The 2001 DOJ >$13m is alleged *stated top-prize total* at the initial announcement, not net Jacobson cash or verified recovered money. Contemporary S003 described **one** $500,000 winning claim with an alleged $70,000 allocation intended for Jacobson; not every prize had this split. Many $1m games paid $50k annually; do not equate the advertised amount with immediate bank deposits. S009's $21m is Simon's claimed total redemption value in later insurance litigation, **not** an update to $13m covering identical definitions.

## The legal counterevidence is substantive

The `Chandler` opinion (S005) recounts Jacobson's testimony that he gave stamps to **ten recruiters** and deliberately kept recruits separate. It notes the government had not proved these four appellants **knew** the pieces they redeemed were stolen. Transfers of game pieces were publicly tolerated; rule violations alone could not substitute for proof of the charged conspiracy. The 11th Circuit ordered acquittals rather than a new trial for those four. It did **not** overturn Jacobson's own guilty plea or establish that no recruiter ever knowingly assisted him.
