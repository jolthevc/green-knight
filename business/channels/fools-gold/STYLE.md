# Magician-inspired editorial illustration

A vintage stage-magic vocabulary used to reveal business mechanisms: curtains, spotlights, false fronts, mirrored boxes and objects with something different behind them. Approachable, adult and lightly theatrical.

The magician-inspired direction was discussed. Exact colors, fonts and production conventions below are proposed for a pilot. No permanent magician mascot is selected.

## Palette

| Color | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warm ivory | #F6EEDD | Explanatory canvas and light objects |
| Deep plum | #34263F | Stage backgrounds, primary dark text |
| Muted gold | #C5A05A | Apparent value and selected reveals |
| Dusty coral | #BD7667 | Discrepancy, friction or an opposing layer |
| Lavender gray | #A9A1B4 | Supporting shapes and secondary fills |

Use deep plum on ivory for reading, or ivory on deep plum. Gold is an accent, not small body text. Verify contrast on the actual frame; labels and shapes should convey meaning alongside color.

Do not turn every scene into a dark stage. Alternate theatrical setups with clean light diagrams so the explanation stays clear.

## Typography

Bodoni Moda Semibold: short title cards and theatrical editorial headings.
DM Sans Regular/Medium: explanations, captions and diagram labels.
DM Sans Bold: numeric emphasis and thumbnails.
IBM Plex Mono Medium: optional compact evidence/date labels.

Confirm availability and licenses. Display serifs stay large; do not use fine decorative letterforms for small chart labels. Limit the number of type treatments and inspect at phone size.

## Visual vocabulary

Hand-drawn or cut-paper adult figures, contracts, coins, ledgers, boxes, shop displays, masks of credibility and revealed compartments. A curtain can separate appearance from underlying reality; the mechanism itself should use precise labeled diagrams.

Props must follow the topic. A counterfeit-product episode can reveal an object's provenance; a financial scheme can reveal the true funding path. No compulsory rabbit, top hat or magician in every shot.

Use symbolic misdirection without representing a real person as a criminal caricature. A stylized character is not an authentic likeness or documentary evidence.

## Signature device: appearance versus reality

Show two clearly labeled layers:
- What participants were told or shown.
- What verified evidence establishes.

Build the underlying flow progressively. If part of it remains uncertain, label the gap rather than filling it with an invented transaction.

A reveal should change understanding, not merely add a dramatic animation. Use one purposeful curtain, false-panel or lighting transition at a major explanatory moment, rather than a trick after every sentence.

## Real evidence and movement

Blend illustrated scenes with verified document extracts and accurate charts. Document inserts carry source/date and remain readable. Separate actual evidence from reconstructions.

Never create a realistic fake audit, bank statement, newspaper page or court record as proof. If a teaching diagram needs a simplified record, label it illustrative and avoid reproducing an executable forgery template.

Selective motion: cash follows its real route, a claimed asset becomes an empty labeled box, an evidence tag appears, or the apparent system separates into layers. Hold essential information long enough to read.

## Pilot and production

Ask for a 30–45 second integrated sample showing a theatrical setup, a clear mechanism diagram and a sourced evidence insert. Include a title card and thumbnail preview.

The editor should challenge costly or unclear choices and recommend sustainable equivalents. Keep consistent edge treatment, proportions, palette and type. Reuse the vocabulary while creating original episode-specific explanations.

The style can communicate intrigue, but it cannot establish that an accusation is true or guarantee clicks.
