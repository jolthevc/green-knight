# On the Map scripts: 8–10 minutes

Research and synthesize first, then write the full initial narration in one pass. Revise internally before adding sparse audio guidance. Follow the shared editorial gates without requiring approval for each intermediate step.

## Narrative jobs
| Movement | Discovery |
| --- | --- |
| Opening puzzle | A recognizable object, journey, result or human predicament makes this place interesting |
| Orientation and early answer | Where we are, why it matters, and one substantive part of the answer |
| Starting conditions and foothold | What existed before, what was difficult, who chose the place and why |
| Reinforcement or divergence | How one development altered incentives for the next; why activity stayed or changed |
| Money, people and constraint | Who benefits, who pays, what limits the system and whether responses work |
| Resolution | A precise answer to why here, what sustains it and what the viewer now understands differently |
| Closing | Brief relevant next-watch invitation after delivering the answer |

Adapt order to the evidence and format. Do not force a growth cycle onto decline, a single founding moment onto gradual development, or a rescue onto unresolved problems. Total actual runtime is 480–600 seconds, including pauses and closing. Do not pad or speed-read to fit.

## No unexplained transformation
An intriguing present-day outcome may open the episode. Then establish the relevant starting conditions. Do not spend the opening on generic history or treat a mature ecosystem as a free starting asset. Explain each important transition, including access, demand, infrastructure, labor, capital or policy where decisive. A bigger number alone is not a new insight.

Distinguish origin from persistence. Ask why here rather than a plausible alternative without inventing a rivalry. Show the people/institutions making decisions; avoid treating a city as a single actor. The success of an employer or property market is not proof of shared resident prosperity.

## Discovery and first-listen clarity
Prepare two or three honest hooks and select one aligned with the title and actual ending. Orient listeners quickly and deliver useful partial value in the opening movement, normally within the first minute as an editorial hypothesis. Let the partial answer raise a deeper question instead of withholding all substance.

In the outline, record what each beat changes in understanding and why the next follows. One central question should organize the video. Explain proximity using a tangible consequence before introducing a term such as agglomeration. Use concrete verbs, selective numbers and understandable comparisons; preserve full arithmetic and qualifications in research. Essential relationships must make sense in audio alone.

Use history only when it changes the explanation. Limit place-name and date accumulation. Reorient a returning location by its role, not a repeated geography lesson. Document geographic pronunciation and speak numbers naturally. Humor may emerge from real incongruity, not invented facts or ridicule of residents.

## Review checks
Apply the newcomer and informed-skeptic lenses. Can a newcomer explain where the activity is, why it started and what sustains or changes it? Does an informed viewer learn beyond a familiar slogan? Test the weakest middle stretch, plausible alternative, causal evidence, who gains, meaningful constraint and opening/ending agreement. Check geographic scope, period, statistics and source independence. Do not announce why a place won if research only establishes correlation.

Keep the final insight specific and defensible. It may reveal opportunity, dependence, unintended consequences or unresolved trade-offs. No mandatory upbeat ending, doom, tourism pitch or claim of a universal development recipe.

## Canonical script and production
script.txt begins with exactly TITLE: <metadata.title>, one blank line, then narration. The header is non-spoken; exclude it from audio, word counts and P001 numbering. SHA-256 covers the entire exact file including header. Keep citations, cues, pronunciation notes and headings outside the spoken body. Update version/hash/evidence/cues together after changes.
