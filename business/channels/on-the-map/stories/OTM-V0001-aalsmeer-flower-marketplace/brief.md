# Selected story brief

- **IDs:** OTM-I0007 / OTM-V0001.
- **Selected by/date:** Owner explicitly selected OTM-I0007, 2026-10-11.
- **Channel/format:** On the Map, gateway/crossroads with industry-cluster transitions.
- **Title:** How a Dutch Town Became the World's Flower Marketplace.
- **Alternatives:** Why the World's Flowers Go Through the Netherlands (too absolute for current evidence); The Dutch Town That Prices the World's Flowers (price control needs qualification).
- **Boundary:** Aalsmeer town as the historical starting point; RFH hubs, Schiphol and competing European logistics as economic catchment. Starting position mid-1800s; foothold 1911–12; current evidence up to 2026.
- **Everyday entry:** A bouquet, where the flower grew versus where it was traded.
- **Central question:** Why Aalsmeer, why continued trade concentration after growing globalized, and who gets the economic benefit?
- **Audience promise:** Learn the unlikely journey from growers' bargaining problem to a network of auction, digital, logistics and distribution services.
- **Research finding:** The initial draw was production and cooperative price discovery; persistence relies on buyer and service agglomeration. Schiphol amplified, not created, the original cluster.
- **Money mechanism:** Growers make product; trading and logistics coordinate transactions; wholesalers/retailers sell to customers; no blanket inference of profits from traded product value.
- **Human stakes:** Grower bargaining power, fresh goods, jobs, wage risks, environmental costs, consumer price and waste.
- **Lane:** Geography and local persistence are essential. Not a Pretty Penny 'how much does a bouquet cost' episode, and not Louisville OTM-I0002's parcel hub.
- **Dedup:** Full 45-entry ledger checked at selection; no same canonical answer. Same cluster theme as OTM-I0002 but different participants, ownership and price discovery.
- **Evergreen/freshness:** Historical cooperative geography evergreen; RFH 2025 figures and 2026 digital offerings need pre-upload recheck.
- **Production constraints:** Audio-first explanation in 480–600 seconds; no fabricated route, voice selection or visual treatment. Editor owns visuals.

## What changes for a broad viewer

Initially: an expensive flower detour seems wasteful. First useful value: collective market access solved a bargaining problem before aviation. Later twist: the place can matter even when flowers grow elsewhere and buyers trade digitally. Deeper question: what remains location-bound and who bears hidden costs?

## Script v2 editorial reframing, 2026-10-11

The user-requested critical review found the original script did not fully test persistence. The documentary now opens with Royal FloraHolland's **March 2026 decision not to expand location-independent centralized auctions**, and later examines its **spring 2026 Aalsmeer peak delivery problems**. The geographical answer is: farmers first formed a cooperative marketplace near production and Amsterdam demand; municipality and transport retained activity as it expanded; today prices and deals can migrate digitally, but many physical services must match time-sensitive demand. The 2026 retreat is management's conditional strategic assessment, not proof every rose needs a Dutch hub. The buyer mixed-flowers example is explicitly illustrative. v2 is `scripted`, not recorded or production-ready.
