# Voice: a curious guide to how places work

Warm, observant, precise and conversational. Speak to an intelligent newcomer. Let surprising relationships carry excitement; avoid travel-ad enthusiasm, textbook cadence, geopolitical bombast, stereotypes and constant dramatic emphasis.

Narrator/provider/model/accent are unselected. Use the shared [narration standard](../../NARRATION_STANDARD.md) and versioned narration.json. Audition a representative opening, spatial explanation, number passage and reflective ending. Log real settings, recordings and listening; no claims of approval from text alone.

## Writing and cues
- Orient with one useful relationship at a time: near the port, beyond the mountain, within the delivery window. Avoid a stack of compass directions and proper names.
- Introduce a person's role before biography. Use familiar short labels consistently for locations.
- Provide verified pronunciation references for place names and relevant people, with local variants noted when needed. Do not assume a voice engine interprets phonetic text or SSML.
- Put sparse intent, emphasis, pace and pause cues in the sidecar and readable narration guide, anchored to the exact script paragraphs/version/hash. Never insert them into spoken narration.
- Slow for causal or numerical relationships, briefly pause after a discovery, and allow a reflective ending. Do not mark every sentence or force artificial suspense.
- Phrase numbers for listening with units, period and a meaningful comparison. Keep detailed calculations in research.

During production listen for mispronounced names, incorrect stress, geographical ambiguity, swallowed units, robotic lists, retake seams and cue text accidentally read aloud. Compare beginning/middle/end with the approved reference and measure complete runtime. Edit substance before accelerating speech to meet 8–10 minutes.
