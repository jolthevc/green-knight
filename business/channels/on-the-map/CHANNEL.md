# On the Map: why here, why it stayed, what it created

## Audience and promise
A curious adult should discover the business logic underneath a place without needing geography or economics training. Enter through a familiar object, journey, desire or human predicament. The emotional experience is discovery: the viewer will look at a place differently afterward. Ambition, convenience, status, belonging and livelihood make the economics matter.

The place and its surrounding system must be essential to the final answer. This is economic storytelling through places, with a clear central question per episode. Avoid generic city profiles, travel itineraries, rankings, country stereotypes, unrelated geographical trivia, promotional investment pitches and broad histories that cannot be explained in 8–10 minutes.

## Establish the starting conditions
Open with an intriguing outcome, then reconstruct the relevant world before it became obvious. Do not begin by assuming a mature airport, skilled workforce, investor confidence or supplier network. Identify what was present, absent or difficult; who made an early choice; why it was viable; and how that foothold enabled something else.

Never label an inhabited place as having started from nothing. A relevant starting point is not necessarily the city's founding date. Include earlier residents, livelihoods, institutions, dispossession or labor arrangements when they materially explain the transformation. Do not add centuries of unrelated background.

A strategic location must be strategic for an actor, activity, route and constraint. Cheap land, taxes, weather or a port alone rarely explain why this place rather than another. Explain which costs, access, risks or opportunities changed, and for whom.

## Origin is not persistence
Separate the first attraction from the later reasons activity remains. A documented chain might run from an early employer to trained workers, specialist suppliers, new employers and accumulated infrastructure. Prove the links; the presence of several ingredients does not establish causal order. Concentration can also weaken, relocate or fail to form.

Do not force every case into a virtuous cycle, a single founder story or an inevitable success. Identify geography, policy, institutions, markets, migration, capital, technology, chance and timing according to evidence. Prefer a few well-supported mechanisms to a list of everything that might matter.

## Recurring research questions
- Why did activity begin here, at this time, given actual starting conditions?
- What made the next participant choose this place, and what new advantage did their arrival create?
- Why did activity stay, grow, move or decline after the initial advantage changed?
- Which plausible alternative location or competing explanation tests the account? No invented rivalry or unsupported counterfactual certainty.
- Who supplies money, labor, land and infrastructure; who captures income, profits, asset appreciation or tax receipts; who bears costs?
- Which constraints threaten the system; what responses were attempted; what did they cost and accomplish?

A prosperous place and prosperous residents are different claims. Separate output, employment, real wages, property wealth, tax revenue, cost of living and profits accruing elsewhere. Distinguish activity passing through a place from value retained there.

## Six formats
| Format | Promised answer | Evidence discipline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Industry cluster | Why this activity concentrates here | Origin, supplier/labor/customer links, persistence and rivals |
| Gateway or crossroads | Why goods, people or information pass through here | Routes, time/cost constraints, capacity, location decisions and local capture |
| Destination and status | Why people pay to visit or access this place | Demand, access, scarcity, operators, workers and economics |
| Place reshaped by a decision | How an intervention changed the local system | Before/after, decision context, finance, implementation and alternative causes |
| Dependency and reinvention | What changes when an economic engine weakens | Exposure, timing, responses, winners/losers and evidence of recovery or continued decline |
| Competing places | Why comparable places took different paths | Comparable geography, periods and metrics; differences that undermine comparison |

These are formats within one channel, not obligatory series brands. Early candidates should favor bounded towns, districts, corridors or regional systems. Country-scale topics need a sufficiently narrow question. Failures and incomplete reinventions are eligible; endings need not be optimistic.

## Boundaries and duplication
Pretty Penny explains an ongoing money system; On the Map explains why it takes a particular spatial form. Money Moves follows a strategic choice; On the Map must explain the wider place and consequences. First Billion follows a fortune or business-building path; On the Map follows an economic location. Fallen Angels follows business decline; On the Map can follow what dependence or decline means for a community. Deals, family transfers and fraud stay with Changing Hands, Silver Spoon and Fool’s Gold when those are the promised answer.

Apply [lane routing](../../LANE_ROUTING.md). Remove the place name as a diagnostic: if the answer stays materially intact, the geographic premise may be decorative. This is a judgment test, not a ban on generalizable insights. Record related ideas and compare final answers before revisiting a subject. Emirates/Dubai proposals must explicitly check MM-I0002.

## Editorial contract
Reconstruct real choices without fabricated dialogue, motives or composite residents passed off as actual people. Distinguish documented history, analysis, causal inference and illustrative examples. Do not attribute all outcomes to one policy simply because it preceded them. Local promotional narratives require independent checks.
