# House Money: the business of housing

## The promise
**Show how money is made, moved, invested, borrowed, earned, lost and captured in the housing system.** Follow a home or resident through land, building, mortgage, sale, rent, maintenance, insurance, financing and resale to uncover the people and businesses on the other side of the transaction.

A housing purchase is emotionally recognizable. The hidden business mechanics — a land option, mortgage-servicing right, rent roll, development pro forma, commission, insurer's risk pool or factory production line — turn the familiar into a captivating story.

**Dual-viewer test:** A prospective renter, first-time buyer or curious outsider sees why this affects real life. An informed lender, developer, property operator or housing executive learns a sourced, precise and potentially surprising mechanism or its limitation.

## The seven lanes
1. **Homebuilding economics:** production builders, land strategy, development, construction debt, density, pricing, residential subcontracting, builders' financial services.
2. **Mortgages and housing finance:** origination/brokers, warehousing, underwriting, servicing, securitization, agency structures, default/loss allocation, affordability and interest incentives.
3. **Renting and landlord economics:** multifamily, single-family rentals, manufactured-home communities, property management, vacancy, expense ratios, financing, resident service.
4. **The transaction machine:** listing/search platforms, agents, brokerages, title/escrow, appraisal, inspections, taxes, closing-cost distribution and business-model changes.
5. **Costs of ownership:** insurance, HOA/condo fees, maintenance, property taxes, utilities, warranties, home service businesses as part of the housing cost stack.
6. **Land, permission and supply:** zoning/entitlements, permits, infrastructure capacity, land economics, subdivision approvals, utilities, density and why supply does/doesn't emerge.
7. **Housing models and innovation:** manufactured/modular/prefab, build-to-rent, co-living, alternative housing finance/ownership, new construction economics, successful/failed business-model tests.

## Repeatable narrative forms
- **Follow the Money:** who gets paid when a house changes hands, a monthly mortgage payment flows, or rent is collected.
- **The Hidden Business:** open a familiar product or service and reveal its distinct unit economics and operators.
- **The Housing Empire:** reconstruct one documented company or platform's expansion and structural advantage.
- **The Housing Paradox:** investigate a surprising market outcome, competing explanations and who benefits.
- **The Big Bet:** follow a developer, lender or operator making a material decision with capital, risk and alternatives.

## What it is not
No interest-rate stock-picking, neighborhood recommendations, property flips/guru content, daily headlines or false certainty on housing policy. Housing affordability, investor ownership and landlord-tenant disputes have multiple dimensions; present factual mechanisms, serious counterevidence, distributional effects and relevant perspectives without pushing a political position. No unverified claim that an entire industry is colluding or that a single actor caused all price changes.

## Cross-network boundary
- **Hard Costs:** who physically finances, bids, builds and bears risk in the construction of a landmark/project. **House Money:** the housing industry's underlying property business, resident costs and capital flows. HC-I0006 already asks why empty land cannot always be built on: House Money's neighborhood/affordability story must explain residential land-to-lot economics with a different answer, not re-upload the same utilities/permissions explainer.
- **Built to Scale:** how a contractor recruits, finds customers, borrows, acquires and runs teams. House Money can profile a *homebuilder* when the dominant insight is land inventory, mortgage buy-downs, community economics and the homebuyer transaction, not a contractor scaling lesson.
- **Pretty Penny:** one familiar payment's economics versus housing-specific system or institutional organization. **Money Moves:** one corporate decision; House Money: a residential ecosystem and its participants. **On the Map:** why an exact city or region formed an economic cluster; House Money: wider housing incentives and constraints. **Fallen Angels:** why a company collapsed; House Money: housing supply/financing mechanism. Only greenlight the same company/period in two channels when evidence, mechanism, story and audience takeaway genuinely differ. Link related IDs in the global ledger and avoid duplicate scripts/videos.

## Truth and ethics
Distinguish house price from construction cost, land cost, developer profit, assessed value, market value, loan balance, equity, mortgage rate vs APR, principal vs interest, payment vs servicing income, rent vs net operating income and enterprise value vs owner proceeds. Date and geography every economics claim. Do not present illustrative financial models as an actual household's or company's figures, promise financial returns or give personalized mortgage/investment advice. Treat residents, renters, homeowners, operators and policymakers with dignity; show winners AND those bearing costs.
