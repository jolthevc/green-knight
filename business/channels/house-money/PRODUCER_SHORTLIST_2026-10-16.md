# House Money: 10 best episode pitches for a prospective producer
**Prepared 2026-10-16. Ranked editorial shortlist, not an episode commission.**

This is the precise shortlist supplied for the House Money Fiverr outreach. All ten entries **reuse the existing IDs** in `business/ideas/registry.json`; their titles are preserved as aliases to catch near-duplicate future ideation. They are all **proposed** (unresearched/unselected); no HM-V story has been reserved. Before any script/production, verify primary evidence, actual company history, titles and source feasibility. No producer has been proven to receive or accept this brief.

**What House Money is:** Premium ~8–10-minute business documentaries explaining the hidden economics of the housing system. Strong dual audience: a smart homebuyer/renter and an informed real-estate/finance operator. Modern MBA's investigative business storytelling, Wendover's system visualizations and WSJ's financial graphics, with authentic residential footage. Lead with a recognizable human puzzle, introduce the economic mechanism, stage source-backed reversals, and reward the viewer with a real, defensible insight. Not realtor advice, stock footage montages, home-flipping tips, housing-rate predictions or purely political commentary.

## Pitches and visual directions

### 1. Why America Struggles to Build Starter Homes — HM-I0001
**Viewer hook:** Why builders struggle to produce affordable new houses.
**Investigative story:** Why do land, regulation, infrastructure, finance, product design and demand make entry-level new homes difficult to deliver in some markets?
**Visual production path and caveats:** Compare subdivisions, developed lot costs, builder pricing and home-size mix; reveal a transparent home-cost bridge. Qualify geography and timeframe.
**Source starting points to inspect (not pre-verified):**
HUD/Census new-home completion and starts; NAHB builder cost surveys; public builder filings; county subdivision/zoning and infrastructure records.

### 2. How Zillow Makes Money From Homes It Doesn't Own — HM-I0008
**Viewer hook:** The hidden business behind the free property-search platform.
**Investigative story:** Follow a user's search to advertising, agent-related services and other verified revenue segments. Distinguish past iBuying from the current model.
**Visual production path and caveats:** Show app search, leads and revenue flow; filings/screenshots and animated business model; confirm the accuracy of the 'doesn't own' framing for period.
**Source starting points to inspect (not pre-verified):**
Zillow annual and quarterly SEC filings, historical iBuying shutdown reports and product/segment disclosures.

### 3. The Strange Business of America's Mobile Home Parks — HM-I0005
**Viewer hook:** Owning the home while renting the land beneath it.
**Investigative story:** The ownership split between manufactured home and leased plot creates particular incentives, costs and resident constraints.
**Visual production path and caveats:** Separate home vs land visually, trace lot rent, park operating costs and capital, investigate protections and local exceptions.
**Source starting points to inspect (not pre-verified):**
HUD manufactured housing statistics; resident/land lease statutes; owner/operator disclosures and community regulatory records.

### 4. How Wall Street Turned Houses Into a Rental Business — HM-I0006
**Viewer hook:** The financial engineering and operating model behind institutional single-family rentals.
**Investigative story:** How portfolios of scattered homes can be bought, financed and managed; what scale actually achieves and where it falls short.
**Visual production path and caveats:** Animate acquisitions, geographic density, rent/expenses/debt waterfall; verify institutional share and housing-market effects rather than overclaim.
**Source starting points to inspect (not pre-verified):**
Public single-family rental company filings; Census/ACS; independently sourced local market estimates and financing disclosures.

### 5. What Actually Happens to Your Mortgage After Closing? — HM-I0003
**Viewer hook:** Where the mortgage goes, who collects payments and why.
**Investigative story:** Explain lender, loan buyer, servicer and mortgage-backed securities without conflating loan ownership with servicing.
**Visual production path and caveats:** Animate ownership/servicing flows, note transfers, borrower payment, pooling and investors with carefully labeled diagram.
**Source starting points to inspect (not pre-verified):**
FHFA/Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac and CFPB mortgage origination, servicing and securitization materials; actual servicing transfer notices.

### 6. How D.R. Horton Became a Homebuilding Giant — HM-I0002
**Viewer hook:** The business model behind the largest-volume U.S. homebuilder (verify relevant period).
**Investigative story:** Reconstruct the growth and economics of a major publicly traded homebuilder through land control, lot supply, production, customer incentives and capital.
**Visual production path and caveats:** Use homes/community footage, real SEC filings, controlled-versus-owned-lot diagrams and dated financials; avoid invented growth causes.
**Source starting points to inspect (not pre-verified):**
D.R. Horton SEC annual reports, investor presentations, land option and lot disclosures, Census homebuilder deliveries.

### 7. Why So Many New Apartments Are Marketed as “Luxury” — HM-I0011
**Viewer hook:** Why new rental developments disproportionately target higher rents and how economics shape marketing.
**Investigative story:** Test the role of land costs, project finance, construction, density, market demand and the ambiguity of the word luxury.
**Visual production path and caveats:** Visualize an apartment development budget and rent feasibility; contrast specific markets, sizes and years.
**Source starting points to inspect (not pre-verified):**
Census multifamily construction/cost data, municipal project feasibility records and local development pro formas where accessible.

### 8. Why Homebuilders Offer Mortgage Rates Banks Can't Match — HM-I0004
**Viewer hook:** How builders use finance incentives and who really pays.
**Investigative story:** Analyze builder rate buydowns, possible affiliated financing, advertised rate versus effective purchase price, and risks/tradeoffs for buyers.
**Visual production path and caveats:** Animate an illustrative monthly payment and buydown funding flow; compare like-for-like terms/APR and date-specific actual offers.
**Source starting points to inspect (not pre-verified):**
Public builder financing disclosure and homebuyer offer terms; CFPB/TRID loan estimates and APR/closing-cost materials.

### 9. Why Home Insurance Is Becoming a Housing Crisis — HM-I0009
**Viewer hook:** How insurance prices can change the real cost and availability of housing.
**Investigative story:** Trace claims severity/frequency, reinsurance, building costs, regulation and geographical concentration through the housing bill.
**Visual production path and caveats:** Use real damage footage, catastrophe/risk maps and premium/claims graphics with precise state/period and meaningful counterevidence.
**Source starting points to inspect (not pre-verified):**
State insurance regulator filings, NAIC reporting, catastrophe modeling reports (with limitations), state-specific premiums and claims data.

### 10. Who Gets Paid When You Buy a $500,000 House? — HM-I0007
**Viewer hook:** Follow the money at a familiar home purchase.
**Investigative story:** Trace buyer/seller funds, agent arrangements, lender, title/escrow, taxes and other documented closing expenses. $500,000 is hypothetical.
**Visual production path and caveats:** Build illustrated closing disclosure and a flow diagram of gross price versus buyer closing costs; do not double count payments or assume universal fee splits.
**Source starting points to inspect (not pre-verified):**
CFPB closing disclosure materials, settlement documents, state transfer-tax schedules and current brokerage contract/commission rules.

## Scope and deduplication rules
- All ten ranked titles point to **existing proposals**. Do not create a second idea if a title or rephrasing matches any of these IDs; attach alternate title as an alias and update the same idea instead.
- All remain **proposed**, without researched claims, script, approved narrator, assigned editor or producer confirmation.
- Titles that suggest universal conclusions ('banks can't match', 'housing crisis', 'luxury', 'Wall Street') are hooks to test against actual market, financial and legal records, not predetermined findings. Rewrite a title if the evidence does not warrant it.
- Distinguish from Hard Costs general physical construction and Built to Scale contractor-growth case studies. House Money must follow the residential asset, transaction or financing mechanism rather than repackaging those video concepts.
- The existing 14-concept House Money `PILOT_SLATE.md` still includes four additional proposed candidates (HM-I0010, HM-I0012–HM-I0014). This ten-topic slate is a **ranked subset**, not a replacement or ten new identifiers.
- Prospective Fiverr workflow is subject to explicit approval and terms: owner supplies ideas, producer can recommend an idea and draft research/script, owner reviews and approves the completed script **before production**, and owner approves the voice. Request a sample/pilot before a batch. Channel-wide approved director/evidence/QA standards apply.
