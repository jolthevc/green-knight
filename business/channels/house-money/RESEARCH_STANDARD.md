# House Money: evidence standard
Define the **home, residence, owner, property or financing instrument** in question, the geography and time period, and which of the seven housing-business mechanisms is central.

## Evidence map
1. **Baseline:** What does the average buyer/renter think happens? Which actors and contracts actually participate? Anchor terminology plainly.
2. **Transaction/operation:** Follow cash, property/title, contract rights, land, risk, accounting entries and counterparties. Identify who gets paid, when, out of what assets or fees, and who bears downside.
3. **Chronology:** In company case studies, reconstruct actual founding, pivots, expansions and market conditions using primary filings, annual reports, original disclosures, court records or reputable independent evidence.
4. **Compare:** Different states, land markets, rental types, interest rates, insurance conditions and building scales can yield different answers. Avoid treating a national average as a local universal.
5. **Challenge:** Test at least one serious competing explanation: land versus infrastructure versus zoning versus labor versus materials versus lender constraints versus demand. Correlation does not prove causation.
6. **Impacts:** Evaluate consequences for residents/tenants, builders/owners, lenders/insurers and taxpayers, including potential risks and the distribution of value.
7. **Quantification:** Distinguish sale price, build cost, land entitlement/lot cost, soft costs, builder margin, all-in monthly occupancy cost, mortgage principal/interest, rate vs APR, servicing fee, securitized balance, NOI/cash flow, yield, appraised/market value and equity.
8. **Sources:** Prioritize SEC filings, FHFA, CFPB, HUD, Census/ACS, BEA/BLS, local zoning/permit records, county assessors, municipal budgets, insurer/rate regulator filings and sourced operator documents. These are **source categories**, not claims that any page has been accessed. Record actual URLs and quote locators once research begins.
9. **Dates/legal:** Mortgage servicing regulation, broker commission arrangements, insurance rules, HOA law, zoning and agency programs change by jurisdiction/time. Recheck at script and publish stages. Do not present broad tax, mortgage or legal advice.

## Financial visualization and scenario discipline
- Build one transparent *example* home/loan/rent/development budget when useful; declare assumptions on screen and aloud.
- Do not blend a home's sale price, mortgage principal and lifetime payments into one supposed cost pool or count a transfer twice.
- Do not infer company-specific purchase fees, EBITDA, net worth, default rates or land ownership from industry examples.
- Verify each corporate number by period, entity and accounting definition; a homebuilder's controlled lots are not all owned land.
- When portraying policy, distinguish the intended objective from observed effects, and consider contrary evidence and distributional winners/losers.
- Do not assert every rental investor harms or helps affordability; actual empirical context must be supported.

**Research gate:** credible original evidence, real pivotal question, explainable money mechanism, honest countercase, feasible legal visual assets, and an expert-level insight distinct from Hard Costs, Pretty Penny, Money Moves, On the Map and Fallen Angels.
