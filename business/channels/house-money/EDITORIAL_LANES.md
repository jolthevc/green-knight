# House Money: seven documentary pillars and narrative openings
**These are idea territories, not a schedule or strict quotas.** Every film needs a person/home/customer, an economic mystery, real evidence, a consequential choice or reveal and a non-obvious final insight.

## 1. The business of building homes
**Question:** How do land, lot development, construction, incentives and capital become a saleable home — and who takes the risk?
- How D.R. Horton Makes Money Building Houses (public-company case; verify each claim).
- Why So Few Starter Homes Get Built (historical and regional qualifications required).
- Why Homebuilders Don't Always Own Their Land (options, controlled lots and risk).
- How a Neighborhood Is Financed Before the First House Is Sold.

## 2. Mortgages and housing finance
**Question:** How does a home loan become an asset owned by someone else and serviced by yet another entity?
- What Happens to Your Mortgage After Closing?
- Why Homebuilders Offer Mortgage Rate Buydowns (incentive/effective price).
- Who Actually Profits From Servicing Your Mortgage?
- What Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac Actually Do (market structures and legal/regulatory changes by date).

## 3. Renting and landlords
**Question:** How do property operations and financing convert a dwelling into a rental business?
- The Business of America's Mobile Home Parks (land vs structures; resident consequences).
- How Institutional Investors Run Scattered Rental Homes (scale and limits).
- Why Apartment Owners Care About Vacancy More Than Rent Headlines (test conditions).
- How a Rental Property Makes Money — and Where the Cash Really Goes.

## 4. Buying and selling homes
**Question:** Who receives a dollar of transaction spending, and which institutions are actually necessary?
- Who Gets Paid When You Buy a $500,000 House? (clearly illustrative purchase price).
- How Zillow Makes Money Without Usually Buying the Homes It Lists (specify segment and historical iBuying exceptions).
- Why Title Insurance Works So Differently From Other Insurance.
- The Business Behind Real Estate Commissions (avoid outdated assumptions about rules).

## 5. The cost of owning
**Question:** Why does the annual cost of occupying a home keep moving after purchase?
- Why Home Insurance Has Become More Expensive in Some Markets (geography/period/risk).
- The Hidden Business of Homeowners Associations.
- Who Makes Money When a House Needs Repairs?
- Why Property Taxes Don't Move Exactly Like Home Prices (jurisdiction by jurisdiction).

## 6. Land, permission and supply
**Question:** Why does adding homes often cost so much time and infrastructure capital?
- Why Building a Neighborhood Takes Years (residential master plan, entitlements, lot delivery).
- Why So Many New Apartments Are Marketed as Luxury (segment, land cost, financing, regulatory and demand).
- Why a Cheap Plot of Land May Be Expensive to Build On (differentiate HC-I0006 general infrastructure).
- How Sewer and Power Capacity Shape Where Homes Are Built.

## 7. New housing models
**Question:** Which innovation actually makes housing cheaper or better, and what stops it from scaling?
- Why Factory-Built Homes Haven't Taken Over America (definitions and market share essential).
- Can Homebuilding Work Like an Auto Factory? (cost and customization economics).
- Why Build-to-Rent Communities Became an Investment Strategy (verify period and scale).
- Can a New Way to Finance a Home Change Who Can Buy One? (underwriting/consumer safeguards).

## Five signature film engines
1. **Follow the Money:** animated end-to-end transaction or recurring payment with each real party labeled.
2. **The Housing Paradox:** a widely felt contradiction with multiple testable hypotheses.
3. **The Hidden Business:** unit economics or asset economics of an overlooked sector.
4. **The Housing Empire:** source-grounded company history and financing strategy.
5. **The Big Bet:** a decision involving land, leverage, underwriting, rents, rates or new production methods.

**Avoid filler:** A mortgage-securitization film must explain what happens to a homeowner's payment, not simply introduce acronyms. An apartment luxury story must show what features of development costs and market segmentation really influence new supply, not declare every developer greedy. Every ending should reveal *why the system persists, for whom it works and under what circumstances it changes*.
