# Behind Everything: narrator and sentence craft

## Sound
Premium, conversational documentary narrator with immediate warmth, restrained wonder and sharp confident delivery. Natural American English is a *working audition preference*, not an approved final voice identity. Neither monotonous TTS nor a performative hype voice. Curious, wry, sometimes playful; the excitement belongs to the real story.

## Sentence style
- Start with concrete, vivid speech: "You hand a jacket back to the store. What happens to it next?"
- Write for the ear. One image or causal fact per sentence; use money figures sparingly with plain-language comparisons.
- Use 'you' only when it makes the scene tangible, not as a repetitive rhetorical tic.
- Vary pace, sentence length and emotional temperature. Reserve silence for genuine consequence.
- Define jargon via the scene before naming it. Avoid generic "In today's fast-paced world", "little did they know", "here's where things get interesting", "But the truth is...", "Let's dive in" and rhetorical-question chains.
- Humor is welcome when it grows from an actual irony; never demean individuals.

## Narration direction workflow
Use the full approved spoken script verbatim in the separate narration director. Mark non-spoken performance instructions and take variation when helpful; no invented script copy. Audition a 60-90 second scene with short and long lines, surprising revelation and a quieter explanatory passage. Record voice profile/version/actual WPM only after testing. Owner must approve final actual voice and full spoken script before production.

## Ending sound
Stop with a specific image or reversal of the opening, not a meandering business-school moral or repeated subscribe plea.
