# Behind Everything: channel constitution

## Core idea
**Make the familiar fascinating.** Every film begins with a thing almost anyone recognizes: an airport, cruise ship, retail store, hotel, bottle of water, return package, stadium or everyday ritual. The film asks a specific question with a surprising, legitimate answer.

Our hero is often an everyday object, the consumer, a place, a decision or a business model. **The story is about how the thing actually works, not a list of facts about it.**

## Four promises
- **Click:** A recognizably relevant subject plus a specific contradiction the viewer wants resolved right now.
- **Stay:** Each chapter changes our understanding. Answer a small question, reveal a harder one, escalate evidence and consequences.
- **Finish:** Resolve the title/thumbnail question specifically, fairly and memorably.
- **Return:** Every episode delivers the same pleasurable experience of discovering an unexpected system behind normal life.

## Audience
Primary: curious mainstream adult, including people with no knowledge of finance, business or the specific industry. Secondary: an informed viewer who wants evidence and an insight more sophisticated than popular myths. Do not assume accounting, industry or historical knowledge.

## Main territories
1. **The hidden transaction:** where money goes, who pays, who benefits, and why an everyday purchase feels odd.
2. **Designed behavior:** stores, queues, hotels, apps, products and experiences shaped by deliberate choices and constraints.
3. **Impossible-seeming business:** a well-known company/product with an apparently contradictory business model or economics.
4. **Everyday empires:** how retail, logistics, entertainment and hospitality systems became pervasive.
5. **Cultural economics:** how weddings, sports, luxury, travel and entertainment became enormous and sometimes strange markets.
6. **Invisible infrastructure:** the behind-the-scenes network making a familiar experience possible.

These are editorial search areas, not on-screen chapter templates. A story can be a triumph, a weird compromise, a near miss, an industry equilibrium or a consumer paradox; it does not have to be a downfall.

## Distinction
This channel follows the **viewer experience and extraordinary underlying system**. Pretty Penny may follow a narrow transaction split; Money Moves a single corporate decision; Internet Famous internet virality; Fame & Fortune a star. Duplicate central questions must be routed elsewhere or materially reframed with related ID links.

## Brand personality
Intelligent, warm, casually confident, cinematic, witty when earned, never preachy. The narrator sounds like a brilliant friend telling you a story, not a professor or a clickbait announcer.

## Red lines
No vague business-school explanation, overstuffed data, isolated facts, brand promotion disguised as journalism, forced villains, unsupported psychology, fake 'secrets', implausible customer motive claims, manufactured suspense or sensationalism that the record cannot support.

## Launch operating hypothesis
Four 8-10 minute films per month, outsourced editing around $60 each. The editorial premise, packaging and script must be created/reviewed before editing. The model is a test, not a guarantee of video quality, reach, earnings or production capacity.
