# Topic greenlight: kill boring ideas before scripting

**Use before every commission.** Score privately 0-5 for each dimension, with a written reason and a real candidate thumbnail. This is editorial judgment, NOT a prediction or platform rule.

| Criterion | Weight | What 5/5 looks like |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Familiarity / universal relevance | 20 | Normal adult instantly recognizes subject and has a reason to care |
| Specific paradox and emotional tension | 20 | Truly consequential, crisp question without fabricated mystery |
| Honest title + phone-readable thumbnail | 15 | Instantly graspable, evocative visual-object contrast |
| Narrative engine / revelation depth | 15 | Several evidence-based developments that change our model |
| Distinct insight and share impulse | 10 | Surprising sourced takeaway a viewer can repeat |
| Visual feasibility at editing budget | 10 | Rights-aware archival/object footage, documents and basic charts can tell it |
| Evergreen / topical runway | 5 | Still relevant months later, unless an event story is intentional |
| Evidentiary feasibility | 5 | Accessible credible primary evidence and opposing interpretations |
| **Total** | **100** | Weighted sum: score/5 * criterion weight |

**Provisional commissioning threshold:** 80/100, plus no score below 3/5 for familiarity, paradox, narrative depth, or evidentiary feasibility. A hypothetical 90 is not evidence of market demand. Below threshold: REFRAME or REJECT, not 'make the title louder'.

### Gate A: Before full research
Write exact familiar subject; a plain-English uncomfortable question; concrete stakes; three competing package hypotheses; two 0-15s cold opens; strongest visual; early reveal; deeper midfilm complication; a possible ending; potential primary sources; distinguishing mechanism vs already registered episodes; editing-cost/rights red flags.

### Gate B: After evidence collection
Verify the strongest title-worthy claim with sources; distinguish observations from intentions and correlations from causation. Find the best counterargument. If research destroys the hook, *change the topic or promise* rather than fudge it.

### Gate C: Before editor handoff
A skeptical cold reader can recount what happened, why to stay and what was newly learned. The weakest minute has been rewritten. The film's concluding answer directly fulfills the chosen package. Fact and rights checks completed, owner script/voice approvals recorded.

### Launch-batch composition
Four initially proposed top-potential videos should test *different viewer entrypoints*, not manufacture a balanced set of mediocre topics. One familiar retail object, one aspirational travel/leisure experience, one relatable consumer frustration, one everyday macro curiosity is a working portfolio hypothesis. No topic is 'selected' until owner approves it.
