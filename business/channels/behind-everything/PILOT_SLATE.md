# Behind Everything: 30 audience-first episode concepts

**Created 2026-10-19. All are PROPOSED and UNRESEARCHED.** These are title, thumbnail and story hypotheses rather than verified assertions. No topic is owner-selected; no BE-V episode, contract, approved source log or published film exists.

## How to use this slate
Do not select a topic because it belongs to an expensive ad vertical or sounds educational. Demand familiar mass-market relevance, an unusually specific real mystery, documentary twists, a defensible nonobvious payoff, and an affordable real-world visual plan. Apply [TOPIC_GREENLIGHT.md](TOPIC_GREENLIGHT.md) and [CLICK_AND_HOOK_STANDARD.md](CLICK_AND_HOOK_STANDARD.md). Tier A is a subjective first-round priority, not a measured forecast.

## Recommended first four, still pending owner selection
1. **BE-I0001: Where Your Returned Clothes Really Go.** *Possible opening:* "You returned the jacket. You got your money. But the jacket's story isn't over." Track an individual returned item through possible real decisions, avoiding unsupported disposal statistics.
2. **BE-I0002: The Cruise Ship Isn't Just Selling You a Vacation.** *Possible opening:* "You buy a ticket to a floating vacation. But the moment you board, you're inside another business." Follow verified fare/onboard economics; never assert the fare is a loss leader without company evidence.
3. **BE-I0003: Why IKEA Makes You Walk Through Everything.** *Possible opening:* "You came here for a lamp. Why are you walking through an entire apartment?" Focus on physical customer routing; NOT MM-I0001 on assembly. Document actual formats and exceptions.
4. **BE-I0004: Why Every City Is Starting to Look the Same.** *Possible opening:* "Two different cities. So why are you seeing the same stores?" Explore chain retail and leasing and credible alternative explanations. Do not claim every city is identical.

## Minimum package before a full script
- Research and independently verify the shocking part, not just the basic setting.
- Develop 10 honest titles, 3 materially different thumbnail concepts, and two alternative 15-second openings.
- Identify first valuable reveal, stronger middle surprise, strongest alternative explanation, emotionally satisfying resolution, and a memorable closing callback.
- Pass the newcomer test and expert test. Demonstrate genuine narrative progression, not a chronology, slide lecture, or monotonous AI listicle.
- Assess source and rights feasibility, cost at roughly $60/edit, real footage/graphic plan and name overlap. Then owner selects and approves script/voice before the editor packet.
- After upload, inspect real first-30-second retention, scene-level dips, source-specific CTR and 7/28-day performance before claiming a formula works.

**Editorial law:** Entertainment is the invitation. Story earns the next minute. Evidence makes the surprising ending worth sharing. Never boring; never false.
