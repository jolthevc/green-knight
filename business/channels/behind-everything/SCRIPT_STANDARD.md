# Behind Everything: writing and retention standard

## Runtime and narration
- **8-10 finished minutes, 9 minutes target**, including pauses, visual holds and close. Prefer a sharp shorter script over stretched narration.
- Calibrate word count from the selected narrator's *observed* WPM and the actual render. Rough drafting reference: 1,050-1,350 spoken words at a hypothetical 135-155 WPM plus visual holds; not a pass/fail count or duration guarantee.
- Full script is audio-first: every sentence must be easy to understand at listening speed, with no screen needed to grasp the causal spine.

## Internal structural map (flexible)
A. Cold open: real object/scene + precise tension, no host greeting.
B. Concrete early reveal: a fact that proves the story is worth the click and introduces a deeper question.
C. Familiar reality: what the consumer thinks is happening, anchored in an understandable journey.
D. The machine underneath: follow actual decisions, cash, incentives, regulation, design or constraints.
E. Complication/reversal: the first explanation doesn't account for an important documented fact.
F. Real trade-off or challenge: strongest alternative explanation; who wins, who pays, what it costs.
G. Payoff: resolve the question with its boundary conditions, not a glib one-liner.
H. Closing callback: one vivid, specific re-interpretation of the opening object, and optional relevant next-watch invitation after value.

These are editorial purposes, **not** compulsory headings, a fixed act count or fictional drama. Not every story contains a villain or chronological conflict.

## Mandatory script preflight
- One-sentence title/thumbnail Promise Lock, with evidence anchor and forbidden implications.
- Three high-stakes factually plausible title/thumb pairs, two materially different first-15-second approaches.
- Full causal beat map: viewer belief BEFORE each movement; new scene/fact/action; revised belief AFTER; stakes; next question; evidence.
- Mark real first payoff, genuine strongest middle surprise, strongest counterargument and final callback.
- Source/claim log with date, units, geography, scope and disputed claims flagged.
- Budget-aware visual plan with actual findable objects and evidence, not endless expensive bespoke animation.

## Canonical spoken file and integration
Once selected and researched, `script.txt` begins with exactly `TITLE: <metadata.title>`, then one blank line and spoken paragraphs only. TITLE is metadata and not voiced. No stage directions, source brackets, timestamps, '[dramatic pause]', section headings or cue IDs inside clean narration. Maintain a separate non-spoken P001... paragraph map, evidence map, director readthrough and visual scene plan. Compute SHA-256 over the exact complete `script.txt` including header. A wording/title change invalidates old sidecars and requires synchronized updates.

## Beat-by-beat skeptic review
For every movement, record (1) audience question, (2) first-listen clarity, (3) factual change/reveal, (4) genuine interpretive novelty, (5) desire for next minute, (6) exact repair action. A line's poetry does not rescue missing momentum. A factually correct script that feels like a Wikipedia overview is returned, not passed.

## Editor packet
Only after source review and owner approval of the COMPLETE script and selected voice:
1. `01_SCRIPT.txt`: byte-identical clean canonical narration, non-spoken TITLE header.
2. `02_NARRATION_DIRECTOR.md`: all the narration copied **verbatim** by labeled non-spoken scene/segment, with human-readable, non-spoken pronunciation, speed, emphasis, pauses and emotional pacing.
3. `03_VISUAL_DIRECTOR.md`: ONE selected title, thumbnail, first frame, precise first 30 seconds, paragraph/segment-linked visual progression, evidence/rights status, on-screen text, maps/graphs, music and closing callback.

Integrate all three by matching paragraph IDs and exact speech before release. Full synthetic/human voice choice and final script require owner approval.
