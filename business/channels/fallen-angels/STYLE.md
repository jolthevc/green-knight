# Illustrated case-file identity

Warm paper, controlled shadows, illustrated business scenes and real evidence. Curious and cinematic, with enough light and clarity to stay approachable. This is a business investigation, so avoid horror imagery and constant ominous effects.

The illustrated case-file direction was discussed. This palette, type system and scene vocabulary are proposed implementations for the editor to test.

## Palette

| Color | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parchment | #F2EBDD | Main backgrounds and document panels |
| Charcoal | #242526 | Text, ink and essential contrast |
| Slate | #5B6670 | Supporting structures and neutral diagrams |
| Muted burgundy | #8A3D42 | Warning points, stress and key chronology |
| Aged brass | #B49A64 | Small evidence highlights |

Use charcoal on parchment for reading. Slate, burgundy and brass are restrained accents, not tiny low-contrast text. Label risk and change rather than relying on color alone. Usually one main accent per scene.

## Typography

Libre Baskerville Bold: short editorial titles and chapter cards.
IBM Plex Sans Regular/Medium: narration-related labels, captions and explanations.
IBM Plex Sans Bold: numeric emphasis and thumbnail text.
IBM Plex Mono Medium: dates, exhibit IDs and compact evidence labels.

Confirm availability and licenses in the production toolchain. If unavailable, propose a consistent comparable serif/sans pair. Keep body text large and sparse; inspect at phone size. Type choices remain pilot recommendations.

## Three visual layers

1. Illustrated scenes: storefronts, products, offices, warehouses and people simplified for an adult audience.
2. Miniature-style scenes: illustrated or cut-paper dioramas that make changing scale, empty space or organizational pressure visible. No complex 3D production is required.
3. Real evidence: verified headlines, filing excerpts, dated announcements and charts. Integrate into the paper layout while keeping readable source/date labels.

The subject determines the scene. An airline story should not look like every retail story. Preserve line treatment, palette, lighting and typography across different objects.

## Signature device: the case timeline

Build a clean timeline as the story progresses: advantage → pressure → warning → response → outcome. Each marker corresponds to supported events. Treat this as a navigational device, not proof of causality.

A companion “what changed?” panel can compare the working model with the damaged one. Highlight evidence selectively instead of filling a wall with strings and sticky notes.

## Motion and sound

Use subtle pans, parallax, a document reveal, a chart change or a physical object losing support. Animate mechanisms where it adds understanding. Let important numbers/documents hold long enough to read. Avoid fake camera shake, sirens and a dramatic sting at every turn.

Music supports narration with restraint. Let significant evidence register without an automatic swell. Voice intelligibility takes priority over atmosphere.

## Evidence integrity and brand distinction

Actual documents stay distinct from reconstructions. Label illustrative figures, representative scenes and recreated environments. Never generate a fake newspaper headline or court page and present it as evidence.

Do not invent documentary photos of real events. Maintain accurate dates, units, entity names and source labels as editable text overlays. A blank visual reconstruction can be useful without pretending it is a historical artifact.

Keep it distinct from Pretty Penny's bright economic illustrations and Money Moves' monochrome stick characters. Fallen Angels uses depth, paper layers and dated evidence, without making every scene dark.

## Pilot for editor feedback

Request one illustrated scene, one miniature scene, one real-source insert, a timeline, a title card and a thumbnail. Test an integrated 30–45 second sequence using selected narration and selective movement.

Invite pushback on cost and readability. The editor can simplify texture and animation while preserving the paper-based investigative identity. Neither a still reference nor an attractive style board proves finished-video quality or reach.
