# Fame & Fortune: creative contract

**Narrative fantasy:** Walk behind the red carpet, then follow the money into the contract, boardroom or commercial gamble nobody explains on celebrity TV.

**Emotional palette:** Recognition -> disbelief -> rising stakes -> power/control tension -> legitimate twist -> satisfying understanding. A real deal's overlooked terms are often more interesting than a manufactured scandal.

**Thumbnail-title-cold-open contract:** The star face and one conflict/number/object form a clear question. First frame proves the video is about that exact question. First 15 seconds introduce a specific extraordinary tension. By 30 to 45 seconds, give one credible, source-supported fact or concrete answer that increases the mystery, not a generic biography.

**Allowed hook families:** counterintuitive contrast ("The endorsement wasn't the fortune"); uncomfortable decision ("To own this, they first gave up..."); unequal bargain ("One party got the fame. Another got the rights"); near miss ("The deal almost failed because..."); reversal ("What looked like a loss changed their leverage"). All exact claims must be researched before final packaging.

**Prohibited hook families:** fake secret revelations; invented private conversations; "exposed" without evidence; unsupported "stole", "scammed" or "betrayed"; using paper wealth as bank-account cash; treating disputed valuations as verified exits; defaming a real person.

**Story motor:** The first apparent explanation is incomplete. At least one documented negotiation, constraint, contract structure, decision, market reaction or second-order consequence changes the answer. Bring stakes to life through evidence rather than exposition.

**Weak-middle killer:** No generic company history montage, wealth ranking, long early-life biography, a list of unrelated businesses, sponsor-like brand praise, or repetitive "and then they invested" scenes. Make the middle a connected chain of choices and consequences.

**Final payoff:** Resolve the precise opening tease, specify whose economics changed, identify what the star actually controlled, and finish with a sharp line that reinterprets the opening without overclaiming.

Read network POPCORN_STANDARD and the channel's CLICK_AND_HOOK_STANDARD. Every episode needs two candid verdicts: irresistible to a newcomer AND unexpectedly informative to a financially literate skeptic.
