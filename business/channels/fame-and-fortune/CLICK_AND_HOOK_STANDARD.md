# Fame & Fortune: click, hook and retention operating standard

## Audience-acquisition objective
We are selling an irresistible **truthful unanswered question** in a world a mainstream viewer already recognizes. Optimize for curiosity, concrete stakes, emotionally charged contrast and instantly recognizable subjects. The title is not a research abstract. The thumbnail is not a generic face collage. Never trade trust for a temporary click.

## Pre-research packaging: three candidates
For each proposed episode produce three distinct title-and-thumbnail hypotheses internally and assess:
- Recognizable star/brand on a phone?
- Can a non-business viewer explain the stakes in one breath?
- Is there one specific monetary, ownership or contractual surprise?
- Does the evidence promise a payoff that actually exists?
- Would a knowledgeable viewer discover a non-obvious mechanism?

Select one **after primary-source verification**, not because a false premise has higher click appeal. A/B testing is only claimed if truly performed.

## Title recipe
[Known person] + [unexpected action/outcome] + [unresolved question or meaningful stakes].
Strong archetypes (phrasing conditional on sourcing):
- "How [Star] Turned [Fame/Event] Into a Business Empire"
- "The Deal That Made [Star] Richer Than [Original Career]"
- "Why [Star] Gave Up [Valuable Thing] to Own [Another]"
- "[Star]'s [Brand] Looked Unstoppable. Then [Documented Event]."
- "The Hidden Economics of [Star]'s Biggest Bet"

Reject: "The INSANE Truth..." without a precise promise; vague "Net Worth Explained"; "How [star] became famous"; unfounded "Everyone Lied About..." or "secret" where public filings make the information widely known.

## Opening: 0:00 to 1:30
- **0:00 to 0:08 | Cold-impact line and image:** drop directly into a real conflict, impossible-looking contrast or documented high-stakes deal. No channel intro, "welcome back", "in this video", or throat-clearing.
- **0:08 to 0:25 | Stakes:** sharpen what they stood to gain/lose and what the viewer thinks happened.
- **0:25 to 0:45 | First earned reveal:** share a verified detail or partial answer immediately. No holding every answer hostage.
- **0:45 to 1:30 | Story promise and propulsion:** pose the larger question and move into the scene that explains it.

Write the first 15 seconds to work without context. Verify the exact title's promise gets paid off later.

## Long-form retention, default 15 minutes
- A recognizable person must confront a real business choice. Every major movement must change the stakes, apparent ownership, interpretation or causal explanation.
- Use open loops with honest, scheduled payoffs, not manufactured cliffhangers every minute.
- Keep biographies minimal: only background that becomes causal later. Make the central deal or problem understandable before technical terminology.
- A meaningful surprise is a new fact that changes belief, not a gratuitous SFX.
- At act endings, write a *next-question bridge*: "But that left one problem..." followed by a documented problem. No hollow "you won't believe what happens next".
- Show an early economic model and later complicate it. Make any numbers legible, dated and source-citable.
- If 15 minutes is unsupported by the actual story, trim toward 10. No filler to satisfy runtime.

## Mandatory greenlight tests
1. **Truthful curiosity:** title, thumbnail and opening can be defended by real sources.
2. **Emotional stakes:** there is a human/business consequence, not just a dollar recital.
3. **Concrete reveal in 45 seconds:** first quick payoff creates appetite for more.
4. **Two or more meaningful reversals or deepening discoveries** where the evidence permits, not an artificial surprise quota.
5. **Weakest-middle check:** identify the softest section and rewrite its actual story function.
6. **Specific closing revelation:** resolve the click promise and add a fresh, defensible explanation.
7. **Editorial ethics:** no unsourced allegations about living people, invented scenes, unlicensed or manipulated documentary assets.

## Editorial scoring (internal judgment, never fabricated analytics)
Rate 1 to 5 each: initial click premise, 15-second line, first-45-second payoff, protagonist/stakes, middle movement, expert insight, final payoff, factual support, editing feasibility. Any score below 4 on hook, payoff or evidence returns REVISE. Scores are judgment only; real performance must be learned from YouTube analytics.
