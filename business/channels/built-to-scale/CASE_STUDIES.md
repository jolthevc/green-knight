# Built to Scale: named-company growth research leads
This list is **not** a set of verified case studies, approved episode scripts, claimed interviews or proof of causation. Before selecting a company documentary, research public original records, dates, ownership history, primary-source acquisitions, financing and credible alternatives. If no unusual repeatable mechanism emerges, reject the company-led pitch despite a famous name.

## 1. Quanta Services — the decentralized acquisition thesis
**Question:** How does a group of specialty contractors become a major national platform while retaining operator expertise? Investigate original company formation, acquisition history, incentives, capital, centralized versus local decisions and examples where this approach worked or struggled. Beware of turning an acquisition count into proof of synergies. **Proposed:** BTS-I0020.

## 2. A1 Garage Door Service — turning a local service trade into a multi-market company
**Question:** What mix of sales, customer service, management, hiring, branding, branch growth and potential acquisitions enabled expansion? Verify founder chronology and reported scale by date and source; avoid relying only on founder promotional claims. **Proposed:** BTS-I0021.

## 3. Roto-Rooter — standardizing a service experience
**Question:** How did a specialized drain-cleaning service become recognizable across many communities? Investigate chronology, franchise/company-owned mix, brand, processes, technology, territory economics and quality control. Avoid attributing growth entirely to one invention. **Proposed:** BTS-I0022.

## 4. Comfort Systems USA — contractor platforms and commercial specialization
**Question:** How can mechanical contracting organizations build the expertise, capacity, leadership and capital structure to operate across markets and project sizes? Confirm financial statements, operating-unit structure, acquisitions, organic growth and adverse examples; explain why a local firm may not have those resources. **Proposed:** BTS-I0025.

## 5. Neighborly — scaling service brands through franchising and acquisition
**Question:** What does a franchisor actually scale: staff, consumer trust, systems, operators or geography? Reconstruct real acquisitions/franchise relationships, incentive alignment, unit economics, capital needs and where quality control or network costs become a burden. **Proposed:** BTS-I0026.

## Case-study research checklist
- Source-backed formation and initial business, first significant expansion decision and genuine timeline
- How customers were won and service quality preserved across scale
- Hiring, local managers, branch/franchise systems and owner decision rights
- Financing/ownership changes, where terms are public, with caveats about confidentiality
- Actual acquisitions and integration mechanics if applicable; no presumed synergies
- Real measured outcome and source period, not founder net worth inferred from revenue
- Risks, failed initiatives, competitors and inability of smaller companies to reproduce key advantages
- One practical, conditional operator lesson delivered through a watchable documentary

Where two titles seem like generic big-contractor histories, choose *different* mechanisms (customer acquisition vs capital structure vs franchise ownership vs acquisitions), else archive or reserve only one. Start with primary sources, not this lead sheet.
