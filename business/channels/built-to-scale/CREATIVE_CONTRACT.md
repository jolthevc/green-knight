# Built to Scale: creative contract
**Entertainment first:** We follow an ambitious person/business trying to expand and discover why the next step is harder than it appears. Hiring, customers, borrowing, debt service, management, deals and systems are compelling because the viewer sees a dream collide with a constraint.

**The four promises:** (1) accessible, watchable story; (2) documented or clearly modeled growth mechanics; (3) useful 101-level financial/operational understanding, without a lecture; (4) a specific distinction between an operator who can make that move and one who cannot.

**Start with an ambition:** the first $10M project, a second branch, enough repeat clients to buy another truck, a historic company leap, a loan that could allow a takeover. Numbers are tools for stakes, not all the stakes. Real growth might mean stronger systems, profitable market share, leadership, resilience, capacity or owner freedom.

**Story engine:** actual operator/company and initial stage -> enticing growth opportunity -> what customer/capital/people reality requires -> first decision and earned answer -> inevitable new growth constraint -> serious alternative or setback -> specific mechanism behind outcome -> what a similarly situated operator could and could not replicate.

**Case-study version:** real founder/builder, first customers/relationships, financing, hires, acquisition choices, management innovations, failed steps, documented outcome and sources. No mythology, unsupported personal wealth or hindsight storytelling.

**Playbook version:** anchored in actual operators, authentic prices/sources and clearly declared assumptions; compare a second crew, new branch, big bid, debt option or acquisition through transparent visuals. Do not present an invented company as real.

**Expert-newcomer dual test:** sophisticated contractors get real strategic nuance (bonding, surety, customer acquisition, management span, working capital, dilution or service density), and outsiders can follow the human choice on one listening. No management lecture, unearned “10 secrets,” fake quotes/documents, guaranteed growth or overwhelming deal jargon.
