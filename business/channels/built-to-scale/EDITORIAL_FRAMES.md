# Built to Scale: growth-playbook story engines
**Central question:** How can a small or mid-sized trade business become bigger, stronger and more valuable without coming apart? These engines yield documentaries, not lecture modules. One real company, decision or credible worked situation should give each episode its stakes.

## The six growth engines and potential hooks
### 1. Customers and commercial growth
- **The First 100 Customers:** build initial demand and referrals, understand acquisition cost and conversion.
- **The First Big Contract:** qualify, bid, bond, staff and finance a transformative commercial project.
- **Own Your Market:** service radius, dispatch density, local brand, customer reputation and multi-location demand.
- **The Recurring Customer Machine:** installations versus maintenance/service, upsell and installed-base economics.
- **The Customer Concentration Trap:** bigger customers may increase power and cash risk, not necessarily quality.

### 2. Hires, leadership and organization
- **The First Five Hires:** who actually makes the company less founder-dependent, and the real fixed costs.
- **The Great Delegation:** transition tradesperson -> working owner -> leader -> operator with managers.
- **Ten Crews, One Standard:** training, lead techs, quality systems, forecasting and ownership of outcomes.
- **The Manager You Cannot Afford NOT to Hire:** an evidenced inflection point when overhead enables scale rather than destroys it.
- **The Founder Trap:** owner works harder as revenue increases; why systems or authority fail.

### 3. Capital and financial engineering
- **Fund the Second Crew:** trucks/equipment and payroll financed before invoices are collected.
- **Debt or Equity?:** a true decision including dilution, loan covenants, underwriting and guarantees.
- **The Big Project's Hidden Balance Sheet:** bonding, cash cycle, retainage, suppliers, payroll and credit.
- **Buy vs Lease vs Subcontract:** equipment/fleet capacity, utilization and capital risk.
- **Growing Broke:** how rising sales can reduce available cash and which financing tools really address the mismatch.

### 4. Repeatable operations and unit economics
- **The 100-Truck Machine:** the dispatch, marketing, training, branch leadership and geographic density behind regional scale.
- **One Crew Becomes a Branch:** repeatable profitable unit -> supervisor -> operations system -> accountable location.
- **The Margin Engine:** job selection, pricing, change orders, labor/materials, callbacks and collection quality.
- **The Owner-Free Test:** when a business can deliver to customers if the owner disappears for a month.

### 5. Geographic expansion and acquisitions
- **The Second-City Decision:** starting from zero versus an acquisition, with financing and execution risk.
- **Your First Competitor Acquisition:** price, due diligence, customer retention, seller involvement and integration.
- **The Roll-Up That Actually Works:** what to standardize centrally versus keep local.
- **Franchise vs Company-Owned:** operators, incentives, unit support, quality and economics.
- **From Local Company to Regional Institution:** marketing, recruiting, working capital and multiple locations.

### 6. Growing without breaking
- **The $5M Ceiling:** management, systems, cash, labor and owner skills at a transition point; amount is illustrative until sourced.
- **The Contract That Could Sink You:** extraordinary opportunity that exceeds bonding, cash or delivery capacity.
- **The Growth Crash:** overexpansion, overhead, customer service decline and corrective decisions.
- **The Owner's Exit:** succession/ownership structures that preserve value and keep the business running.

## The four film franchises
1. **Growth Playbook:** structured, realistic stage-by-stage scaling challenge (not a listicle).
2. **Built From Nothing:** verified real operator/company history, including acquisitions and reversals.
3. **The Big Decision:** informed fork in the road; alternatives modeled with clear assumptions.
4. **Growing Pains:** what breaks on the way up; how some operators adapt or fail.

## Historical/company-led films
A Quanta Services, Comfort Systems USA, Roto-Rooter, A1 Garage Door Service or Neighborly episode must **begin with a genuine, sourceable turning point**. Reconstruct the financing, org design, market expansion or acquisition mechanics, compare plausible alternatives, show constraints and examine nontransferable advantages. Don't use a big brand as a substitute for a story. See CASE_STUDIES.md.

## Greenlight
Every episode should answer three things: **What was the growth goal? What exact bottleneck/choice mattered? What does the outcome actually teach under stated conditions?** If it's only advice, add a researched protagonist and jeopardy. If it's only a company biography, show the repeatable operating mechanism. If it's only money, show the customer/team/capacity mechanism behind the number.

Preserve older operational frames where they illuminate a **growth constraint**; standalone training, generalized workplace comparisons and narrow role-specific tutorials are not the core lineup.
