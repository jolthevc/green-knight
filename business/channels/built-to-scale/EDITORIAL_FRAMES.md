# Built to Scale editorial frames
**These are optional narrative engines, not quotas or instructional lesson plans.** Prefer broadly intriguing business puzzles, concrete decisions and real stakes. An intelligent viewer who has never owned a business should still want the next minute.

## I. How businesses grow
1. **The $100M Playbook:** correctly defined, evidenced scale and its financial/operational prerequisites.
2. **The Growth Blueprint:** company growth stages and what changes at each inflection.
3. **Built From Nothing / The First Million:** first customers and the real starting point of a successful company.
4. **The Inflection Point:** a documented decision that changed scale or trajectory.
5. **The Customer Machine:** acquiring, keeping and serving customers.
6. **The Recurring Revenue Machine:** maintenance, subscriptions, service agreements and repeat work.
7. **The Acquisition / Roll-Up Playbook:** financing, integration, control, cash and risk.
8. **The Owner's Exit:** sale, succession, real proceeds and ownership trade-offs.

## II. How great businesses operate
9. **The Way It Works:** one surprisingly powerful practice or process behind an exceptional firm.
10. **The Business X-Ray:** open up one credible unit, margin structure, or operating process.
11. **The Operating System:** dispatch, management, systems, estimating, quality and organizational depth.
12. **The Better Shop:** why some employers serve customers and workers unusually well.
13. **Inside the Job:** manage real delivery, dependencies, quality and coordination.
14. **The Crew / Productivity Puzzle:** why similar effort can create different outcomes.
15. **The Career-to-Competence Story:** real growth in skill and judgment, without niche foreman-training tutorials.

## III. The decisions that matter
16. **The Million-Dollar Decision:** two legitimate choices and trade-offs under uncertain constraints.
17. **The Pricing Decision:** selecting customers, saying no and finding an edge.
18. **The Trade-Off:** outsourcing/hiring, overtime/capacity, service/install, quality/speed, new branch/acquisition.
19. **If We Owned It:** evidence-grounded, explicitly hypothetical business decision simulations.
20. **The Deal Room:** a single real transaction or clearly modeled financial agreement.
21. **The Management Mistake:** a consequential organizational decision and alternative.

## IV. Why businesses succeed or fail
22. **The Tale of Two Operators:** defensible contrast with truly comparable operators or openly hypothetical scenarios.
23. **The Breaking Point:** when success creates new management or capital failures.
24. **The Turnaround:** proven recovery decisions and their actual costs.
25. **The Estimate Versus Reality:** plan versus job/customer conditions, with consequence.
26. **The Scheduling War:** dependencies, timing and what reliability really costs.
27. **The Staffing Problem:** retention, training, shortages and human capital.

## Broader, non-wealth story examples
- Why the Best Contractors Turn Down Work
- How Great Construction Companies Run Without Their Owners
- Why Some Construction Jobs Run Like Clockwork
- Why Some HVAC Companies Never Struggle to Find Customers (claim to verify)
- Why Some Companies Are So Much Better at Training People
- What Happens When a Contracting Company Gets Too Big?
- Why Two Equally Busy Crews Produce Different Results
- Why Good Businesses Sometimes Stop Growing
- Why the Best Companies Don't Always Hire the Most Experienced Workers (hypothesis, not established fact)

**Selection test:** If the title reads like a training presentation, substitute a real mystery, person, outcome or decision. If the conclusion is merely "hire good people," dig for a specific mechanism. If its only appeal is "$100 million," ask what makes the story entertaining without that figure.
