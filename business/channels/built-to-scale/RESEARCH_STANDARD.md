# Built to Scale: growth evidence standard
Research must establish **how** an actual trades business grew, what it required, what broke, what it cost and which lessons could generalize.

## Baseline for every topic
- Define business trade, residential/commercial mix, geographic market, date, customer type, ownership, stage and precise scale measure. $10M in contracts won is not $10M collected; 100 trucks is not 100 profitable crews.
- Reconstruct a stage-by-stage growth or decision timeline, with real first customers/contracts where possible, constraints **known at the time**, plausible alternatives, financing and subsequent outcomes.
- Find sourceable people/cases and actual documentary evidence: filings, case studies, sourced interviews, company/investor materials, records, business press, court records, association data and original trade documentation. Company self-report is not independent proof.
- Record sources and counterclaims at paragraph/scene level under shared evidence.json/claim rules. If a case cannot substantiate headline figures, revise title or reject.

## Growth mechanisms to investigate
**Customers:** lead sources, close rates, service areas, customers won/lost, recurring work, contracts, referrals, effective customer acquisition and concentration.

**People:** initial hiring sequence, salary/payroll and overhead, skill scarcity, production crews, management layers, training, service quality, turnover, delegation, founder dependence.

**Capital:** working capital timing, upfront labor/materials, invoicing and retainage, equipment debt/leases, revolvers, bank/other credit, surety/bonding, collateral, personal guarantees, interest/debt service, equity dilution, contingent financing, covenants and downside. No generic debt-is-good endorsement.

**Operations:** crew/service-unit contribution, schedule/dispatch, job costing, overhead, branch ramp losses, utilization, callbacks and quality.

**Acquisitions:** purchase/equity/enterprise values, seller involvement, financing sources and loan exposure, acquired customers/workforce, systems integration, centralization versus autonomy, lost customers, synergies and multiple expansion; never assume private deal values or successful integration.

**Outcomes:** revenue versus recurring sales, backlog versus cash, EBITDA versus distributable earnings, owner salary versus owner equity, site/branch count versus profitable sites, paper returns versus realized exits. Dates, inflation and geography matter.

## Company-history standard
Named leads (Quanta Services, A1 Garage Door Service, Comfort Systems USA, Roto-Rooter, Neighborly) require a chronology of *documented* decisions, contemporaneous sources, competitor explanations, failures/risks and the particular aspect an individual operator might adapt. An exceptional scale outcome does not prove each anecdotal decision caused it. Label unverifiable anecdotes and note survivor bias.

**Models:** all worked examples and assumed unit economics clearly disclosed verbally and on screen. Sensitivity-test bad acquisition, high interest rates, crew shortages, delayed collections, poor branch performance and competing growth priorities. Avoid individual legal/tax/bonding/lending advice.

A good research package reveals at least one actual decision, one surprising cost or bottleneck, one credible alternative and one limitation to the “playbook.”
