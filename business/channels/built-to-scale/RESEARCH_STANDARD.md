# Built to Scale research standard
Every episode must investigate **why a real decision, process, behavior or business difference matters**, and whether that explanation actually holds.

1. Define business type, location, period, customer mix, work type, outcome and the competing hypotheses.
2. Trace authentic businesses, available records, operator interviews actually conducted (if any), customer economics, workforce claims and primary/independent sources. Do not claim access to private P&Ls or internal dashboards.
3. Analyze a concrete operating unit when appropriate: lead -> sold job -> scheduled crew -> materials -> service delivery -> collections -> repeat customer. Specify pricing, capacity, labor, operating expenses, quality and cash timing.
4. Reconstruct decisions with options actually available at the time, cost of each, information then available, resulting consequences and other plausible explanations.
5. For a Tale of Two Operators, control for location, trade, company size, workforce, customer mix and time; correlation is not causation. If comparison is hypothetical, say so on screen and aloud.
6. For worker productivity, culture and training stories, take workers' perspective seriously. No unsubstantiated lazy-worker villain, medical/safety/legal advice or invented managerial testimony.
7. For choices, show the conditions under which the preferred choice would FAIL, including capital, customer availability, regulation, worker capacity and owner preferences.
8. Carefully distinguish revenue, recurring revenue, gross margin, operating income, cash, business enterprise value, equity and owner proceeds. $100M revenue is not $100M founder wealth.
9. Check original evidence, specific sections/locators, dates, permission to use photos/footage and counterevidence. Report research blockers rather than force a popular conclusion.

The most valuable outcome is a conditional, transferable **understanding**, not universal advice. Follow the shared evidence.json, source-and-claims, scene coverage and hash/review standards.
