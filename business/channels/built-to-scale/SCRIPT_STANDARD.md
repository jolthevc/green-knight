# Built to Scale script and approval standard
**Goal:** an accessible, high-engagement growth documentary that can serve as a lasting 101 to scaling a trade business **without sounding like a training course**. Follow the network's 8–10-minute configured target for now; the channel has not finalized an editor-recommended alternative duration.

A growth documentary follows an ambition, an operating/financing constraint, a choice and real consequences. Examples: getting 100 plumbing customers, opening an HVAC second branch, financing payroll before the GC pays, hiring the first general manager, or integrating a first acquisition. A human-protagonist or company-history episode must uncover the same financial/managerial mechanism rather than simply narrate dates.

**Film structure (adaptive):** vivid real-world initial situation -> specific growth goal -> first surprising obstacle -> operational/debt/customer mechanism -> new choice and alternate path -> consequence and counterargument -> paid-off ending that changes how the viewer understands scaling. Don't recite business advice or overfill charts. Always explain bond/interest/margin/equity concepts plainly at first appearance.

**Sourcing and numbers:** Every significant growth claim cites authentic evidence or is announced as an illustrative model. Say explicitly whether the target means revenue, backlog, collected cash, profit, truck count, branch count, enterprise value or owner's equity. Real company milestones and founders are never invented.

**Owner checkpoints for prospective external producer:** The owner may provide a shortlist and the producer choose a topic to propose. The producer may take primary responsibility for research, original draft script, voice candidates, visual plan and editing **subject to written scope and approvals**. The owner **must approve each complete script before recording/visual production** and **select/approve the voice** before it is used. Changed script requires renewed approval; factual/visual fixes also require QA. Read EDITOR_WORKFLOW.md. Owner approval does not turn unsourced claims into facts.

**Canonical Green Knight gates still govern any content committed as a selected or finished episode:** `script.txt` begins exact `TITLE: <metadata.title>`, blank line, canonical spoken body; SHA-256, claim/scene annotations, independent editorial review and integrated voice/visual direction required. Exactly `01_SCRIPT.txt`, `02_NARRATION_DIRECTOR.md`, `03_VISUAL_DIRECTOR.md` may be released in final editor-packet only after true evidence, script, performance, visual and approval checks. An external script proposal is not automatically a GitHub-approved story.
