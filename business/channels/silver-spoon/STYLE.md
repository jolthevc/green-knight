# Proposed illustrated dynasty identity

Refined editorial illustration with a warm porcelain canvas, dark blue ink, pewter accents and simple paper-cut depth. Approachable and lightly witty, with enough elegance to signal inheritance and long-lived ownership.

This is a pilot proposal. No inaccessible earlier image is treated as an exact approved reference.

## Palette

| Color | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Porcelain | #F5F2EA | Main background and light panels |
| Oxford blue | #243448 | Text, outlines and ownership diagrams |
| Pewter | #B5BEC2 | Secondary objects and supporting fills |
| Antique gold | #B69B67 | Selected asset, transfer or control point |
| Muted sage | #8E9C87 | Supporting family branch or scene detail |

Use Oxford blue on porcelain for reading. Pewter, gold and sage are accents, not small text colors. Verify rendered contrast and use labels/line styles alongside color to distinguish branches or rights.

Prefer airy light scenes to a uniformly dark boardroom. This identity should stay distinct from Changing Hands' midnight transaction scenes and Fallen Angels' investigative paper layers.

## Typography

Cormorant Garamond Semibold: short editorial title cards and chapter headings.
Manrope Regular/Medium: explanations, captions and diagram labels.
Manrope Bold: numbers and thumbnail text.
DM Mono Medium: optional dates and compact share/vote labels.

Confirm availability and licensing. Fine serif forms need generous size; do not use them for small chart labels. Keep the hierarchy consistent and inspect at phone size.

## Scene vocabulary

Simplified adult characters, framed family portraits, estates, storefronts, factories, ownership tiles, keys, ledgers and assets represented by recognizable objects.

The actual wealth source determines the scene. A manufacturing dynasty should show the business behind the fortune; do not default to yachts, mansions and champagne. A silver spoon can be a small brand motif, not compulsory imagery.

No permanent mascot or coat of arms is locked. Historical portraits/documents can be sourced evidence inserts; illustrative characters are symbolic stand-ins, not documentary likenesses.

## Signature device: the inheritance map

Build a simple family-and-ownership map through the story. Show only the relevant people, branches and assets. Separate:
- Family relationship.
- Economic interest.
- Voting/control rights.
- Operational leadership.

Label each connection; a family tree alone does not prove ownership. If evidence is incomplete, mark the unknown.

A key moving between generations can illustrate control, while separate asset tiles show wealth. This makes it possible to show a transfer of management without falsely implying a transfer of ownership.

## Motion and evidence

Use selective transitions, a portrait added to a branch, an asset divided, a key transferred or a voting block assembled. Give the viewer time to understand the map.

Real documents, verified ownership charts and historical images carry source/date information. Reconstructions are labeled. Never fabricate a will, trust document, family crest or official ownership register as proof.

Numbers remain editable overlays with units, scope and valuation date. A stylized wealth stack cannot imply a precise amount unsupported by evidence.

## Pilot and editor feedback

Request a 30–45 second integrated sequence with a character scene, an inheritance/control map and a sourced document insert. Include a title card and phone-size thumbnail preview.

Invite the editor to challenge expensive details and propose a simpler sustainable implementation. Keep line treatment, proportions, palette and typography stable while episode-specific props and scenes vary.
