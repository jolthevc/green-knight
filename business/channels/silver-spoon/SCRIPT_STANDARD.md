# Family fortune story: 8–10 minutes

Research the ownership and relationships first. Develop the thesis and internal outline, write the entire spoken draft in one pass, then review and revise internally.

## Narrative architecture

| Approximate window | Story job |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:25 | Recognizable fortune, surprising transfer or control puzzle |
| 0:25–1:30 | Establish the people, asset and first useful insight |
| 1:30–3:00 | Explain how the wealth was built and who held the relevant rights |
| 3:00–5:00 | Show inheritance, succession, division or structural defense |
| 5:00–7:00 | Follow the pressure, competing incentives and complication |
| 7:00–9:30 | Explain what changed, what remained and the specific insight |
| Final 5–20 seconds | Relevant next-video invitation when available |

Windows are planning aids. Finished runtime is 8–10 minutes including holds and closing. Select one clear transfer or bounded period rather than compressing an entire dynasty into a list of names.

## Hook and momentum

Prepare two or three supported hooks internally. Start with a concrete family-and-money puzzle a general viewer can follow. Do not invent an inheritance value, feud or private conversation.

The first 30 seconds must deliver the title/thumbnail promise. Give an early insight into who owns, controls or receives income. Use changing incentives and transfers to sustain curiosity rather than withholding the entire explanation.

Every beat advances understanding. A new heir or date alone is not progress. About every 30–60 seconds check whether the stakes, evidence or mechanism changed; this is an editorial diagnostic, not an official platform rule.

Avoid greetings, early subscription requests, mansion montages and long genealogies before establishing why the story matters.

## Mechanism and human stakes

Explain relationships only as needed to follow the wealth/control map. Show how a structure or decision changed rights, cash, incentives or continuity.

Distinguish economic ownership, voting control and management. Do not equate an inherited position with a personal fortune or a company's market value with cash available to the family.

Use actual evidence for conflict, stated intentions and outcomes. Do not invent motives, dialogue or private scenes. Present family accounts and later memoirs as perspectives.

Test the preferred explanation against resources, execution, outside competition and alternative causes. Preserve uncertainty about private assets and valuation.

## Spoken text and visuals

Concrete, varied speech with names and numbers that a narrator can pronounce. Explain terms through their consequences. Keep exact underlying values and legal details in the source map.

script.txt contains only spoken words. Headings, timestamps, citations and directions live in sidecars. Family/control diagrams must match the current wording and be legible without pausing the video.

## Ending and CTA

Resolve who gained or lost which rights, wealth or influence. State the supported lesson and its limits. Avoid a universal formula about heirs, an inevitable decline narrative or generic wealth advice.

One main CTA follows the payoff, usually an adjacent published story. Use a short channel invitation if none exists. No phantom end-screen links or stack of engagement requests.

## Internal review

Check relationships, ownership/control distinctions, valuation dates/methods, overlapping estimates, transfer status, private-information gaps, causal reasoning, counterevidence and tone.

Complete the shared package plus family/ownership map and transfer timeline in research/synthesis. Validate paragraph anchors and estimated timing. Actual finished duration is verified during production.
