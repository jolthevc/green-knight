# Green Knight: Editor Onboarding & Management Playbook
**Applies to every business channel and external editor, including Fiverr. Internal agent instructions.** Updated 2026-10-11. Read with EDITOR_HANDOFF_STANDARD.md, DIRECTOR_PACKET_REVIEW.md, POPCORN_STANDARD.md, PILOT_ACCEPTANCE.md, NARRATION_STANDARD.md and DELIVERY_QA_STANDARD.md. This is the **relationship and production-coordination protocol**, not a fourth editor-facing episode document.

## Purpose and core relationship
Green Knight supplies the fully researched, approved story, exact script, full narration direction, chosen title/thumbnail/first frame/visual storytelling and source constraints. The editor uses professional judgment to turn that direction into exceptionally engaging, high-quality video. They are a valued creative production partner, not a script researcher or a substitute for our editorial judgment. Encourage alternative execution techniques and improvements, but seek approval before changing spoken copy, substantive factual representations or the selected narrative/packaging concept.

**Editorial bar:** cinematic, popcorn-good, intelligible to newcomers, genuinely surprising and strategically illuminating to knowledgeable viewers. Editing is not a substitute for a dull script. Maintain a warm, practical, collaborative tone. Avoid excessive demands, corporate jargon and daily micromanagement. The owner is managing other responsibilities: **never say "other channels" in external outreach unless specifically instructed**.

## Agent commands
- **"Onboard a new editor for [CHANNEL]"**: read this file, PILOT_ACCEPTANCE.md, channel brand/style/voice and current candidate terms. Produce one friendly Fiverr introductory/qualification message from templates/editor/FIVERR_MESSAGES.md, an internal intake record using templates/editor/EDITOR_INTAKE_RECORD.md and a short list of genuine unknowns. Do not claim a booking, order, payment, account access or agreement.
- **"Negotiate with [EDITOR]" / "Reply to this Fiverr message"**: preserve already-agreed details, adjust only requested terms, ask no more than necessary. Keep the message short, kind and specific. Never fabricate rates, dates, revision entitlements, voice settings or scope acceptance.
- **"Start pilot"**: confirm actual contract/price, narrator/style/sample references and source permissions; send only the already-approved three-file episode packet. The pilot may be a paid short integrated test and/or full pilot under separately agreed terms. If already approved, do not restart it or force another audition.
- **"Send the ten-video batch"**: verify ten individually released, current, exact-version three-file packets and voice access first. Confirm contract, first five/ten timeline, milestones, revision process and communication rhythm. Do not pretend draft scripts are released.
- **"Review editor delivery" / "Review v2"**: use DELIVERY_QA_STANDARD.md; save timestamped internal QA with inspection limits, then draft ONE friendly prioritized revision message. Never send it without instruction or claim to have watched footage not actually available.

## Phase 1. Pre-contract qualification (minimum practical questions)
Get a specific answer and record confirmation/unknown:
1. **Fit and style:** Can editor execute our channel-specific illustrated/explanatory style, selective animation, source/document use, visual causal transformations, and intentional 0-30s hook, with realistic pricing? Ask for relevant actual prior work, not generic assurances. Editor may say a technique is not feasible; clarify alternatives.
2. **Pilot:** Will they do a 30-60 second integrated sample using a real passage, narrator, meaningful visual change, number and transition? Or an agreed full 8-10 minute pilot? Record pilot cost and whether credited toward batch. Pilot is a suggested first-stage validation, not a mandatory re-test after approved work.
3. **Scope and quote:** Record price **per episode**, total for five and ten, precise duration/quality, thumbnail, voice, captions, SEO, number/scope of included revisions, any additional fees or platform transaction terms. The five standard final deliverables below must be explicitly agreed, not assumed from generic Fiverr descriptions. Research/scriptwriting is supplied by us.
4. **Turnaround:** Ask editor to **propose** reasonable time for first five and all ten **including revisions**, starting after **all approved materials and voice access are received**, and expected initial-cut rhythm. Never invent a 3-day, 3-week or 8-week commitment. If editor says "first batch in three days," confirm whether that means five **first cuts** or **five finalized videos** and where revisions fit.
5. **Working rhythm:** Preferred owner operating model is **batch preparation of all ten complete packets**, delivered together when ready, while editor handles its production schedule. The owner needs time to prepare them; do not promise a particular send date. Editor need not be rushed. A two-step **five + five milestone** structure is a useful proposal, **not a default contractual fact**. First video sets alignment; subsequent videos can overlap after agreement. If an editor prefers one-at-a-time, negotiate without assuming our preference is their commitment.
6. **Rights and usage:** Confirm editor will use music, images, stock, templates, AI assets and fonts that are properly licensed for the intended YouTube use, will not fabricate genuine-looking documentary evidence, and can describe/remove any asset with unresolved rights. Confirm any third-party licensing limitations and whether AI-generated realistic material needs disclosure review. Do not request a huge asset manifest as a standard sixth deliverable.
7. **Tools/access:** Confirm whether editor can use the **owner-approved** ElevenLabs voice/model/settings, how they receive authorized access, and that they won't change voice identity without approval. Do not share credentials in GitHub or promise provider features/permissions without checking. Ask about agreed file transfer/delivery channel and ability to provide all five final outputs.

**Contract summary gate:** Before owner places a paid order, record confirmed scope, pricing, milestones/deadlines, included revision rounds, actual pilot arrangement, agreed files, narrator/access plan, rights expectations and communication/delivery channel. Mark any open point clearly. **Only the owner approves purchases, contractual acceptance and payment.**

## Phase 2. Onboarding and creative handoff
- Read channel's CHANNEL.md, STYLE.md, VOICE.md, CREATIVE_CONTRACT.md and the APPROVED voice/visual references, where they exist. Do not describe unselected drafts as approved identities.
- For each *editor-ready* episode share precisely three finalized files from `story/editor-packet/`:
  1. `01_SCRIPT.txt`: exact approved wording; first `TITLE:` header is not spoken.
  2. `02_NARRATION_DIRECTOR.md`: complete identical spoken copy plus clearly non-spoken delivery/pauses/pronunciation guidance; **do not paste stage directions into TTS**.
  3. `03_VISUAL_DIRECTOR.md`: **one** final title/thumbnail and selected opening, P/S scene changes and factual requirements, sonic pacing, final callback and meaningful execution latitude.
- Editor should follow editorial intent and retain room for practical illustration, composition, motion, editing and sound-design decisions. Suggest improvements before materially departing.
- Keep internal evidence, research audits, reviews, idea registry, alternate titles and private negotiations **out of `editor-packet/`**. Specific cleared/sourceable links required for the film may be in 03.
- Confirm editor has the latest version/hash of the three docs and the authorized narrator. If scripts change after dispatch, explicitly invalidate old packet and re-send approved version; don't silently mix editions.
- Review a short **integrated** voice+visual sample and/or the first complete pilot before scaling production; discuss brand continuity and quality. If this editor has an already finalized approved pilot, record that and proceed.

## Phase 3. Production communications
- Owner can batch send approved materials on a planned workday, then respond asynchronously; do not require daily new scripts or an hour-by-hour check-in.
- One accountable channel in the agreed Fiverr order/thread for briefs, deliveries, revision requests and confirmed changes. If other systems are used, log the exact relevant link without storing passwords.
- Define the **delivery schedule** as milestones and target draft cadence, not a fictional promise of daily delivery. For each milestone record which episodes, what `delivered` means (initial draft vs revision-complete/fully final), expected owner feedback window, and whether editor can work on multiple videos.
- Flag blocked assets/voice access, creative feasibility problems, rights concerns and timeline risks **early**, proposing solutions rather than silently changing facts, narration or required visuals. An editor should not start downstream work on unapproved creative forks.
- Keep change control fair: editorial changes **we introduce after packet approval** may require re-quotation; editor corrections for deviations from agreed instructions are not automatically "new scope." Follow the actual agreed revision terms.

## Phase 4. Deliverables and acceptance
**Five and only five default final deliverables for every 8-10-minute episode** (confirm in writing before order):
1. **Video:** finished mixed, playable 16:9 MP4 at **1920x1080 minimum**.
2. **Thumbnail:** separate 16:9 PNG/JPG, ideally **1280x720 or higher**, legible on phone.
3. **Clean narration:** separate final spoken track WAV or high-quality MP3, without music/SFX, matching final film.
4. **SEO text:** final proposed title, description, relevant tags, and **true clickable chapter timestamps taken from the final cut**, not guessed from script.
5. **Subtitles:** proofread, synchronized **SRT** file, not permanently burned narration captions.
**No default** request for project/source files, music stems, full rights spreadsheet, raw assets or other unrelated services. These can be separately scoped if useful.

On the first cut, mark unavailable final attachments PENDING, not falsely missing contract delivery. Actual final acceptance needs all five. Inspect what is truly accessible; no claim of full listening/watch without doing so.

## Phase 5. Feedback and revisions
- Run full DELIVERY_QA_STANDARD.md against approved 01/02/03. Distinguish **BLOCKER** (material wrong fact, wrong voice, missing major story element, corrupt cut), **MAJOR** (meaningful clarity/pacing/visual execution problem), **MINOR** (small measurable defect) and **SUGGESTION** (optional polish).
- Give ONE concise, respectful, **timecoded** revision message per cut, grouping issues and identifying required vs optional changes. Prioritize the biggest viewer-experience fixes and avoid overwhelming editor with speculative details. If the underlying script/approved direction is boring, return it to Green Knight for restructuring; do not attribute our editorial problem to editor.
- Editor revises according to the **confirmed** agreed process; note which revision rounds are included and approximate turnaround. On v2, explicitly test each earlier issue and check for regressions; don't add new demands arbitrarily.
- Approve the episode only when agreed fixes and final five outputs are verified. Then the owner makes any Fiverr acceptance/payment decision. Publication/YouTube uploading is a separate owner action.

## What to retain so no tribal knowledge is lost
Use templates/editor/EDITOR_INTAKE_RECORD.md **per editor relationship**; store only minimal non-sensitive operational facts. Record editor alias, channel(s), link to actual order, style references, pilot verdict, voice/access plan (NOT passwords), rate and contract scope, five-file agreement, revision policy, milestone clock, target dates, open issues, current status and last confirmation/source message. Preserve editor-specific exceptions. Keep personal account data, passwords, keys and platform payment details out of GitHub. A new agent must prefer the editor's current explicit messages/order over generic playbook defaults; when terms conflict, surface it for owner decision.
