# Creative Greenlight: from an interesting topic to a must-watch story

**GOVERNING AUDIENCE PRIORITY (2026-10-11):** Read [POPCORN_STANDARD.md](POPCORN_STANDARD.md). Within truthful/evidence constraints, **earned engagement is the primary creative objective**, with **mandatory newcomer *and* already-expert viewer value**. A precise but dull lecture or generic business takeaway is not publishable. Story beats must deliver genuine changing stakes, revealing strategic mechanisms and desire for the next minute. Critical review must actually reject/rework weak middle sections and generic insights; no empty claims of 'engaging.'

Effective 2026-10-11 for new ideas and future revisions. Internal editorial review, not a predictive algorithm. Read with NETWORK_CREATIVE_CHARTER.md, IDEATION.md, ENGAGEMENT_STANDARD.md, SCRIPT_REVIEW.md and the selected channel CREATIVE_CONTRACT.md.

## Gate 1: Premise worth clicking

Before deep research or a commissioned episode, write:
- **One-line human hook:** explain the surprising predicament to somebody unfamiliar with the industry, without specialist vocabulary.
- **Primary audience curiosity:** who beyond existing fans of the subject would care? Name a recognizable desire, anxiety, rivalry, conflict, transaction or object.
- **The irresistible question:** what precise answer will the viewer get? Can the viewer anticipate an answer and be surprised by the verified mechanism?
- **The change in understanding:** what counterintuitive but supportable discovery makes this more than an ordinary summary?
- **Three honest title candidates and two thumbnail messages**, materially different in angle, with the same underlying factual promise. Do not use fabricated numbers, overclaim control, or advertise an outcome the script does not substantiate.
- **First 20–30 seconds:** draft two substantially different openings. Identify the actual first payoff and why it deepens rather than exhausts curiosity.
- **Why now or why evergreen:** how can the topic sustain useful viewership? Record whether freshness creates a source-check obligation.
- **Proof path:** original documents, independent reporting, accessible facts, competing explanation, visuals obtainable without fabricated evidence, and a viable 8–10 minute boundary.
- **Network distinction:** why this belongs to the named channel, not another, and why it is not a close repeat of any registry item.
- **Share impulse:** what accurate one-sentence fact or twist might a delighted viewer repeat to a friend? This is a hypothesis, not a forecast.

Reject or reframe if the central question is merely a category, company biography, list of facts, industry briefing, vague history, technical curiosity with no stakes, repeated meme with no fresh evidence, or compelling thumbnail with no answer. A professional audience or lucrative advertiser does not rescue a weak premise.

## Gate 2: Research discovers a better story

Preserve two simultaneous drafts: (a) the factually accurate source-supported explanation and (b) the story a newcomer can follow and care about. Neither may undermine the other.

Answer:
1. What did viewers likely assume, and what does the evidence actually say?
2. Which named actor, customer, owner, competitor, object or place grounds the story?
3. What tangible decision, obligation, revelation, loss or constraint changes the direction?
4. What two or three original findings are stronger than the conventional version?
5. What is the most serious competing explanation or factual uncertainty?
6. Does the research *strengthen* the title promise or reveal the need for a new angle?
7. Can the entire mechanism be taught in three simple spoken sentences before layering details?

If the evidence does not sustain the hook, replace the hook or stop the story. Never reverse-engineer unsupported evidence to preserve an attractive package.

## Gate 3: Dramatic and explanatory outline

Write a short beat map with, for each beat: audience belief before; concrete action/evidence/turn; belief after; stakes/energy shift; reason for next beat; and which primary source supports it. Mark a cold-open image, first payoff, biggest legitimate reversal/complication, middle low point, climax of the *explanation*, and final callback. Some stories have no chronological climax: the major insight can be a revealed flow, relationship or contradiction.

Diagnose monotony: three consecutive paragraphs of exposition, a chain of dates without changing leverage, a dense number run, an uninterrupted caveat train, or repeated second-person hypothetical clauses. If found, restructure rather than adding music or zooms.

## Gate 4: Audio-first script approval

A cold reviewer applies SCRIPT_REVIEW.md and answers:
- Is the first 10 seconds about the subject, not about our knowledge of it?
- Does the first 30 seconds prove the title/thumbnail promise is real?
- Does a newcomer receive a substantive payoff in the first movement?
- Is there a clear person/object/system to follow without memorizing a cast?
- Does each middle section have a distinct causal job and changing stakes?
- Is the strongest real objection integrated at the moment it matters?
- Are figures, names, legal distinctions and hypotheticals intelligible while listening without a screen?
- Does the script sound natural read aloud, with memorable phrasing rather than generic AI transitions?
- Does the last minute resolve the opening question and leave an emotionally or intellectually satisfying afterimage?
- Could a knowledgeable skeptic distinguish facts, source-backed inference and models?
- Would a non-expert recommend the episode because it was a great story, not merely useful homework?

Verdicts: **GREENLIGHT**, **REVISE**, **RESEARCH/REFRAME**, or **REJECT**. No numerical score compensates for an unsupported central thesis, a misleading package or an unintelligible explanation. Written judgments must cite specific paragraphs or passages. Greenlight is an internal editorial decision, not an approved produced episode.

## Gate 5: Real production and publishing

The editor owns visual execution under EDITOR_HANDOFF_STANDARD.md. Review at least one actual integrated pilot per channel at phone and TV sizes. Do not mistake a style guide for an actual premium visual identity.

Full cut: hear the whole narration; test opening, middle and ending at normal speed; inspect real source overlays, motion, silence, color, mixing, readability, pacing, original contribution, asset rights and 8–10 minute measured runtime. No promise of monetization.

After publication: record actual metrics at comparable age and traffic mix, actual viewer feedback, where the audience became confused, and one falsifiable improvement experiment. Never invent analytics or claim that a hook was validated by a script-only review.

## Mandatory popcorn-good hard gates (highest creative priority)
Read POPCORN_STANDARD.md **before Gate 1**. For every recommended new idea, record BOTH **newcomer pull** (accessible predicament, immediate tension, first payoff) and **expert surprise** (what knowledgeable viewers have not already heard or how a new substantiated mechanism changes their interpretation). No viable expert delta? Research/REFRAME, do not compensate with a big figure or clickbait packaging.

Before scripting, the beat map must show a **belief or stakes change** at each major movement and a concrete reason to watch the next one; flag the **most boring plausible middle** and structurally repair it. Story must be exciting *because* a sophisticated causal/strategic insight unfolds, not despite an inserted MBA paragraph.

For Gate 4, write two explicit verdicts `NEWCOMER: PASS/REVISE/FAIL` and `EXPERT: PASS/REVISE/FAIL`, with precise anchors, plus `POPCORN-GOOD: GREENLIGHT/REVISE/RESEARCH-REFRAME/REJECT`. A generic conclusion, low-stakes chronology, repetitive obstacle list or a dull-but-accurate script **cannot GREENLIGHT**. Do not grant a pass for strong research, elegant writing, editor effects or sheer factual density. Rewriting should prioritize reordering discoveries, strengthening causality and testing fresh angles **before** polishing words. Never fabricate drama or novelty.

Gate 5 must audit the **actual viewer experience** at hook, worst middle stretch and ending (listen/watch; not just packet instructions). Record audience retention data only when genuinely available; simulated thoughts are not measured behavior.
