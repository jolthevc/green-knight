# Pilot and editor comparison

Use before ordering repeat batches. This is a review standard, not an automatically sent editor instruction. The owner makes the commissioning decision.

## Before full production

Agree the selected concept, clean script version and an 8–10 minute finished runtime. Commission a 30–45 second integrated sample containing the chosen voice, one explanatory visual, one important number and a transition. A still-image board alone does not demonstrate timing, narration or editing.

Record the approved versioned voice in channel narration.json, the approved visual reference/version in channel.json, and snapshots in story metadata. A style/voice approval does not approve an unseen finished video. Before repeat batches, review one full 8–10 minute pilot and its clean narration; compare early, middle and closing delivery, not just the best short clip.

## Compare editors fairly

Use comparable passage difficulty and the same deliverable brief. Score each 1–5 with specific timestamps, and keep the review in the story's qa.md.

| Criterion | What to inspect |
| --- | --- |
| Narration | Naturalness, pronunciation, restraint, no spoken cues, long-form consistency and retake joins |
| Comprehension | Does the visual make the mechanism/decision easier to understand? |
| Pacing | Purposeful momentum, room for numbers and important reveals |
| Identity | Stable characters, line weight, palette and typography |
| Fidelity | Exact script meaning, supported labels, no fake evidence |
| Finish | Phone-size readability, sound mix, caption placement if commissioned |
| Production economics | Sustainable cost, turnaround, agreed revisions and asset access |

Factual distortions, spoken stage directions or unreadable essential information require correction regardless of aggregate score. Views and SEO performance cannot be proven by a pilot.

## Quote before batch purchase

Record pilot price and 10-video package price separately, with exactly what is included: imagery, selective animation, narration, editing, thumbnail, metadata, captions, revisions and editable assets. Research and scripts are supplied by us. Ask the editor to propose cost-saving adjustments that preserve the intended result.

Confirm delivery cadence, revision rounds, asset usage rights and handover formats. No unconfirmed promise of ten videos for an earlier quoted price is treated as agreed scope.

## Finished pilot decision

Review the entire cut against qa.md and actual 480–600 second runtime.
Outcome: proceed / revise / change production approach / try another editor.
Record owner, date, exact approved versions, remaining corrections and quote.

Publishing is a separate action. A reviewed production sample cannot justify setting the story status to published.

## Test the learning experience

Alongside technical scoring, inspect whether a curious newcomer can follow the opening and money/control mechanism, whether each major beat earns continued interest, and whether the final insight changes understanding. Apply ENGAGEMENT_STANDARD.md. If feasible, obtain voluntary feedback from a real newcomer and an informed viewer: what did they learn, where did they become confused or want to stop, and what can they explain afterward? Record actual participant feedback and timestamps separately from internal editorial judgments. This is useful qualitative feedback, not proof of future retention or virality.

## Director-led treatment
Apply EDITOR_HANDOFF_STANDARD.md and the three reviewed instructions. Green Knight directs the opening, visual storytelling, thumbnail and voice performance. The editor may propose practical equivalent techniques but not replace the narrative concept unilaterally. Evaluate the actual rendered and audible result; a paper review does not substitute for real inspection.

## Creative and mass-audience acceptance

In addition to the technical scorecard, read [CREATIVE_GREENLIGHT.md](CREATIVE_GREENLIGHT.md) Gate 5 and the channel's `CREATIVE_CONTRACT.md`. Judge whether a previously uninterested but curious adult wants the next moment, whether the first minute delivers value, whether the middle genuinely changes stakes or understanding, whether the narration sounds like an exceptional documentary rather than generic explanatory content, and whether the ending resolves its exact promise. Compare the previewed phone-size title/thumbnail to the actual first 30 seconds. Editor visuals should enhance the story, not compensate for an incoherent script. Capture specific actual listener timestamps and reactions when such testing occurs, with no invented retention or audience response claims.

## Current director-led acceptance standard
The former editor-led visual treatment provision is superseded. Require three reviewed editor-packet instructions; judge actual title/thumbnail at phone size, 0–30-second cinematic visual opening, varied narration pace and breaths, organic chapter-like visual/sonic dividers, persistent visual metaphors, visual causality (not image per sentence), music and silence, factual asset integrity and ending callback. Commission a short integrated sample before repeat batches. Keep true rendering/listening evidence separate from predictions.

## Ongoing delivery acceptance
The current post-production acceptance protocol is [DELIVERY_QA_STANDARD.md](DELIVERY_QA_STANDARD.md), governing first cuts and revised videos for all channels. Exactly five final episode deliverables are standard: MP4, thumbnail, clean narration audio, SEO title/description/tags/accurate chapters, and synchronized SRT. On draft cuts missing final attachments may be pending, but final approval is blocked. Use honest media inspection logs and timecoded revisions.
