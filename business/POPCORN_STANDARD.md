# Popcorn-Good: the Green Knight engagement mandate
**Effective 2026-10-11. Governs the original eight business channels, Hard Costs as the ninth, Built to Scale as the tenth, House Money as the eleventh, new channel creation, idea selection, episodes, review/revise, direction and delivered-video QA.**

## The north star
**Make business documentaries so absorbing that people *want* the next minute, while the deepest economic or strategic insight makes the story more exciting rather than interrupting it.**

We are building **the dramatic pull of an exceptional thriller, the strategic insight of a top-tier investor/MBA case, and the immediate accessibility of the best YouTube storytelling**. These must reinforce one another. The audience should be entertained in the moment and substantially smarter afterward. Elegant prose, historical completeness, fact volume, exhaustive chronology, sophistication signaling and adherence to a template have **no independent value** when they reduce attention or comprehension.

**Creative priority:** Within non-negotiable factual/ethical constraints and the truthful title promise, maximize *earned viewer engagement and satisfaction*. A factually correct, thoroughly researched, beautifully written film that is boring **FAILS**. Likewise an exciting film with a generic 'product-market fit matters' takeaway or misleading claims **FAILS**. Accuracy is a floor; gripping story **and** differentiated explanatory depth are both required to pass.

## Two-viewer, one-film test (BOTH mandatory)
**Newcomer (no knowledge):** Can a smart stranger quickly grasp what is happening, why it matters and why to watch another minute? Does each explanation land on first listen, not by pausing or reading a chart? Never presume the viewer knows the company, jargon or outcome.

**Expert (already knows the famous story):** What *specific, verified or explicitly reasoned* thing changes an informed viewer's mind? It can be new primary evidence, unusually sharp synthesis, a counterintuitive mechanism, hidden incentive, original comparison, decision counterfactual with explicit assumptions, distribution of value, capital/ownership or a constraint/mitigant that changes the strategic conclusion. Merely repeating Wikipedia with better narration, inserting bigger numbers, saying 'network effects,' or attaching an MBA-sounding moral is **not** expert value.

An expert should feel they discovered a new lens; a newcomer should feel included in that discovery. If a topic is overcovered and no defensible differentiated insight emerges, **reframe, research further or reject** it. Do not assert originality merely because it sounds novel.

## The engagement engine
- Start with a vivid, honest, accessible **situation** (not an essay thesis, cast list or jargon).
- Pose a specific tension or question the viewer actively wants resolved; deliver a real early answer that makes a **deeper, better question** inevitable.
- Keep *changing* the story: credible decisions, consequences, reversals, obstacles, discoveries, conflicting evidence, revealed incentives and rising/falling stakes. The audience's interpretation after each meaningful movement differs from before. Known endings still permit gripping causality.
- Reveal the **strategy through the predicament**: show the actor's choices, constraints, alternatives, costs and resulting advantage/disadvantage; make the insight the turning point or payoff, not a lecture pasted after it.
- End by paying off the exact opening promise, then change its meaning through a specific, memorable, warranted insight. Aim for a line the viewer wants to tell a friend.
- Design narrator, images, evidence, silence, pacing and transitions together to **express the thought**. Motion is not engagement when nothing intellectually changes. Quiet, suspenseful or emotional moments can be more effective than rapid cuts.
- No compulsory cliffhanger spacing, arbitrary cut speed, bait-and-switch thumbnail, fake villains, invented numbers, forced jokes, breathless artificial narration or fabricated certainty. Never sacrifice truthful causality for a story beat.

## Good / bad example (illustrative, not purported historic narration)
**BAD Quibi:** 'It raised $1.75B, launched in a pandemic, struggled with mobile-only viewing and product-market fit, then failed. Lesson: distribution matters.' This is accurate-looking but predictable, chronology-driven and strategically shallow; the expert already knows everything.

**GOOD direction:** Open with Quibi introducing television apps immediately before announcing shutdown. Ask why a well-funded service was still fixing *where people could watch* when the deeper question was whether anyone would *pay to return*. Follow the conversion, acquisition and content-rights evidence as connected choices. Then use the Roku rights acquisition to distinguish **valuable content** from **a viable standalone paid platform**, while accurately identifying rights acquired and avoiding unsupported claims about revenue or permanence. A novice follows a real paradox; a veteran gets a sharp, bounded model of content production, distribution and customer ownership. The scene must *earn* that interpretation with evidence, not recite a slogan.

## Mandatory beat-by-beat audit
For the opening, each major movement and especially the weakest middle stretch, record:
1. **Audience question / tension:** What makes someone care *right now*?
2. **Newcomer comprehension:** What is understood on the first listen? Which term, name or number needs simplification?
3. **New, earned development:** What concrete event/evidence/action changes the narrative or viewer belief?
4. **Expert-level strategic delta:** What does a knowledgeable viewer now see differently? If none, which later payoff is it indispensably setting up?
5. **Next-minute desire:** Why would a viewer voluntarily continue? Avoid the empty answer 'to learn more.'
6. **Compression/repair:** Cut, combine, reorder, concretize, visualize, find a stronger source-backed angle, or replace filler. Specify the actual P/S anchors and changed wording/sequence.

No minute-by-minute surprise quotas. A connective passage may be needed; repeated passages with no narrative, explanatory or emotional purpose are not.

## Hard editorial decisions
**GREENLIGHT / editor-packet-ready:** Both viewer lenses pass; no dead opening or weak undiagnosed middle; distinct, causal business insight; compelling stakes and changes; the narrative works *by ear*; thumbnail promise is paid off; evidence is defensible; the visual/audio plan intensifies understanding and mood. Exact narrator selection remains a separate release prerequisite.

**REVISE:** Strong premise and evidence, but underpowered tension, obvious middle, generic conclusion or lecture sections. Edit *structure and causal revelation first*, then prose; rerun a fresh cold review.

**RESEARCH/REFRAME:** Topic has promise but cannot presently support a differentiated expert insight, honest stakes or earned twists.

**REJECT / DO NOT COMMISSION:** Even excellent evidence cannot yield a story someone would choose to watch, or the only hook requires misleading claims.

Require *two explicit written verdicts*, newcomer and informed expert, with specific reasons and anchors. A one-line 'very engaging' score, general compliments, literary polish, or an invented retention forecast is not QA.

## Evidence and learning
Treat predicted engagement as **editorial judgment**, not data. After publishing, analyze actual first-30-second retention, average viewed duration, section dips/replays, traffic mix and voluntary feedback, with attention to sample size and confounding. Use real findings to change hooks, presentation and underlying story selection. Do not pretend interviews or A/B tests happened. Factual integrity, fair treatment, evidence limits and trust remain non-negotiable.
