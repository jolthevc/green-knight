# Green Knight creative audit: network and six scripted pilots
Audit date 2026-10-11. Repository main snapshot: 95215ca54eb1a9f99314e6386a455b0cb4bce633. Scope: eight business channels, shared editorial standards and templates, repository idea registry, six canonical script.txt files, representative packaging, voice and visual-style standards. Other legacy channel directories are separate systems and were not reformatted.

## Executive verdict

**STRONG FOUNDATION, CREATIVE GREENLIGHT UPGRADE REQUIRED.** The eight lanes are meaningfully different and the source/claim/QA architecture is unusually disciplined. The risk is a portfolio of accurate and worthy explainers that fail to become must-watch entertainment. Improvements: earlier and more ruthless topic packaging, dramatic premise and stakes, midpoint momentum, warmer original voice, tighter audio-first exposition, distinctive emotional identities, and proof through finished pilots.

No actual audio, thumbnail, final edit or live YouTube analytics was available to inspect; no claim of viewer testing, virality, monetization eligibility or empirical retention can be made. Six scripts are marked scripted, not published. Six scripts sit in Changing Hands (two), Fool's Gold, Silver Spoon, First Billion and On the Map. Pretty Penny, Money Moves and Fallen Angels do not yet have canonical script.txt pilots in this snapshot. The ledger includes 45 ideas total, 39 proposed and six scripted. Review is editorial, not independent verification of every cited claim.

## Global findings ranked by leverage

**P0 creative strategy**: A good researchable topic is not always a mass-audience premise. Make strong one-sentence hook, title promise, everyday stake and changed understanding greenlight requirements before expensive research.

**P1 narrative vitality**: Mechanism-rich scripts sometimes proceed as consecutive descriptions instead of unfolding dramatic changes. Require distinctive beats, consequential constraints, real reversals and emotional variation; do not fake scenes or facts.

**P1 premium execution**: All eight have technical/identity rules; many art choices are pilots, First Billion and On the Map deliberately leave design to editors. A written identity is not a demonstrated premium final product. Commission and inspect integrated full-length examples.

**P1 channel distinctiveness**: Topic boundaries are strong but voice identity largely converges on warm/curious/precise at ~150 WPM. Distinguish emotional reward, scene rhythm, dominant story motor and permissible humor. Channel-specific CREATIVE_CONTRACT.md files now define these tests.

**P1 package before prose**: Final packaging exists, but idea-stage approval needs substantially different, source-supportable title/thumbnail promises and opening experiments before a full script.

**P1 audio economy**: Genuine rigor is at times overrepresented in narrated disclaimers, dates, percentages, titles and secondary actors. Move exhaustive proof to source files while preserving consequential nuance in natural speech.

**P2 empirical learning**: Readiness validators measure artifact completeness, not creative quality; actual comparable retention, first-listen comprehension and audience repeat behavior must be measured after pilot/launch.

## Script-specific cold-read notes

### DH-V0001: The Team That Made Millions Without Playing in the NBA
**Strength:** unusually strong universal paradox, clear contract entitlement, excellent closing 'The Spirits never made it into the NBA. Their contract did.' **Risk:** several paragraphs around fractional TV share, expansion protection and broadcast scope could become explanatory flat spots. The 2014 financing/control treatment is appropriately qualified, but its payoff should have maximum clarity. **Next:** cold-listen the midpoint and distinguish the surprising lifetime economics from the legal detail that must be retained. No automatic rewrite or invented closing evidence.

### DH-V0002: How Steve Jobs Became Disney's Biggest Individual Shareholder
**Strength:** celebrity and beloved film characters supply a broad entry; the twist is that Disney already had substantial film rights but needed Pixar's future creativity. **Risk:** the long negotiation chronology and dated exchanges can feel like a legal transaction recap before the stock-swap reveal. **Next:** foreground what Pixar was unable to do with its own characters and make the Jobs/Iger leverage shift the narrative driver; trim dates that do not change bargaining power. Confirm deal chronology against primary records before any substantive change.

### FG-V0001: The Man Who Rigged McDonald's Monopoly
**Strength:** perhaps the clearest high-appeal package in the batch; immediately familiar game, real ticket/illicit route distinction and strong investigative reveal. **Risk:** the final appellate-conviction nuance, while ethically necessary, may displace the human-scale sense of a broken game promise. **Next:** preserve legal distinctions, simplify how the appellate reasoning is introduced, and end on the exact customer trust violation. Avoid claiming McDonald's ran the scheme.

### SS-V0001: How the Gucci Family Lost Gucci
**Strength:** globally recognizable brand, extraordinary two-stage sale, name/ownership paradox and strong final image. **Risk:** too many family relationships, vote distinctions, transaction dates and competing narratives may slow the middle. **Next:** organize around Maurizio, the selling cousins and Investcorp's opposed incentives; use the actual shares as the story object. Keep ownership distinct from wealth and avoid gossip.

### FB-V0001: What Would It Take to Build a Billion-Dollar Storage Business?
**Strength:** unusual candor and excellent first-unit logic; avoids magical financing, handles dilution and real competition. **Risk:** repeated 'suppose/assume/might' financial modeling creates an extended conditional lecture with no actual protagonist; late leap from a tiny management fee to 180 large assets demands substantial listener effort. **Next:** frame as a participatory business-building challenge, make a small number of go/no-go moments emotionally and mathematically vivid, reduce narrated assumptions without removing necessary warnings. Preserve explicit hypothetical label and ownership/downside distinctions. Test two alternative packaging questions before any re-script.

### OTM-V0001: How a Dutch Town Became the World's Flower Marketplace
**Strength:** genuinely novel mechanism and strong ending: digital price agreement is not physical delivery. Research carefully distinguishes origin, persistence and costs. **Risk:** the 2026 cooperative technology decision and unfamiliar town name are cognitively demanding cold-open territory; middle can feel like a local-industry lecture. **Next:** begin from a single familiar rose and the international path, let the location emerge as a surprising answer, then introduce the cooperative and 2026 reversal only after the audience understands the logistical puzzle. Preserve caveat that many flowers bypass the town; never claim it controls all global flowers.

## Creative quality contrasts

The clearest broad-market premises currently are the defunct ABA team's TV payments, McDonald's Monopoly scheme, Gucci family exit and Jobs/Disney ownership twist. The most intellectually distinctive but riskier mass-market hooks are the Dutch flower marketplace and modeled self-storage empire. All six have useful research; the question is their theatrical/comprehension ceiling, not merely whether the subject is valuable.

## Limits and next empirical work

This audit does not revise canonical scripts, assign new story IDs, assert source verification, select unapproved voice/visual assets, claim publication or change the owner-approved production budget. Re-review each completed draft against the new gate before production, check current source facts after any substantial rewrite, inspect six full rendered pilots and gather actual outsider feedback and retention before claiming we have proven premium mass reach. Apply critique proportionally; preserve the uncommon rigor already achieved.
