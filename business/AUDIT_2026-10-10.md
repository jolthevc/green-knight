# Six-channel audit and fixes

Audited 2026-10-10 UTC against commit 0ac68f8b7c8299837195670212e517fe1b1541cc. Scope: all 72 business-workspace files, six channel definitions, shared workflow, templates, registry and validators. No finished scripts, approved voices or actual cuts were available. This is a system/content-direction audit, not a listening or audience-performance audit.

## Findings and implemented changes

| Area | Gap | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cross-channel handoff | Shared visual instructions assumed Pretty Penny's ink style | Handoff now references the selected channel and approved asset version |
| Voice identity | Episode fields existed but no canonical provider/model/settings/reference lock | Versioned narration.json for every channel, per-story snapshots and archive rules |
| Long-form voice quality | Short auditions and spot checks could miss drift/joins | Full-pilot review, full normal-speed narration listening, reference comparisons and take/retake log |
| Pronunciation | Notes existed without a repeatable verification/test process | Spoken forms, authoritative pronunciation leads, profile-version testing and separate correct caption spelling |
| Script identity | Matching opening anchors could miss changes later in a paragraph | Exact UTF-8 script hashes in metadata, cues, evidence and review records |
| Source/visual linkage | Markdown records were not checked for links or scene coverage | Canonical evidence.json, source/claim references, source access status and every-paragraph visual coverage |
| Stage readiness | Some template boilerplate and unsupported completion states could pass | Filled-artifact checks, internal editorial review, approved profile snapshots and final-cut review gates |
| Runtime | Estimated pace was treated too simply; short-concept guidance was ambiguous | Estimate limitations made explicit; actual cut governs and shorter work is not declared compliant |
| Idea generation | “Top three” conflicted with requests for one or two ideas | Up to three recommendations, never more than requested |
| Routing and persistence | Overlap/reframe behavior lacked a shared protocol | LANE_ROUTING.md; preserve original IDs/history and linked destination ideas |
| Repository integrity | Missing channel files/orphan story folders/non-finite timing were not caught | Six-channel completeness checks, orphan detection, stricter values and regression coverage |
| Upload decisions | Reusable styling/AI production could be confused with automatic eligibility | Current official originality/disclosure references and actual-asset review at upload |

## Each channel

| Channel | Main risk found | Specific improvement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pretty Penny | Too many numbers or a false sense of audited dollar allocation; an overly childish read | Listener must follow actors and money flow; simplify calculations and distinguish illustrative models |
| Money Moves | “One genius decision” framing and tonal hindsight certainty | Listener must understand real alternatives and trade-off; confidence does not erase uncertainty |
| Fallen Angels | Collapse tone can replace causality or make warnings seem predictive | Listener separates weakness, warning and trigger; restrained chronology without constant ominous emphasis |
| Changing Hands | Financial jargon and large-number delivery can obscure obligations | Listener can restate owned/owed positions and turning-point constraint; slower mechanism passages |
| Fool’s Gold | Theatrical intrigue can convert allegations into apparent fact | Listener separates apparent claims from established evidence; settled findings and humane consequences |
| Silver Spoon | Too many surnames and conflating wealth, votes and leadership | Listener can follow relevant branches and rights; use role-based reorientation and clear distinctions |

The lane definitions provide six distinct repeatable promises. Their real audience separation and breadth must be tested through episodes. Changing Hands remains a working name, not silently finalized.

## Open empirical decisions

Select actual pilot topics. Test short voice candidates, approve references and review full pilots. Confirm editor scope/price and sustainable turnaround. Link exact visual references and lock production versions after review. Use initial comparable uploads to learn packaging/retention rather than assume generic targets.

No code checker can establish that a source supports an argument, that a voice sounds natural, that a reviewer truly listened, or that a style attracts viewers. Those limits are explicit in the workflow.
