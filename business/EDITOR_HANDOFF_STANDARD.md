# Green Knight director-owned editor packet standard

**This document supersedes every older editor-owned visual-treatment and multi-file handoff rule throughout business/, channel instructions, templates and reviews.** All configured business channels apply it. For explicitly authorized producer-written pilots, the producer's first draft is an **input** to Green Knight's owner-approved final script and direction; the exact three-file director packet is the **final release**, not the initial paid research/scripting brief.

## Role division
Green Knight owns researched truth, exact narration, dramatic movements, title/thumbnail/click promise, visually choreographed first frame and 20–35 second cold open, recurrent imagery, transformations, shot intentions, on-screen editorial text, sound/breathing points, and factual constraints. The editor produces illustrations/assets, animation, voice takes, compositing, music mix and final film within agreed economics. Encourage editor execution improvements, but do not delegate construction of the documentary's core visual argument.

## Exactly three editor-facing documents
Only the final three documents belong in each video folder's `editor-packet/`:
- **01_SCRIPT.txt**: byte-identical to approved canonical `script.txt`, including non-spoken TITLE header. Narration body after first blank line is the single authoritative spoken copy, no cue markup.
- **02_NARRATION_DIRECTOR.md**: the COMPLETE unchanged narration body with clearly separate non-spoken directives and exact paragraph/sequence IDs, including phrasing, emphasis, varied speed, breath, numerical pronunciation, pauses, section-level dramatic intent and emotional restraint. Spoken text must equal 01 exactly. Never paste voice directions into TTS as spoken text; actual AI voices may need segmented takes, timing edits and supported controls.
- **03_VISUAL_DIRECTOR.md**: one selected title and one selected thumbnail concept with actual phone-size composition (alternatives are internal); thumbnail-title-first-frame-opening payoff alignment; one chosen beat-by-beat cold open (alternatives are internal); 4–6 flexible dramatic movements with narrator and image breathing points; roughly 6–9 connected visual sequences (flexible), persistent environments and recurring objects, transformations and meaningful comparison rather than literal noun-by-noun stills, documentary evidence/asset sourcing, exact key on-screen text, selective short emphasis cards, audio/music/silence cues, section dividers, final image callback and budget/production feasibility. The packet is a director's treatment, not fifty prescriptive asset prompts.

Never put a fourth file in editor-packet; internal research, evidence, metadata, packaging, QA, cue JSON, detailed reviews and editor-source history remain outside. Any concise editor-needed verified source links and usage limitations belong in 03. Video, thumbnail, clean audio, optional caption track and agreed editable sources are RETURN deliverables, not instruction documents.

## Narrative grammar and creative standard
Design the opening as its own miniature cinematic sequence, not a slow intro or logo. Thumbnail is a composed miniature poster, not a random still, and cannot misrepresent the story. Opening must quickly fulfill its click promise, supply an earned partial answer, and lead into escalating consequence.

Movements have distinct dramatic jobs and appropriately restrained transition beats. A pause can be a held composition, a music shift, silence, a return to an earlier image, or very brief editorial lettering. Avoid forced Chapter 1/2 cards, generic interstitials, or arbitrary timing quotas. Use the same illustrated universe as it changes on screen: accumulate, remove, transfer, contrast, reveal, return. Selective documentary evidence adds texture; fictional archival artifacts masquerading as genuine evidence are forbidden. Accurate selectable closed captions are good; default permanent full word-for-word subtitles are not. Short facts/figures/quotes must be accurate and readable on a phone.

## Review and revision: all three, every time
Read [DIRECTOR_PACKET_REVIEW.md](DIRECTOR_PACKET_REVIEW.md). Every user-requested review/revise/finalize call performs an independent pass on **script**, **narration direction**, **visual direction**, then a shared-film integration pass. If a file is still missing, report NOT READY and create/revise its working draft instead of silently approving. Cross-check same version/full-byte script SHA256, exact narrated words, paragraph and sequence IDs, promise/payoff, movement breaks, music/pause/image choreography and fact provenance. Record specific verdicts/fixes for 01, 02, 03 and combined integration, then re-review after revisions; keep reports in story `reviews/` and summary in `qa.md`. Do not claim actual listening without audio.

Script or title changes increment script_version and change SHA; resynchronize evidence/claims/cues/metadata, regenerate and re-review 02 and 03. Performance or visual-only edits preserve spoken hash but still require integration review. Previously scripted episodes are not assumed packet-ready. **Only produce the three final files in editor-packet/ after all revisions, no blockers and recorded integrated approval.** If a released upstream source changes, mark packet STALE in internal QA and regenerate/re-review before handing it to the editor. The existing registry statuses are unchanged absent actual stage transition; packet release is a separate gate.

## Initial execution test
Before commissioning a whole episode from an unproven editor, inspect a real 30–90-second integrated sample with narration pace, a visual transformation and movement transition. Then review the completed 8–10 minute cut, thumbnail, sound, runtime, captions and sourcing honestly. Do not invent view data, approved edits or licensing.

## Editor clarity and decision hierarchy (mandatory)
An editor packet is an **execution brief, not an ideation brainstorm**. Internally consider competing titles, thumbnail concepts, narration takes and visual cold opens; send the editor **one selected title, one selected thumbnail direction, one selected first-frame/opening concept and one recommended execution path**. An alternative may be included only if it is a specifically approved fallback with a clearly labeled trigger (e.g. asset unavailable). Do not ask the editor to choose among unresolved creative options. Do not include internal vote/rank discussion or unresolved script-review debates.

Prioritize each instruction:
- **MUST**: exact spoken script; correct names, numbers and documented events; approved voice/profile; selected packaging/first-frame promise; key visual causality; required segment transitions; rights and evidence boundaries.
- **SHOULD**: recommended scene treatments, asset reuse, pacing feel, suggested visual callbacks, sound texture and limited strategic on-screen lettering. Editor can propose a simpler equivalent that preserves the narrative function.
- **OPTIONAL**: implementation methods such as motion style, drawing technique, camera move, software, alternate prop styling. Do not turn these into arbitrary mandatory tasks.

One scene instruction must answer in plain English: **What is on screen? What changes? Why does it matter? Which exact paragraph(s) trigger it? What must not be shown incorrectly?** A useful format is `S03 | P010–P014 | Purpose | Start frame → transformation → end frame | Essential factual labels | Pause/transition | Editor discretion`. Minimize jargon such as “visual grammar”, “causal afterimage”, “belief state” and “shot intention” in the actual editor-facing file; translate to observable actions. The intended **effect** is mandatory, while a reasonable editor-proposed equivalent **technique** is welcome.

The three documents have explicit purposes. `01` is the definitive spoken script. `02` is the human narration direction and includes the unchanged entire script in a clearly labeled **SPOKEN COPY** section, with **NOT FOR TTS** cues in their own sections. `03` describes the chosen picture and sound approach and identifies *only necessary* exact screen text. If 02 and 03 refer to S03, they refer to the same passage and transition. Do not give contradictory pause durations, titles, asset labels or runtime targets. Where duration is an estimate, label it **approximate** rather than pretending cuts are already timed.

**Voice handoff**: specify actual approved narrator name/version, voice ID/model/settings or a plainly labeled `not yet approved` blocker. If using a shared ElevenLabs workspace, never assume access until verified; editor must confirm access to the correct voice and do a 30–60s pronunciation, pacing and emotional-range audition before full narration. Provide exact readings of difficult names and numbers, meaningful pause points, and instructions to produce separate takes / edit silence if model controls fail. Neither full-length monotonous speech nor artificial “dramatic” inflection on each line is acceptable. Voice model selection is not permission to change the narrator or text. Ask questions about unsupported voice-control techniques BEFORE generating the whole film.

**Asset handoff**: explicitly name the channel style/reference, important recurring scenes and objects, must-show authentic documents and their verified URL or owner-supplied asset location, optional references, required factual on-screen data with dates/units, and asset provenance/rights issues. Never write “use archival footage” without identifying what kind, whether it actually exists, and whether it is essential. If a requested exact asset is unavailable or expensive, ask or propose a compliant illustration; never fabricate a document, headline, person or statistic. The packet should be self-contained to execute without dozens of attachments or a pile of internal research documents.

**Transitions and sound**: distinguish a narration pause (voice stops), a visual hold (picture stays), and a music transition (sound bed changes). State which are needed at each major movement, while leaving frame-precise duration to editing and playback review. Not every narrative movement needs a visible card or dramatic silence. Short title cards and numbers only when they add clarity.

**Final pre-send editor test**: read only 01/02/03 as if unfamiliar with the research. Can the editor find the approved voice; know what to render and what not to read; identify first frame, first 30 seconds, main visual sequences, required numbers/assets and sound pauses; design thumbnail; handle missing assets; and submit the first sample without contacting us for conceptual direction? If not, simplify and repair the packet. Do not increase file count to solve ambiguity.

## Engagement and intellectual-payoff direction
Before releasing the three documents, read POPCORN_STANDARD.md. The selected opening/thumbnail must create immediate curiosity for strangers and avoid boring experts; the full 02 performance treatment must vary pace, warmth, tension, restraint, breath and silence **around actual revelations**; the 03 visual treatment must make economic mechanisms visibly transform through decisions/evidence and build new meaning, not use constant camera movement to disguise exposition. At every dramatic movement, ask what an already knowledgeable viewer learns *that reframes the familiar story* and what the first-timer intuitively follows. An immaculate packet with generic insights, repeated middle beats or an artificial voice is NOT release-ready. Editor discretion does not give permission to replace the narrative causal reveals with decorative animations.
