## 3. Creative Freedom

You are expected to bring visual judgment.

The production packet uses:

### REQUIRED
Do not materially change without flagging.

### PREFERRED
Our best current treatment. Improve it if you have a better solution.

### OPEN
Use your creative discretion within the channel style.

If an instruction looks weak, generic, or impossible to execute well, do not follow it mechanically.

Improve it or flag it.

---

## 4. Narration Production

If synthetic narration is in scope, generate it from the approved script using the locked channel voice.

You may regenerate passages and adjust delivery, pauses, spacing, and pacing to make the edit work naturally.

Do not change script wording, factual meaning, or narrator identity without approval.

Use the recorded channel model/settings as the baseline where applicable.

---

## 5. Quality Bar

The video should feel:

- intentional
- polished
- coherent
- visually researched
- trustworthy
- native to the channel

Avoid:

- generic filler stock
- cheap template motion
- random imagery
- obvious AI artifacts
- cluttered graphics
- visuals that merely illustrate nouns
- repetitive visual treatment

Specific real visuals are strongly preferred where they add credibility.

---

## 6. AI-Generated Imagery

[ALLOWED / LIMITED / NOT ALLOWED]

## 8. Deliverables

Expected:

- first cut
- revision cut(s)
- approved final master
- thumbnail if assigned
- relevant project/source files as specified
- relevant narration exports when synthetic narration is generated in production
- any missing-asset or rights flags

---

## 9. Flag Before Assuming

Flag issues involving:

- factual meaning
- unclear narration
- missing critical asset
- rights uncertainty
- misleading visual treatment
- major structural change
- package promise mismatch

Do not silently solve editorial problems by changing the story.

---

## 10. Final Reminder

Green Knight wants creative editors, not robots.

Use taste.

The goal is to make the strongest possible version of the product while preserving the story, evidence, and channel identity.
