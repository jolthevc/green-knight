# Voice and Writing Standard

**Status:** Canonical channel-template artifact  
**Purpose:** Define how the channel sounds, explains, entertains, and communicates.

---

## How to Use This File

This file should make the channel recognizable through language even without seeing the logo or thumbnail.

Do not define voice only with adjectives such as "smart," "funny," or "conversational."

Translate those adjectives into specific writing behavior.

Use examples and anti-examples liberally.

This file governs narration and written editorial voice. It does not replace packaging or visual standards.

---

## 1. Voice in One Paragraph

Describe the narrator or editorial voice as if briefing a highly capable writer who has never encountered the channel.

Include:

- intelligence level
- attitude toward the viewer
- energy
- warmth
- seriousness
- humor
- confidence
- curiosity
- emotional distance
- pacing

---

## 2. Relationship With the Viewer

How does the channel treat the audience?

Possible relationships include:

- knowledgeable friend
- curious investigator
- sharp explainer
- storyteller
- insider
- guide
- skeptical analyst
- enthusiastic enthusiast

Define what applies and what does not.

The channel should never sound patronizing.

---

## 3. Sophistication

Define the intended intellectual level.

Explain:

- how much terminology is acceptable
- when jargon must be translated
- how complex ideas should be simplified
- how to avoid dumbing down
- how to introduce unfamiliar concepts without breaking pacing

---

## 4. Sentence and Paragraph Style

Specify tendencies such as:

- average sentence length
- preference for active voice
- use of fragments
- rhetorical questions
- paragraph length
- transitions
- repetition
- emphasis
- cadence

Avoid turning these into rigid formulas.

---

## 5. Opening Style

Describe how videos should begin.

Cover:

- how quickly the core promise appears
- whether the opening should pose a question, make a claim, tell a scene, or establish a contradiction
- how much context is acceptable before the viewer understands the point
- what opening behaviors are prohibited

Include strong and weak examples.

---

## 6. Explanation Style

How should the narrator explain difficult material?

Define principles for:

- analogies
- examples
- definitions
- numbers
- timelines
- technical mechanisms
- causal chains
- uncertainty
- competing interpretations

---

## 7. Storytelling Style

Describe how narrative should work.

Consider:

- scenes versus exposition
- chronology
- suspense
- reveals
- callbacks
- escalation
- character
- conflict
- payoff

---

## 8. Humor

If humor is appropriate, define:

- frequency
- type
- intensity
- whether jokes can interrupt explanation
- whether sarcasm is acceptable
- whether humor should come from language, editing, observation, or subject matter

Also define humor that would feel wrong for the channel.

---

## 9. Use of Numbers and Data

Explain how statistics should be presented.

Possible rules:

- prefer comparisons viewers can intuit
- explain why a number matters
- avoid number dumps
- distinguish estimates from facts
- do not add false precision
- use scale analogies selectively

---

## 10. Confidence and Uncertainty

Define how the channel speaks when evidence is incomplete or contested.

The script should distinguish:

- established fact
- strong inference
- estimate
- disputed interpretation
- speculation

Confidence should come from clarity, not pretending uncertainty does not exist.

---

## 11. Language to Favor

List useful tendencies, phrases, structures, or linguistic qualities.

Do not turn this into a repetitive phrasebook.

---

## 12. Language to Avoid

Include channel-specific anti-patterns.

Potential examples:

- corporate filler
- generic AI transitions
- fake excitement
- "in today's fast-paced world"
- excessive rhetorical questions
- constant superlatives
- unnecessary moral judgments
- repetitive "but here's the thing" constructions
- unsupported certainty
- jargon used to signal intelligence

---

## 13. Strong Examples

Provide representative passages that sound exactly right.

Explain briefly why each works.

---

## 14. Anti-Examples

Provide passages that are technically competent but wrong for the channel.

Explain what makes them wrong.

Anti-examples are often more useful than additional adjectives.

---

## 15. Narration Considerations

If using synthetic or human voiceover, define the audible narrator identity as well as writing choices that improve spoken delivery.

For synthetic narration, this section should agree with `narration.yaml` and `NARRATION_CALIBRATION.md`. Green Knight selects and locks the narrator; the production editor generates and iterates delivery against picture.

Consider:

- narrator personality and audible register
- punctuation
- sentence length
- pronunciation
- acronyms
- numbers
- parenthetical information
- difficult proper nouns
- rhythm
- pauses
- delivery qualities to avoid

---

## Completion Standard

This document is ready when two capable writers using it independently would produce work that feels recognizably part of the same brand without sounding mechanically identical.
