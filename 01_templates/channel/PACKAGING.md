# Packaging Standard

**Status:** Canonical channel-template artifact  
**Purpose:** Define how the channel earns the right click through titles, thumbnails, and opening promises.

---

## How to Use This File

Packaging is part of the product.

A strong video that no one chooses to watch has failed to reach its audience.

This document should define the channel's packaging philosophy without reducing title and thumbnail creation to formulas.

The objective is to create truthful, compelling expectations and then fulfill them.

---

## 1. Packaging Philosophy

Explain what makes packaging compelling for this audience.

Define the balance among:

- curiosity
- clarity
- familiarity
- novelty
- stakes
- mystery
- specificity
- emotional intensity

---

## 2. The Viewer Question

Every strong package should trigger an immediate reason to click.

Define the types of questions or tensions this channel should create in the viewer's mind.

Examples:

- How can that possibly be true?
- Why does that familiar thing work this way?
- What happened next?
- Why would a company choose that?
- Who actually controls this?
- Why is this so expensive?

Use channel-specific patterns.

---

## 3. Title Principles

Define principles for:

- length
- clarity
- specificity
- recognizable subjects
- numbers
- proper nouns
- questions
- declarative titles
- negative framing
- superlatives
- emotional language
- technical terminology
- punctuation
- capitalization

Explain why each principle exists.

---

## 4. Thumbnail Principles

Define:

- visual simplicity
- focal point
- number of objects
- use of faces
- use of logos
- text
- contrast
- scale
- diagrams
- maps
- before/after imagery
- emotional expression
- abstraction versus literal depiction

Focus on the idea the thumbnail communicates, not just design mechanics.

---

## 5. Title and Thumbnail Relationship

The title and thumbnail should generally add information rather than duplicate one another.

Define how the pair should work together.

A useful package often creates one combined thought that neither element communicates as effectively alone.

---

## 6. Curiosity Without Misrepresentation

Define the boundary between compelling packaging and dishonest packaging.

Packaging may simplify or withhold information to create curiosity.

It may not promise a story, conclusion, magnitude, or claim the video cannot support.

---

## 7. Familiarity vs Novelty

Explain how the channel should use:

- recognizable brands
- familiar objects
- well-known people
- everyday experiences
- obscure but fascinating subjects

Document whether familiar anchors are preferred and how unfamiliar topics can be made accessible.

---

## 8. Packaging Development Process

Define the expected process for a video.

Example process:

1. identify the core viewer promise
2. identify the most compelling tension
3. generate diverse title directions
4. generate diverse thumbnail concepts
5. create title-thumbnail pairs
6. critique each pair for clarity, curiosity, fit, and truthfulness
7. select a small finalist set
8. verify the script actually fulfills the promise
9. choose final package

Channel-specific workflow details may be added later.

---

## 9. Strong Title Patterns

Document title patterns that fit the channel.

For each pattern:

- explain why it works
- provide examples
- state when not to use it

Do not turn patterns into mandatory templates.

---

## 10. Weak Title Patterns

Provide examples that are:

- too abstract
- too technical
- too generic
- too long
- too clever
- misleading
- repetitive
- dependent on context the viewer does not have

Explain why.

---

## 11. Strong Thumbnail Patterns

Provide examples or descriptions that fit.

Explain what the viewer understands in less than one second.

---

## 12. Weak Thumbnail Patterns

Document common failure modes such as:

- too many objects
- text overload
- no focal point
- merely illustrating the title
- generic stock imagery
- visual ideas that require explanation
- copying category conventions without differentiation

---

## 13. Packaging Anti-Patterns

List channel-specific behaviors to avoid.

---

## 14. Packaging Experiments

Record meaningful hypotheses worth testing.

Examples:

- recognizable subject vs category-level framing
- question vs declarative title
- one-object vs multi-object thumbnail
- number in title vs no number
- explicit mechanism vs withheld mechanism

Do not assume the result in advance.

---

## 15. Opening Promise Alignment

The first 30 to 60 seconds should rapidly confirm that the viewer clicked the correct video.

Define what must be established early to satisfy the package.

Packaging should influence the script, not merely decorate it after completion.

---

## Completion Standard

This document is ready when an AI or packaging specialist can generate many distinct options that feel native to the channel, and can explain why one pair is stronger than another without relying only on personal taste.
