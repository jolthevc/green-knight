# Channel Definition

**Status:** Canonical channel-template artifact  
**Purpose:** Define the identity, strategic thesis, viewer promise, and reason this channel deserves to exist.

---

## How to Use This File

Complete this document before a channel enters production.

This is the first channel-specific document a new AI, operator, or collaborator should read. It should provide enough context to understand the channel at a strategic level without requiring access to prior conversations.

This file should explain the channel clearly, but it should not duplicate the detailed audience, content, voice, packaging, visual, or production standards stored in the other channel files.

The goal is to create a durable definition of the property.

### One-Line Description
In one sentence, explain what the channel is.

A strong answer should communicate the subject, the distinctive framing, and the viewer experience.

**Template:**

> [Channel] is a [type of media property] that helps [audience] understand / experience / discover [subject] through [distinctive approach].

### Short Description
Write 2 to 4 sentences explaining the channel in plain language.

A person unfamiliar with the project should immediately understand what the channel makes and why someone might watch it.

## 2. Core Thesis

Explain the central belief behind the channel.

Why should this channel exist?

What audience behavior, curiosity, unmet need, entertainment demand, or content opportunity are we betting on?

What do we believe is true that creates an attractive opening for this property?

This section should distinguish between:

- the **market thesis**, meaning why there is room for the channel
- the **editorial thesis**, meaning why this specific creative approach should work

---

## 3. Viewer Promise

Define the recurring promise made to viewers.

The promise should be durable across individual videos.

It should answer:

> If I subscribe, what can I reliably expect to receive from this channel?

Avoid promises that are too generic, such as "interesting videos" or "high-quality information."

A useful viewer promise usually describes a repeatable intellectual, emotional, practical, or entertainment payoff.

---

## 4. Why Viewers Subscribe

Explain why a viewer who enjoyed one video should want more from this specific channel.

Consider:

- What recurring need is being satisfied?
- What type of curiosity is being rewarded?
- Is the viewer returning for subject matter, storytelling, perspective, entertainment, utility, identity, or some combination?
- What creates loyalty beyond a single successful topic?

---

## 5. Distinctive Advantage

Explain why this channel should be chosen over adjacent channels.

This does not need to be a conventional business moat.

Useful forms of differentiation can include:

- a distinct audience
- a different level of sophistication
- unusual subject selection
- a recognizable framing device
- better storytelling
- stronger visual explanation
- a particular emotional tone
- entertainment-first treatment of educational material
- deeper research
- faster pacing
- a broader or narrower content universe
- a distinctive combination of existing formats

State clearly what the channel is trying to own in the viewer's mind.

---

## 6. The Channel Experience

Describe how watching the channel should feel.

Consider:

- intellectual level
- emotional register
- pace
- energy
- seriousness
- humor
- density
- surprise
- accessibility
- sense of discovery

This section should describe the overall experience rather than line-level writing rules. Detailed language belongs in `VOICE.md`.

---

## 7. Core Content Engine

Explain the repeatable mechanism that can produce many strong videos.

A content engine is not simply a list of topics.

It should answer:

> What repeatable kind of question, story, tension, or discovery gives this channel an ongoing supply of compelling videos?

Examples of content engines might include:

- reveal the hidden economics behind familiar products
- explain why ordinary infrastructure works the way it does
- reconstruct strange historical decisions and their consequences
- investigate businesses whose apparent business model is not their real one
- show how familiar systems behave under extreme conditions

The final version should be specific to the channel.

---

## 8. Scope of the Opportunity

Summarize why the channel has room to become meaningfully large.

Consider:

- breadth of potential audience
- content supply
- expansion paths
- geographic reach
- evergreen potential
- adjacent subject matter
- monetization quality
- ability to create repeat viewing

Do not invent precision that does not exist. Separate evidence, estimates, and judgment.

Detailed channel evaluation may live in portfolio-level systems. This section should explain the strategic opportunity in narrative form.

---

## 9. What This Channel Is Not

Define important boundaries.

This prevents drift and helps future AI systems distinguish between adjacent ideas that technically fit the subject and ideas that actually fit the brand.

Include statements such as:

> We are not a news channel.

> We are not an investing-advice channel.

> We do not make generic listicles.

> We do not assume professional expertise.

Use channel-specific boundaries.

---

## 10. Success Definition

Describe what success means for this property.

Include both qualitative and quantitative dimensions where useful.

Examples:

- a clear and repeatable viewer proposition
- evidence that the channel can generate organic distribution
- a growing evergreen library
- attractive view economics
- strong retention relative to comparable content
- recurring audience behavior
- scalable production
- low dependence on any individual contractor
- multiple viable content pillars

Do not hard-code portfolio-wide kill or scale thresholds here unless they are genuinely channel-specific.

---

## 11. Core Strategic Risks

Identify the most important ways the thesis could be wrong.

Examples:

- audience is smaller than expected
- subject matter is interesting but visually weak
- topics perform individually but do not create subscribers
- production cost is too high
- channel becomes repetitive
- category monetizes poorly
- recognizable topics work but expansion topics do not
- audience expects a human personality
- packaging requires excessive sensationalism

The purpose is not pessimism. It is to make the initial thesis falsifiable.

---

## 12. Initial Hypotheses to Test

List the most important assumptions the early video slate should help test.

For each hypothesis, capture:

- **Hypothesis**
- **Why we believe it**
- **What evidence would support it**
- **What evidence would weaken it**

This section should make the first content slate informative, not merely productive.

---

## 13. Open Questions

Record meaningful unresolved questions that should not be silently converted into assumptions.

Delete questions when resolved and update the relevant canonical section.

---

## Completion Standard

This document is ready when a new reader can explain:

1. what the channel is
2. who it is for at a high level
3. why it deserves to exist
4. what recurring value it provides
5. what makes it distinctive
6. what kind of content engine sustains it
7. what it explicitly is not
8. what major assumptions the launch is testing

If those answers remain vague, the channel is not sufficiently defined.
