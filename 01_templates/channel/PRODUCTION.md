# Production Standard

**Status:** Canonical channel-template artifact  
**Purpose:** Define how this channel's episodes are manufactured and handed between AI systems and human production resources.

---

## How to Use This File

This document governs the practical production model.

It should make responsibilities explicit, reduce ambiguity for contractors, preserve replaceability, and establish a reliable quality baseline.

It should not duplicate the broader visual philosophy in `VISUAL_STYLE.md` or line-level writing guidance in `VOICE.md`.

---

## 1. Production Model

Describe the channel's intended production model.

Include:

- AI responsibilities
- human responsibilities
- expected contractor roles
- where judgment is required
- where execution should be mechanical

---

## 2. Target Format

Define:

- long-form or other format
- target runtime range
- target publishing cadence
- aspect ratio
- resolution
- frame rate if important
- narration style
- expected production complexity

---

## 3. Required Inputs Before Production

List everything the production team should receive before editing begins.

Typical inputs may include:

- approved script
- narration audio
- production packet
- source log
- visual references
- graphics requirements
- brand assets
- thumbnail brief
- pronunciation notes
- music guidance

Editing should not begin while critical upstream decisions remain unresolved.

---

## 4. Production Packet Expectations

Explain how detailed the production packet should be.

Define whether it should contain:

- timestamp or section
- narration block
- visual objective
- preferred footage
- alternative footage
- graphic instruction
- on-screen text
- source link
- pacing note
- transition note
- sound note

The packet should reduce low-value interpretation while leaving room for editorial taste.

---

## 5. Editor Responsibilities

Define what the editor owns.

Potential responsibilities include:

- sourcing appropriate legal assets
- assembling visuals
- pacing
- music
- sound design
- motion
- typography
- visual continuity
- basic fact-to-visual alignment
- flagging impossible or problematic instructions

---

## 6. Green Knight Responsibilities

Define what Green Knight provides.

Potential responsibilities include:

- approved thesis
- research
- script
- narration
- brand standards
- production packet
- key source materials
- required graphics
- final editorial approval

---

## 7. Thumbnail Production

Define who produces the thumbnail and what they receive.

Document whether thumbnail production occurs:

- before video edit
- during edit
- after first cut
- through a separate designer

---

## 8. Revision Process

Define:

- expected number of revision rounds
- what constitutes a revision
- how notes are delivered
- what changes require approval
- how scope changes are handled

The goal is predictable production, not rigid bureaucracy.

---

## 9. Quality Assurance

Before publication, verify at minimum:

- narration matches approved script
- visuals do not contradict narration
- factual graphics match source material
- names and numbers are correct
- no obvious spelling errors
- audio is balanced
- music does not overpower narration
- rights-sensitive assets are handled appropriately
- AI-generated material does not mislead
- title and thumbnail promise is fulfilled
- export settings are correct

Add channel-specific checks.

---

## 10. Asset and Rights Standards

Define approved asset sources and rights expectations.

Never assume an online image or video is free to use.

Document procedures for:

- stock libraries
- Creative Commons
- public domain
- screenshots
- news footage
- social posts
- music
- AI-generated assets
- logos
- copyrighted clips

Detailed portfolio-wide rights governance may live elsewhere.

---

## 11. File Naming and Delivery

Define naming conventions for:

- project file
- first cut
- revisions
- final export
- thumbnail
- captions
- audio
- source assets

Keep naming machine-readable where possible.

---

## 12. Source Files

Define which source files Green Knight expects to retain.

The goal is to preserve transferability if a contractor changes.

---

## 13. Handoff and Access

Define how contractors receive:

- files
- credentials
- permissions
- feedback
- deadlines

Prefer revocable access over ownership transfer.

---

## 14. Cost Expectations

Document the target cost range for this channel once known.

Track:

- editor cost
- thumbnail cost
- voice cost
- asset cost
- other variable cost
- fully loaded production cost

Do not sacrifice quality merely to hit an arbitrary benchmark.

---

## 15. Production Failure Modes

Examples:

- editor is forced to invent the story because the packet is weak
- generic stock footage dominates
- motion is excessive
- asset rights are unclear
- contractor retains critical project files
- revisions become endless because the brief was vague
- visual polish hides weak editorial content
- different editors make the channel feel like different brands

Add channel-specific risks.

---

## Completion Standard

This file is ready when a qualified production resource can understand exactly what Green Knight will provide, what they are responsible for, what quality means, and how final work is delivered.
