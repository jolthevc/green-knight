# Visual Style Standard

**Status:** Canonical channel-template artifact  
**Purpose:** Define the channel's visual language so different editors can produce work that still feels like one coherent brand.

---

## How to Use This File

This document should explain what the viewer sees and how visual storytelling supports the narration.

It is not merely a brand color guide.

A strong visual standard defines the relationship among footage, graphics, text, motion, pacing, and story.

The production system should preserve room for editors to exercise taste while ensuring the channel remains recognizable.

---

## 1. Visual Identity in One Paragraph

Describe the desired viewing experience.

Consider:

- premium vs raw
- cinematic vs informational
- playful vs restrained
- fast vs patient
- dense vs spacious
- documentary vs internet-native
- polished vs intentionally simple

---

## 2. Primary Visual Building Blocks

Define the expected mix of:

- licensed or stock footage
- archival footage
- still photography
- screenshots
- screen recordings
- maps
- diagrams
- charts
- typography
- motion graphics
- AI-generated visuals where appropriate
- original illustrations
- product imagery
- documents
- headlines
- data visualizations

Explain what each is good for.

---

## 3. B-Roll Philosophy

Define what makes B-roll useful.

Good B-roll should usually:

- clarify the narration
- establish place or scale
- create emotional tone
- provide evidence
- illustrate mechanism
- maintain visual momentum

Avoid footage that merely fills time without adding meaning.

---

## 4. Graphics and Explanation

Define when to use:

- diagrams
- animated arrows
- timelines
- maps
- charts
- labels
- callouts
- simplified mechanisms

Explain the desired level of complexity.

---

## 5. On-Screen Text

Define:

- frequency
- amount
- capitalization
- whether full sentences are acceptable
- use of numbers
- use of quotations
- source labels
- emphasis
- subtitles if relevant

---

## 6. Typography

Document preferred characteristics.

If exact fonts are not yet selected, describe the desired feeling and usage hierarchy.

---

## 7. Color and Brand Treatment

Define:

- core palette
- accent usage
- background tendencies
- chart treatment
- title-card treatment
- whether colors vary by content type

Do not let branding overwhelm editorial clarity.

---

## 8. Motion

Define the preferred motion language.

Consider:

- camera movement
- push-ins
- pans
- zooms
- parallax
- animated graphics
- transitions
- kinetic typography
- map movement

Explain what should feel energetic versus distracting.

---

## 9. Editing Pace

Describe pacing by section.

The opening may behave differently from explanatory sections, narrative sections, or emotional payoffs.

Avoid arbitrary rules such as "change visuals every three seconds" unless evidence genuinely supports them.

---

## 10. Music and Sound

Define:

- role of music
- emotional range
- use of sound effects
- intensity
- whether narration should ever compete with music
- transitions
- silence

---

## 11. Use of Faces and Human Presence

If relevant, define how people appear in a faceless channel.

Examples:

- archival interviews
- public figures
- photographs
- hands
- silhouette
- crowds
- documentary footage

---

## 12. Visual Evidence

For factual or investigative content, define how the video visually distinguishes:

- source evidence
- illustrative footage
- recreations
- AI-generated imagery
- speculation

The viewer should not be misled about what is real.

---

## 13. AI-Generated Visuals

Define whether they are allowed and under what circumstances.

Consider:

- disclosure
- realism
- historical reconstruction
- fictional scenes
- diagrams
- stylization
- consistency
- rights
- risk of misleading the viewer

---

## 14. Strong References

Include reference videos, frames, channels, films, shows, or other visual sources.

For each, explain what to learn from it.

Do not copy the reference wholesale.

---

## 15. Anti-References

Include examples of styles the channel should avoid.

Explain why.

---

## 16. Editor Judgment

State explicitly where editors are encouraged to improvise.

A visual standard should improve taste and consistency, not turn editing into assembly-line compliance.

---

## Completion Standard

This document is ready when a new editor can produce a first cut that feels directionally on-brand without requiring the channel owner to specify every visual choice.
