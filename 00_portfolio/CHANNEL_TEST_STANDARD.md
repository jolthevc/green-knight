# Channel Test and Capital Allocation Standard

**Status:** Canonical portfolio-wide operating standard  
**Purpose:** Make Green Knight's test, scale, reposition, and kill philosophy concrete without creating a complicated scoring system.

---

## 1. Principle

A new channel is an investment thesis.

It should receive enough capital and enough videos to test the thesis, but it should not continue indefinitely because time and money have already been spent.

Green Knight does not use a universal numerical score to decide channel quality.

The decision should combine:

- audience response
- repeatability of strong ideas
- production economics
- evidence about packaging and retention
- quality of the audience / monetization
- operational burden
- confidence in what can still improve

---

## 2. Initial Test

Before launch, every channel should have an explicit initial test plan.

At minimum define:

- expected first-test slate
- maximum initial production budget
- expected publishing cadence
- what the test is trying to learn
- when the first formal reassessment occurs

### Default starting point

Unless the channel has a reason to differ, treat the first **5 published videos** as the initial calibration set.

This is a checkpoint, not an automatic kill line.

A channel may be reconsidered earlier if research or early production disproves the thesis.

A channel may reasonably need more than five videos if the evidence remains genuinely inconclusive.

---

## 3. Reassessment Questions

At the checkpoint, ask:

### Audience
- Is there evidence that the intended audience exists?
- Are impressions being earned?
- Do people click when packaging is strong?
- Do they continue watching?

### Content Engine
- Can we still identify many strong future videos?
- Are ideas becoming repetitive?
- Does the channel have more than one viable pillar?

### Product Quality
- Are the videos reaching the intended editorial and visual bar?
- Are weaknesses in results actually channel-thesis problems, or execution problems we know how to improve?

### Economics
- Is production cost reasonable relative to observed or plausible monetization?
- Does the audience appear economically attractive?
- Is there a credible path to positive contribution?

### Operations
- Is the channel unusually difficult to research, script, source, or edit?
- Does it consume disproportionate human attention?

---

## 4. Channel Decisions

### TESTING
We are still gathering meaningful evidence.

### SCALE
Increase output or investment because both the content thesis and economics show enough evidence to justify more capital.

### MAINTAIN
The channel is useful and healthy at roughly its current level, but does not currently justify aggressive expansion.

### HARVEST
Reduce active investment while continuing to benefit from the existing evergreen library or low-maintenance output.

### REPOSITION
The underlying audience or content opportunity remains attractive, but the current positioning, content engine, packaging, or format needs a deliberate change.

### KILLED
Stop active investment because the expected value of further testing is no longer attractive.

---

## 5. Decision Rights

AI may recommend:

- SCALE
- MAINTAIN
- HARVEST
- REPOSITION
- KILLED

A human makes the channel-level capital allocation decision during the early portfolio.

Do not automatically kill a channel based solely on one model judgment.

---

## 6. Avoid False Precision

Do not invent fixed rules such as:

- "CTR below X means kill"
- "RPM above Y means scale"
- "five videos without a breakout means failure"

without enough Green Knight portfolio data to justify them.

Different channels may have different:

- audience sizes
- RPMs
- video lifecycles
- production costs
- evergreen curves
- packaging profiles

As the portfolio matures, evidence may justify more specific channel-level benchmarks.

Until then, use disciplined judgment rather than fake precision.
