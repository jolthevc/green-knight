# Research Packet Standard

**Status:** Canonical portfolio-wide standard  
**Purpose:** Gather and organize the factual intelligence required to build a strong, defensible video.

---

## 1. Role of the Research Packet

The research packet is not a draft script.

It is the evidence base from which a story can be built.

Its job is to give the outline and script stages enough reliable material that they do not need to invent facts, rely on memory, or conduct hidden ad hoc research.

The packet should preserve complexity even when the eventual script simplifies it.

---

## 2. Research Principles

Research should be:

- relevant to the stated brief
- source-aware
- skeptical of convenient claims
- willing to disprove the initial thesis
- explicit about uncertainty
- organized for editorial use
- rich enough to reveal better framings than the one originally proposed

The researcher should not force evidence to fit the brief.

---

## 3. Required Sections

### Executive Findings
Summarize the most important conclusions.

Include whether the initial thesis appears:

- supported
- partially supported
- weakened
- contradicted
- unresolved

### Thesis Evidence
Organize evidence for and against the proposed thesis.

### Core Facts
The foundational facts the story depends on.

### Mechanism
Explain how the relevant system, business, technology, event, or behavior actually works.

### Numbers and Scale
Include relevant quantitative evidence.

For each important number, capture:

- value
- unit
- date or period
- population or scope
- source
- caveats

### Timeline
Where chronology matters, build a clean sequence.

### Key People and Organizations
Identify important actors and their roles.

### Important Events
Capture moments that may become narrative beats.

### Counterpoints and Complications
Include evidence that makes the story less neat.

### Alternative Explanations
Document plausible explanations other than the preferred one.

### Interesting Details
Capture specific facts, anecdotes, scenes, contradictions, quotes, or mechanisms that may make the story vivid.

### Visual Opportunities
Note documents, maps, charts, archival footage, screenshots, objects, locations, or mechanisms that could be visually useful.

### Open Questions
State what remains unresolved.

### Research Gaps
State what could not be found or adequately verified.

### Editorial Opportunities
List possible angles or story structures revealed by the research.

Do not decide the final outline here.

### Suggested Reframe
If research materially changes the video, state the recommended new framing.

---

## 4. Source Quality

Prefer primary sources where practical.

Useful source classes may include:

- company filings
- government data
- court documents
- academic research
- official reports
- original interviews
- reputable industry publications
- high-quality journalism
- books
- credible databases

Secondary sources can be valuable for context, discovery, and interpretation.

Low-quality summaries should not become the sole support for consequential claims.

---

## 5. Source Traceability

Every important factual claim should be traceable to the `SOURCE_LOG`.

The packet may use source IDs such as:

`[S014]`

Do not allow unsupported claims to become more authoritative simply because they appear in polished prose.

---

## 6. Quotes

Quotes should be used selectively.

Capture only the relevant portion and enough surrounding context to understand its meaning.

Do not rely on a quote if paraphrasing the underlying fact is more accurate or useful.

---

## 7. Depth

There is no fixed word count.

The packet should be as deep as required by the story.

A straightforward consumer mechanism may require a few thousand words.

A complex business, historical, financial, legal, scientific, or investigative story may require substantially more.

Depth should be driven by decision usefulness, not page count.

---

## 8. Research Audit Questions

Before passing research, ask:

- Does the evidence actually support the thesis?
- What evidence weakens it?
- Are key numbers current and correctly scoped?
- Are causal claims supported or merely correlated?
- Are we relying on one weak source?
- Are important facts contested?
- Is there enough material for the intended runtime?
- Is there enough visual material?
- Did the research uncover a better story?
- What would a skeptical expert challenge?

---

## 9. Permitted Research Review Outcomes

### PROCEED
Evidence supports a viable story.

### RESEARCH_MORE
The story may work, but critical gaps remain.

### REFRAME
The underlying topic remains attractive, but the proposed thesis should change.

### KILL
The story is not sufficiently supported, compelling, feasible, or appropriate.

---

## 10. Failure Modes

Avoid:

- research written like a generic encyclopedia article
- collecting facts without relation to the thesis
- hiding conflicting evidence
- treating repeated secondary reporting as independent confirmation
- stale statistics presented as current
- false precision
- unsupported causal conclusions
- excessive background with little story value
- source URLs without explanation
- polished prose that obscures weak evidence
- research that never asks whether the video should still exist

---

## 11. Completion Gate

Research passes when the outline stage can:

1. understand the subject
2. distinguish fact from interpretation
3. support the core claims
4. identify narrative opportunities
5. understand important uncertainty
6. trace consequential facts to sources
7. build a story without performing substantial hidden research
