# Video Postmortem Standard

**Status:** Canonical portfolio-wide standard  
**Purpose:** Convert published performance into durable learning for the channel and portfolio.

---

## 1. Role

Green Knight should not merely collect metrics.

It should learn from them.

The postmortem connects:

- the original hypothesis
- the creative decisions
- the observed audience behavior
- the economics
- the interpretation
- the next decision

A postmortem should resist hindsight bias.

The original brief matters because it preserves what Green Knight believed before seeing the result.

---

## 2. Timing

Postmortems may be updated over time.

Useful analysis windows may include:

- 24 hours
- 7 days
- 30 days
- 90 days
- lifetime

The definitive postmortem can mature as the video's evergreen behavior becomes clearer.

Do not treat early performance as final when the channel or topic has a long-tail distribution pattern.

---

## 3. Required Sections

### Video Identity
- video ID
- channel
- final title
- publish date
- runtime
- content pillar

### Original Hypothesis
Summarize:

- why we made it
- expected viewer appeal
- expected packaging mechanism
- expected content payoff

Do not rewrite this using hindsight.

### Key Creative Decisions
Record material decisions such as:

- framing
- title
- thumbnail
- opening
- runtime
- structure
- visual style
- experimental variable

### Production Economics
Record:

- production cost
- notable revision cost
- asset cost
- other variable cost
- total direct cost

### Observed Performance
Use standardized metrics from Sheets.

Separate raw observations from interpretation.

### Against Baseline
Compare with the right reference:

- channel median
- recent-video median
- pillar median
- comparable runtime
- relevant cohort

Avoid comparisons that exaggerate the result.

### Packaging Performance
Analyze:

- impressions
- CTR
- title/thumbnail changes
- testing if any
- early distribution behavior

### Content Performance
Analyze:

- average view duration
- average percentage viewed
- early retention
- notable retention drops or peaks
- watch time
- comments or qualitative reaction

### Economic Performance
Analyze:

- revenue
- RPM
- contribution
- payback where meaningful
- ongoing evergreen value

### Observations
Facts only.

Examples:

- CTR was 6.2% versus a 4.7% channel median.
- Retention fell sharply during the 2:10 to 3:00 historical background section.

### Interpretations
Hypotheses about why.

Examples:

- recognizable consumer subject may have improved click behavior.
- historical context may have arrived before the viewer understood why it mattered.

### Confidence
For each important interpretation, state confidence:

- HIGH
- MEDIUM
- LOW

Explain major confounders.

### What Worked
Identify repeatable elements where evidence supports the conclusion.

### What Did Not Work
Identify weak elements.

### What Not to Infer
State tempting but unsupported conclusions.

This is important for avoiding overfitting.

### Next Actions
Examples:

- repeat framing
- retest packaging pattern
- reduce background section
- create sequel
- avoid topic class
- update channel standard
- run controlled experiment

### Learning Tags
Apply structured tags for later cross-video analysis.

### Portfolio Relevance
Could this learning transfer to other channels?

If so, explain how cautiously.

---

## 4. Result Classification

A simple result classification may be useful.

Possible labels:

- BREAKOUT
- WINNER
- ABOVE_BASELINE
- BASELINE
- BELOW_BASELINE
- MISS
- TOO_EARLY

The classification should be relative to context, not based on one universal view threshold.

---

## 5. Observation vs Interpretation

Never merge these.

Bad:

> The technical title caused poor performance.

Better:

**Observation:** CTR was materially below the channel median.

**Interpretation:** The technical wording may have reduced accessibility.

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Confounder:** Topic also had lower subject familiarity.

This discipline makes the dataset more useful.

---

## 6. Cross-Channel Learning

When a pattern appears across several videos or channels, it may deserve elevation into:

- channel `EXAMPLES.md`
- channel standards
- portfolio learnings
- ideation logic
- packaging logic
- workflow prompts

Do not promote one-video anecdotes into universal rules.

---

## 7. Failure Modes

Avoid:

- hindsight certainty
- cherry-picking one metric
- ignoring channel baseline
- assuming correlation equals causation
- attributing success entirely to content when packaging drove the result
- attributing failure entirely to packaging when retention was poor
- learning from tiny sample sizes as if they are definitive
- treating views as the only outcome
- failing to record what was believed before publication

---

## 8. Completion Gate

A postmortem is complete when Green Knight can state:

1. what happened
2. how it compares with a relevant baseline
3. what we think caused it
4. how confident we are
5. what we should do differently or repeat
6. what should be remembered by the channel or portfolio
