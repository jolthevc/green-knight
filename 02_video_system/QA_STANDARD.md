# Video QA Standard

**Status:** Canonical portfolio-wide standard  
**Purpose:** Prevent avoidable editorial, factual, production, rights, brand, and technical errors from reaching publication.

---

## 1. QA Philosophy

QA is not merely typo checking.

It is the final verification that the produced video is the video Green Knight intended to publish.

A polished edit can still fail QA.

A video should not publish until significant issues are resolved.

---

## 2. QA Inputs

Review:

- final candidate video
- approved script
- source log
- production packet
- packaging packet
- channel standards
- thumbnail
- publishing metadata
- captions if available

---

## 3. Editorial QA

Verify:

- video follows approved thesis
- no important section is missing
- edits did not change meaning
- package promise is fulfilled
- pacing does not break the intended story
- ending is intact
- no unresolved placeholders remain

---

## 4. Factual QA

Verify:

- names
- dates
- numbers
- labels
- company names
- map locations
- charts
- captions
- quoted language
- screenshots
- factual on-screen text

Check that visual simplification has not changed the underlying claim.

---

## 5. Visual QA

Verify:

- visuals correspond to narration
- no misleading footage
- no accidental repetition
- no placeholder media
- no broken graphics
- on-screen text is readable
- visual density fits the channel
- charts communicate the intended insight
- maps are correctly labeled
- AI-generated visuals are used consistently with policy and channel standard

---

## 6. Audio QA

Verify:

- narration is complete
- pronunciations are acceptable
- levels are balanced
- music does not obscure narration
- no clipping
- no accidental silence
- no obvious synthetic-voice artifact that materially harms quality
- sound effects are appropriate

---

## 7. Brand QA

Verify alignment with:

- channel voice
- visual language
- pacing
- sophistication
- humor
- packaging philosophy
- audience assumptions

The video should feel like the same media property even if a different editor produced it.

---

## 8. Rights QA

Review potential risk around:

- stock licenses
- music
- archival clips
- screenshots
- social content
- logos
- news footage
- copyrighted video
- AI-generated assets
- attribution requirements

Evidence sourcing does not equal reuse permission.

Escalate uncertain high-risk usage rather than assuming.

---

## 9. Packaging QA

Verify:

- title is final
- thumbnail is final
- title-thumbnail pair is coherent
- package remains truthful after final edit
- opening fulfills the click promise
- spelling and visual details are correct

---

## 10. Technical QA

Verify as applicable:

- resolution
- aspect ratio
- frame rate
- audio channels
- export integrity
- captions
- chapter timestamps
- description
- links
- thumbnail dimensions
- filename
- upload visibility
- scheduling

---

## 11. Severity Levels

### BLOCKER
Must be fixed before publication.

Examples:

- unsupported central claim
- wrong number that changes meaning
- broken export
- obvious rights issue
- package materially misrepresents video
- missing narration section

### MAJOR
Strongly expected to be fixed.

Examples:

- repeated visual mismatch
- important pronunciation issue
- weak graphic
- pacing problem that harms retention
- brand inconsistency

### MINOR
Fix when efficient; does not necessarily block publication.

Examples:

- tiny alignment issue
- nonmaterial stylistic preference
- micro-timing polish

Do not classify factual inaccuracies as minor merely because the correction is inconvenient.

---

## 12. QA Outcome

Allowed outcomes:

- PASS
- PASS_WITH_MINOR_NOTES
- FAIL_REVISION_REQUIRED
- ESCALATE

Record material QA failures so recurring production problems can be identified.

---

## 13. Completion Gate

A video is ready to publish only when there are no unresolved blockers and any accepted remaining issues are deliberate rather than overlooked.
