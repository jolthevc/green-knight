# Packaging Packet Standard

**Status:** Canonical portfolio-wide standard  
**Purpose:** Develop and select the title-thumbnail promise that earns the right audience's click and aligns with the actual video.

---

## 1. Role

Packaging is not a decorative final step.

It is the interface between the video and the viewer.

The packet should explore genuinely different ways to express the video's value, not generate dozens of trivial title variations.

---

## 2. Required Inputs

Use:

- approved thesis
- approved outline or script
- channel packaging standard
- audience definition
- relevant historical packaging performance
- thumbnail visual constraints
- current video framing

---

## 3. Required Sections

### Core Viewer Promise
State what the package needs to communicate.

### Core Curiosity
What should the viewer want to know?

### Packaging Constraints
What must not be implied?

### Title Territories
Create several distinct strategic directions.

Examples of territories:

- mechanism
- contradiction
- consequence
- mystery
- recognizable subject
- scale
- transformation
- comparison

Do not force these categories when they do not fit.

### Title Candidates
Generate candidates within each territory.

### Thumbnail Territories
Generate genuinely distinct visual concepts.

### Title-Thumbnail Pairings
Pair titles and thumbnails intentionally.

### Opening Alignment
Explain how the opening confirms each leading package.

### Risks
Identify:

- misleading implication
- excessive sensationalism
- ambiguity
- weak subject recognition
- visual complexity
- duplication between title and thumbnail

### Finalists
Select a small set.

### Recommended Package
State the recommended pair and rationale.

The final human decision may differ.

---

## 4. Diversity Requirement

The packet should contain conceptual diversity.

Ten titles that differ by one adjective are not ten ideas.

A strong packet explores different viewer questions.

---

## 5. Truthfulness

The package can create curiosity.

It cannot invent a stronger story than the video contains.

Do not:

- imply a number the evidence does not support
- suggest scandal where none exists
- imply causality when the script only shows correlation
- use a thumbnail that falsely depicts an event as real
- hide a qualification that fundamentally changes the claim

---

## 6. Thumbnail Evaluation

Assess:

- one-second comprehension
- focal point
- subject recognition
- emotional signal
- clutter
- text dependence
- mobile readability
- novelty
- relationship to title

---

## 7. Title Evaluation

Assess:

- clarity
- curiosity
- specificity
- familiarity
- novelty
- channel fit
- audience fit
- truthfulness
- redundancy with thumbnail
- jargon

---

## 8. Package-Script Alignment

The package should influence the opening.

If the title promises a mechanism, the opening should establish that mechanism as the central question.

If the thumbnail implies a comparison, the video should address it.

The viewer should feel rapidly reassured that the click was justified.

---

## 9. Testing

Where platform capabilities and traffic justify it, title or thumbnail testing may occur.

Preserve:

- tested variants
- test window
- impressions
- CTR
- other relevant metrics
- interpretation

Do not over-read noisy tests.

---

## 10. Failure Modes

Avoid:

- title generation after the story is already fixed beyond repair
- generic "The Truth About..." formulations without a real truth
- misleading scale
- unreadable thumbnails
- excessive words in thumbnail
- title and thumbnail saying the same thing
- clever titles the viewer cannot parse
- technical accuracy that destroys accessibility
- copying competitor packages mechanically

---

## 11. Completion Gate

Packaging passes when the chosen package:

1. creates a clear viewer question
2. feels native to the channel
3. can be understood quickly
4. truthfully represents the video
5. is materially different from weaker alternatives
6. has an opening that fulfills its promise
